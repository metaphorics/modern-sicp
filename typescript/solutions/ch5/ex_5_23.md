# Exercise 5.23: derived expressions via transformer operations

The exercise's cheat is taken literally: `cond->if` and `let->combination` are machine operations, and the dispatch grows two tests, one per derived form, ahead of the application test. The splice point matters: `application?` is `pair?` here, and a `cond` is a pair, so the derived tests come first. Each entry transforms `exp` and re-enters `eval-dispatch`, so the rest of the controller never knows the forms existed.

`cond->if` builds the book's chain of `if`s from 4.1.2: a clause's predicate is its first element, a bodyless clause's consequent is its own test, and a missing `else` ends the chain in the `false` constant, which is why `(cond ((= 1 2)))` answers `#f`. `let->combination` builds a lambda over the binding names and applies it to the binding initializers in one expression.

The session runs `(classify 0)`, `(classify 1)`, and `(classify 7)` through the transformer and answers `zero`, `one`, and `many`; the bodyless `(cond ((= 1 1)))` answers `#t` and `(cond ((= 1 2)))` answers `#f`; the `let` that multiplies `a` by `b` answers `6`. The base evaluator throws on the same `cond`, so the test pins that the transformers, not an existing path, carry the forms.

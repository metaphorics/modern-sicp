## Exercise 3.14: reverse a pair chain in place

Approach: `mystery` keeps a reversed prefix and a cursor in the unvisited
suffix. On each step it saves the current `second` field, points that field
at the prefix, and advances. The call creates no new `PairCell`.

Why the original reference changes: both `v` and `w` reach the same four
cells. After the traversal, `v` names the singleton prefix and `w` names the
reversed chain. Equal contents alone would not prove that the cells were
reused.

What the tests say: structural comparisons check the resulting data. Identity
checks prove that `w` is the old last cell, followed by the old third and
second cells and then the original head. A separately allocated reversal
would match the data but fail those identity checks.

Class: T (the operation uses typed pair data).

Deviation: textual list output is replaced by structural and identity
assertions.

# Exercise 2.24: the tree reading of 1, (2, (3, 4)) (replace)

The map class is `R` (map row: "draw nested structure in edition
syntax"). Scheme prints `(1 (2 (3 4)))` and the exercise is about
reading that printed form three ways. The OCaml replacement asks the
same three readings of the edition's tree value.

`ex_2_24 ()` is the value
`Node [Leaf 1; Node [Leaf 2; Node [Leaf 3; Leaf 4]]]`. Printed by
Printed by the toplevel it reads exactly as that: one `Node` holding a leaf
a subtree. Its box-and-pointer structure: the outer list cell holds a
pointer to the `Leaf 1` box in its first field and to the next cell
in its second; that cell points at `Leaf 2` and at the innermost
cell, which points at `Leaf 3` and `Leaf 4` and ends in `[]`. As a
tree (in the style of Figure 2.6), the root has three branches (the
leaf 1, the leaf 2, and the subtree whose two leaves are 3 and 4), so
so `count_leaves` of it is 4, which the test also checks.

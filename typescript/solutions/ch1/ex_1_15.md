# Exercise 1.15: how many times p runs, and the growth of sine

## Approach

Each recursive step of `sine` divides the angle by 3 and defers one `p`.
The recursion bottoms out when `|angle| <= 0.1`, so the number of `p`
applications is the number of divisions by 3 needed to bring `a` under
`0.1`: the smallest `k` with `|a| / 3^k <= 0.1`, i.e. about
`log_3(a / 0.1)`. One deferred `p` per frame, `k` frames deep: space
`Theta(log a)`, steps `Theta(log a)`.

## Numbers

`sine(12.15)` applies `p` five times (`12.15 -> 4.05 -> 1.35 -> 0.45 ->
0.15 -> 0.05`) and lands on `-0.39980345741334`, matching
`Math.sin(12.15)` to two decimals - the approximation is the book's, the
identity does the rest. A tenth of the angle: `1.215` needs four. An
order of magnitude up: `121.5` needs seven, `1215` needs nine. An angle
already at `0.05` needs zero.

## Class and deviations

Translated row. `!(abs(angle) > 0.1)` keeps the book's test shape (the
negated `>`), `Math.abs` stands in for Scheme's `abs`, and the counting
variant wraps the same recursion so the answer is measured, not quoted.
No deviation from the statement.

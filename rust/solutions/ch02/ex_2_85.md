# Exercise 2.85: drop projection

## Approach

`install_raise_and_project` keeps the raise entries of 2.83 and 2.84
and adds three projections. Complex projects to real by dropping the
imaginary part through `real_part_dispatch`; real projects to integer
by `round`, the footnote's projection; rational projects to integer
only when the denominator is `1`, and a fraction otherwise rejects
with "a fraction does not project to an integer".

`drop_value` implements the statement's test: project, raise the
projection back up to the starting tag, and keep the lower form only
when the library's `is_equ` answers equal over the round trip. The
descent repeats while a projection still applies, bounded at 8 rounds,
and `apply_generic_drop` rewrites dispatch so that the arithmetic
answers (`add`, `sub`, `mul`, `div`) are dropped after the handler
returns.

The book's examples come out as stated and asserted: adding `(2 + 3i)`
to `(4 - 3i)` lands as the integer `6`; `1.5 + 0i` lowers to
`(real 1.5)` and stops there, because rounding makes the next round
trip unequal (`2` against `1.5`); `1 + 0i` reaches the integer `1`;
and `2 + 3i` cannot be lowered at all, its projection failing the
equality test, so the answer stays `(complex (rectangular (2 . 3)))`.

## Map class

`A`: drop needs project plus equ? dispatch. The `project` entries
supply the downward step, the library's `is_equ` judges the round
trip, and the two together decide each descent of `drop_value`.

## Deviation

The rewritten dispatcher drops only the four arithmetic results;
other operations return undropped. The 2.84 raising layer is also not
composed into it, since the probes' arithmetic entries are all
same-type and dropping rides on plain dispatch.

# Exercise 4.39: the order of the restrictions (translate)

The order of restrictions that follow all five choices cannot change
the answer. It cannot change the shape of the search either: every
complete assignment is still generated. The book's order and the order
with `distinct` last give identical transcripts: one answer, 3124
choice points, and 3124 failures. Only the constant cost of rejecting
one assignment moves, and that cost favors placing the cheap arithmetic
tests before `distinct`.

Order starts to matter when a restriction moves in front of the choices
it does not mention. The reordered procedure chooses Fletcher first
under his own clauses, then Cooper under the adjacency clause that
mentions them both, and puts `distinct` last. It needs 308 choice
points and 308 failures to reach the same answer.

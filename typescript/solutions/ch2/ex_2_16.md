# Exercise 2.16: equivalent algebraic expressions, different answers

## Approach

The demonstration is the smallest instance of the phenomenon:
`xMinusX(r)` computes `subInterval(r, r)` on the 10-ohm 5-percent resistor
of exercises 2.14 and 2.15 and gets `[-1, 1]`, where algebra cancels the
expression to the exact `[0, 0]`. The same subtraction on genuinely
independent intervals (`[10, 14] - [2, 6] = [4, 12]`) is exactly right,
which isolates the cause: not the arithmetic, but the loss of the
information that the two arguments are the same quantity.

The impossibility half of the question is answered in the module doc
comment and here: endpoint bookkeeping alone cannot fix it, because a
binary operation never sees the expression its arguments came from. A
package without the shortcoming would have to propagate each result's
dependence on the underlying quantities - in the limit, representing the
computed expression's exact function form and evaluating its extrema, an
exactness the book itself warns is "very difficult". The exercises 2.14
and 2.15 disagreement is this same phenomenon on a larger expression.

## Numbers

`xMinusX([9.5, 10.5])` asserts `[-1, 1]`; the independent case asserts
`[4, 12]`.

## Map class

`T`: the demonstration and its explanation translate directly; no general
fix is claimed, matching the book's framing.

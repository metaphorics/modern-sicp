## Exercise 1.11: a recursive and an iterative process for the same function

Approach: `fRecursive` is the direct translation of the three-term
recurrence, tree-recursive once `n >= 3` because it calls itself three
times per level. `fIterative` slides a three-value window `(a, b, c)`
holding `f(k), f(k - 1), f(k - 2)` forward one step at a time, seeded at
`f(2) = 2, f(1) = 1, f(0) = 0` and counting down from `n - 2`; `a` at the
end is `f(n)`. Both give `f(10) = 1892`.

Class: A (adapt). SICP's official answer accepts either a recursive
procedure that a tail-recursive Scheme implementation still runs in
constant space, or a genuinely iterative one; this edition writes the
iterative side as `fIter`, marked `tailrec`, since only a
self-call already in tail position compiles that way here, and leaves
`fRecursive` as ordinary recursion, since three branching calls per level
never reduce to one tail call.

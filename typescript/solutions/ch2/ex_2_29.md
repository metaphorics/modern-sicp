# Exercise 2.29: mobiles

## Approach

A mobile is a tagged union: a `Mobile` holds two branches, a `Weight`
holds its load, and a branch holds its length plus the structure it
carries. `totalWeight` adds the weights found on the two sides through
the selectors; `isBalanced` requires the torques `length × weight` on
the two sides to agree *and* every submobile inside to balance, so the
nested mobile in the book's example can be balanced on its own while
the whole is not. Part d repeats the representation as pairs — the
mobile a `readonly [Branch, Branch]`, the branch a
`readonly [number, Structure]` — with new make/selectors; the weight
and balance logic runs unchanged over the re-tagged view of the pair,
which is the point the book's question makes: only the constructors and
selectors change.

## Numbers

The first mobile (2 × 5 on the left, 5 × 2 on the right) weighs 7 and
balances (10 = 10). The nested mobile weighs 10 and does not balance:
left torque 2 × 3 = 6 against right torque 2 × 7 = 14, while its right
submobile alone balances (3 × 4 = 4 × 3). The pair selectors hand back
the very branch objects the mobile was built from.

## Map class

`A`: the book's untyped pairs become tagged unions and readonly tuples,
and the selectors return real types instead of erroring on the wrong
kind of structure.

## Deviation

None.

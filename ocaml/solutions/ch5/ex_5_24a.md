# Exercise 5.24a: and and or as basic controller forms

The addition extends 5.24's treatment: `and` and `or` join the dispatch
as basic forms, each with its own loop over its operand sequence. `and`
evaluates operands until one is `false` (the answer) or the last one,
whose value is the answer in tail position; `or` evaluates until one is
true (its value is the answer) or the last one. `(and)` is `#t` and
`(or)` is `#f`, the identities the chains reduce to.

`ex_5_24a ()` pins the demonstrations: last-value and first-true-value
selection (`3`, `2`), the empty forms, short-circuit in both
directions (`(and #f no-such-variable)` answers `#f` and
`(or 1 no-such-variable)` answers `1`, where an eager evaluator would
die on the unbound variable), nesting, and the `within` predicate that
reads as its definition.

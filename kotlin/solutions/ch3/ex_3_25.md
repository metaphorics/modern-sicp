## Exercise 3.25: a table addressed by a list of keys

Approach: `KeyListTable` is the section's headed list applied
recursively. The header pair's car names the level -- `*table*` at the
top, the subtable's own key one level down -- and its cdr is the chain
of records, exactly the book's remark that a subtable needs no special
header symbol because the key identifying it serves that purpose.
`lookup(keys)` scans the top backbone for `keys[0]`; a hit at the last
key is the record's value, a hit earlier is a subtable header and the
scan recurses into its records. `insert` follows the same descent and,
on a miss at any level, splices a fresh headed subtable -- key in the
header slot, one record inside -- onto the current backbone before
continuing.

Why one recursive pair of operations instead of one- and two-key
variants: the book's 3.3.3 wrote `lookup` and `lookup2d` as separate
procedures that differ only in how many assoc scans they chain; the
general form is those scans in a loop over the key list. With the
recursion the section's two-dimensional table is just the two-key
case, and a three-key entry coexists with a one-key entry in a
different branch -- the test stores `a`, `letters b`, and
`math + int` side by side in one table, values under three different
depths.

The one convention the book leaves open: a key names either a value
or a subtable, not both. Storing under `a` and then under `a b` would
ask one record to be two things; the implementation follows the book
in not addressing that case, and the KDoc says so. Missing keys at any
depth answer null, the book's false, because `assoc` returns null
wherever the scan falls off a chain.

What the tests say: `E3_25Test.kt` covers the three-depth table, the
null at each kind of miss (absent subtable, absent record, absent top
key), overwrite of a record named by a full key list, the splice of a
fresh first key onto a shared backbone, and the two-key case matching
the section's behavior key for key.

Class: `T` (mechanical translation).

Deviation: none.

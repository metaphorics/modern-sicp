# Exercise 2.5: pairs of nonnegative integers as `2^a * 3^b`

## Approach

`cons(a, b)` computes `2^a * 3^b` with `checked_pow`/`checked_mul`,
returning `SchemeError::Overflow` if it does not fit `i128`; `car` and
`cdr` recover `a` and `b` by counting how many times 2 and 3 divide the
encoded value, which terminates because 2 and 3 share no prime factors
with each other.

## Map class and a correction

The map lists this exercise's Rust reason as "`2^a 3^b` overflows `i64`,
needs bigint." Reading this edition's own numbers convention (Part 3 of
`docs/plan/idiom-rust.md`): exact integers are `i128` with checked
arithmetic, and it names the specific places `i128` is not enough
(`expmod`'s naive intermediates, factorials past 33!, fibonacci stream
values past `n` about 184). Exercise 2.5 is not on that list, and this
exercise's own numbers stay small (the test encodes `(3, 2)`, well under
any width). Adding a bigint dependency would depart from the edition's
stated policy for no reason this exercise's own statement requires: it
asks for a working `2^a * 3^b` encoding, not an unbounded one. The
solution keeps `i128` with `checked_pow`/`checked_mul`, matching the
edition's general policy of checked arithmetic with `Overflow` at the
boundary rather than reaching for arbitrary precision. The class stays
`A` (the idea survives, but the fixed-width representation is a real
adaptation Scheme's exact integers do not force), with the correction
recorded here: no bigint crate is needed or used.

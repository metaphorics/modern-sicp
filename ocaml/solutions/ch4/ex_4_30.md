# Exercise 4.30 — forcing in eval-sequence (translate)

The map class is `T`. The edition's rule, stated: sequences evaluate
every expression and force none of them — forcing happens at the point
of use, which is a primitive's operands, an `if` predicate, an operator
position, or the driver. The solution runs the statement's programs
under that original sequence and under Cy D. Fect's proposal, which
forces all but the last expression of a sequence.

Part a, Ben right about `for-each`: the effects run because the
procedure call `(proc (car items))` applies a compound procedure whose
body is itself evaluated, and the effect statement `set!` needs no
forcing; a `display` in the mapped procedure reaches its primitive
application, and primitive operands are forced. Under Cy's sequence the
same program behaves identically, which is part c: forcing the sequence
positions changes nothing where the effect flows through an application,
because his proposal only forces expressions the evaluator already
evaluated.

Part b: `(p1 1)` is `(1 2)` under both — the `set!` is the body's first
expression, evaluated as the sequence goes. `(p2 1)` is 1 under the
original: the `set!` hides inside the delayed operand of `p`, the first
expression `e` is evaluated to that thunk and discarded, and the last
expression `x` still reads 1; the mutation never runs. Under Cy's
sequence the first expression is forced, the `set!` fires, and `x`
answers `(1 2)`.

Part d, the edition's preference is the original: sequence positions
stay lazy and effects happen where they are computed, so a thunk in a
discarded position is genuinely unused; Cy's variant makes the sequence
position strict and silently runs computations the text's rule would
have dropped.

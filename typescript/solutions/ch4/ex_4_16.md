# Exercise 4.16: scan out internal defines

## Approach

Three parts. (a) Lookup is changed to fail when it finds the `*unassigned*`
marker, so reading a name before its set! is an error rather than a silent
wrong value. (b) `scanOutDefines` rewrites a body with no defines remaining:
each internal define contributes a let binding initialized to
`'*unassigned*` and a set! at the front, and the remaining expressions
follow; a body without defines is returned unchanged. (c) The scan is
installed in make-procedure, not procedure-body: the rewrite then runs once
when a closure is created instead of on every application that reads the
body. Because the scanned bodies contain let, the dispatch here is complete
and adds let as a derived expression in the lambda form.

## Numbers

`scanOutDefines` over `(define a 1) (define b 2) (+ a b)` prints as
`((let ((a (quote *unassigned*)) (b (quote *unassigned*))) (set! a 1) (set!
b 2) (+ a b)))`: one let of marker bindings, then the set!s, then the body.
A define-free body compares equal to itself after scanning. The book's f,
with its mutually recursive internal even? and odd?, answers `#f` for
`(f 7)` and `#t` for `(f 8)` under the scanned evaluator. The program
`(define (g) (define a b) (define b 1) a)` fails on `(g)` with RuntimeError
whose detail is `b`: the set! for a reads b while b is still the marker.

## Class and deviations

Translated row. Deviations: this edition's format prints quote explicitly,
so the marker binding reads `(quote *unassigned*)` where the book writes
`'*unassigned*`; and since the base evaluator has no let, the scanned
dispatch carries its own let clause, per exercise 4.6's arrangement.

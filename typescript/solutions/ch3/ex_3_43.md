# Exercise 3.43: exchange preserves the multiset of balances

## Approach

Peter exchanges a1 with a2 while Paul exchanges a1 with a3, on accounts
that start at 10, 20, and 30. Three versions are enumerated on the step
scheduler, each schedule really executed: the text's first exchange with
individually serialized accounts (four steps per process: two balance
reads, then atomic withdraw and deposit chunks), the serialized exchange
(one chunk per process), and the first exchange over raw unserialized
withdraws and deposits (seven steps per process: two exchange reads, the
difference, then access/set pairs). Mechanism: explicit step-scheduler
enumeration.

## Pins

First version, computed: the first violating final is [40, 10, 10] --
the multiset {10, 20, 30} became {10, 10, 40}, exactly the book's
warning that serializing individual accounts is not enough for correct
exchange (Paul read a1 before Peter's update landed and both exchanges
computed their differences from stale balances). Yet every first-version
schedule sums to 60: the withdraw and deposit of one exchange move the
same amount, so the sum is safe even when the multiset is not.
Serialized exchange, computed: the two orders give [30, 10, 20] and
[20, 30, 10], both permutations of the start, so the multiset is always
preserved. Without per-account serialization, computed sums are {40, 50,
60}: the interleaved access/set pairs destroy money outright, the
timing-diagram condition the second half of the exercise asks to draw.

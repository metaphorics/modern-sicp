# Exercise 1.28: the Miller-Rabin test

## Approach

Modify the squaring step of `expmod` to watch its input: a value `half`
that is neither `1` nor `m - 1` yet squares to `1` modulo `m` is a
nontrivial square root of 1, and the exercise's hint says to signal it
by returning `0`. The signal propagates out of the recursion, so any
prime test that sees a `0` answers composite. `millerRabinWitness(n, a)`
runs `mrExpmod(a, n - 1, n)` and demands `1`; `millerRabin` is the
analog of `fermatTest`, drawing witnesses with the section's
`rng.random` shape. The fixed-witness runs pin the transcript under the
deterministic-testing rule.

## Numbers

The known primes `2, 13, 563, 1009, 1013, 1000003` pass with witnesses
`2, 3, 5, 7`. All six Carmichael numbers of footnote 47 fail at every
one of those witnesses - `mrExpmod(2, 560, 561)` and
`mrExpmod(2, 6600, 6601)` return the signaling `0`, where the plain
Fermat test passed for every witness in exercise 1.27 - and `2047`
(`23 x 89`, a strong pseudoprime to base 2 under the plain Fermat
reading) is caught too. The prime `1013` shows the honest path:
`mrExpmod(2, 1012, 1013)` returns `1` with no signal.

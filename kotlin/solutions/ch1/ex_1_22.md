## Exercise 1.22: search-for-primes and timing

Approach: `searchForPrimes` walks consecutive odd integers from just past
`start`, applying `timedPrimeTest`, and collects the first `count` that
pass; `timedPrimeTest` returns `null` for a composite (nothing to time)
and a `TimedPrimeResult` carrying `System.nanoTime` elapsed time for a
prime, the direct restatement of the book's `runtime`-based procedures.
The three smallest primes above 1000, 10,000, 100,000, and 1,000,000 are
1009/1013/1019, 10007/10009/10037, 100003/100019/100043, and
1000003/1000033/1000037.

Timing discussion (not asserted by the test): the testing algorithm has
order of growth Theta(sqrt n), so timing near 10,000 is expected to take
roughly sqrt(10) times as long as timing near 1000, and similarly for
each further factor of 10; whether a given machine's wall-clock timings
actually bear this out depends on JIT warmup, scheduler noise, and clock
resolution, none of which a unit test can hold still, so this edition
records the prediction here rather than asserting a timing ratio.

Class: A (adapt). `runtime` becomes `System.nanoTime`; the procedure
shapes (`timedPrimeTest`, `searchForPrimes`) carry over directly.

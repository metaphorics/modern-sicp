# Exercise 2.3: two rectangle representations

## Approach

The `Rectangle` union has two tags: `Corners` stores two opposite corners,
`BaseHeight` stores one corner plus a width and a height. The abstraction
barrier sits at `widthOf`/`heightOf`: the shared `perimeter` and `area`
procedures consume only those two selectors and never see a tag, so "the
same perimeter and area procedures work using either representation" holds
by construction, and the exhaustiveness-checked switch is what keeps a third
representation honest when it arrives.

## Numbers

The same 4-by-3 rectangle built both ways gives perimeter 14 and area 12;
the corner form with the corners handed over in the opposite order gives the
same two numbers.

## Map class

`T`: two constructor/selector pairs behind one shared pair of procedures,
translated directly; the discriminated union is this edition's spelling of
"two representations, one barrier".

## Deviation

None.

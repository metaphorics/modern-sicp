# Exercise 3.10 — lifetime of the captured ref cell (replace)

The map class is `R` for this edition: the SICP exercise contrasts the
environment structures of two Scheme spellings of `make-withdraw`, one
whose parameter binding mutates through `set!` and one desugared into
an explicit `let`. OCaml has no `set!` on bindings at all, so the
edition replaces the contrast with the question that survives: where
the cell is born, who captures it, and how long it lives.

The traced factory `make_withdraw_traced` builds the section's
withdrawal processor and returns the cell it allocated as a
`balance_cell` witness alongside the closure. Physical equality on the
witness decides sharing: mutating `w1.balance_cell` directly and then
withdrawing shows the closure reads that very cell (it answers
`Balance 700` after a write of 1000 and a request of 300), while
`not (w1.balance_cell == w2.balance_cell)` shows two calls with the
same initial 100 still allocate two different cells.

Sample: `ex_3_10 ()` answers
`(50, true, (50, 70), Balance 700)` — the first call left 50 in `w1`'s
cell, the two cells are physically distinct, `w2`'s own withdrawal
changed only its cell (50 and 70), and the last withdrawal saw the
direct write, proving the cell outlives the call frame that
constructed it.

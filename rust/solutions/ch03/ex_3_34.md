# Exercise 3.34: Louis's broken squarer

## Approach

The book asks why Louis Reasoner's squarer fails. The flaw is
structural: `multiplier a a b` wires the same connector into both
factor terminals. `process_new_value` computes only when two of its
three connectors are known. When only the output is set, both factor
terminals still name the same unset connector, so the "one factor known"
branch never fires. The Rust solution wires the flawed squarer, sets
`b` alone to 25, and reads `a` back as nothing. A companion test
shows the failure is one-directional: setting `a` gives the multiplier
two known connectors at once, both terminals, and `b` comes out 25, so
the forward direction works while the reverse never can.

## Map class

`T`. The exercise's squarer, its flaw, and the explanation are the
statement's own, demonstrated over the translated `multiplier`.

## Deviation

None. The broken construction is deliberate: the exercise asks to
exhibit and explain the failure, and the tests hold both its
directions.

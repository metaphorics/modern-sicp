# Exercise 4.26a: `when` as a derived expression (edition addition)

## Approach

`when` is the mirror of 4.26's `unless`, and the derivation is
macro-style: one rewrite to forms the lazy evaluator already has. [WithWhen]
recognizes `(when condition body ...)` in `step` and lowers it to
`(if condition (begin body ...) false)`; the `false` variable standing
where the parser's missing alternative stands, and `begin` folding the
body exactly as `sequence->exp` does for the book's own forms.

The pin is a before/after trace, as asked: the same `(when (> 3 2) 'yes)`
is an application of an unbound name on the base evaluator and answers
`yes` after the derivation. Two shape pins follow: a false condition with
no else arm answers `#f` (the parser's missing alternative), and a
multi-expression body runs as one sequence answering its last expression.

One connection the derivation makes visible: under the text's
`eval-sequence` rule the `begin` prefix of a `when` body is evaluated but
not forced, so a side-effecting body prefix only runs if its own demand
sites force, the exact question exercise 4.30 debates, and the reason a
side-effect pin would belong to that debate rather than this one.

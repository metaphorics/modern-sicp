# Exercise 5.20: the two drawings

The exercise fixes the starting state: the free pointer at `p1`. The
three conses of `(define x (cons 1 2))` and `(define y (list x x))`
then allocate in a forced order, because a register machine cannot
fill the outer cons cell before it has computed the inner one:
`(cons x (cons x '()))` allocates innermost first. Cell `p1` gets
`(n1 n2)` and `x = p1`; cell `p2` gets `(p1 e0)`; cell `p3` gets
`(p1 p2)` and `y = p3`. The final value of `free` is `p4`.

`memoryVectorDrawing()` runs exactly those three allocations through
the allocation path (`free` started at `p1`) and answers with the
memory-vector drawing, the `the-cars`/`the-cdrs` table of Figure 5.14
with one column per cell, plus the two pointer answers:

    index    0   1   2   3   4   5   6   7
    the-cars e0  n1  p1  p1  e0  e0  e0  e0
    the-cdrs e0  n2  e0  p2  e0  e0  e0  e0

    x = p1
    y = p3
    free = p4

The box-and-pointer drawing of the same structure puts both of `y`'s
halves as arrows into the one `x` box:

    y -->+---+---+
         | * | * |
         +-|-+-|-+
           |   |
           |   |
           v   v
    x --->+---+---+
          | * | *-+--> 2
          +---|---+
              |
              v
             +---+
             | 1 |
             +---+

The drawing shows the exercise's point: the two arrows from `y` both
reach the same box, and the memory-vector drawing says the same thing
as one pointer value written twice, since columns 2 and 3 of
`the-cars` both hold `p1`. Cell `p0` is drawn as `e0`: nothing was
ever allocated there, because `free` started at `p1`.

The model behind the run is the section's `Memory` in the ch5 source
set beside the 5.2 simulator: two vectors and a free pointer, a pair
pointer being an index into the vectors, and typed pointers carried as
tagged words, so `p1` is `VTagged("p", VInt(1))` and the number 1 is
the exact word drawn `n1`.

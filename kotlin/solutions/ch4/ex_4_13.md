# Exercise 4.13: make-unbound!

## Approach

The book leaves the semantics of removing a binding open and asks the
reader to specify them. `WithUnbound` checks for the form in `step` --
it arrives as an application whose operator names `make-unbound!` --
takes the operand's name unevaluated (a variable about to stop existing
cannot be evaluated for its value), and drops the binding from the
FIRST frame only. First-frame-only is the deliberate choice, and the
justification is symmetry: `define` binds in exactly one frame, so
`make-unbound!` unbinds in exactly one frame -- the range where a
binding can be created is the range it can be removed. Unbinding then
never climbs past the caller's frame, and a shadow removed from its own
frame lets the outer binding show through again, which is the
second pin. A name absent from the first frame is left alone and the
form answers `ok`: unbind is idempotent and never a fault, the
frame-local mirror of `set!`-on-unbound being a fault for mutation that
cannot happen. Any other choice (walking outwards, or faulting on a
miss) would make the form's reach differ from `define`'s.

## Map class

`T`.

## Deviation

None. The book specifies no removal semantics; this host states its
choice here and pins it.

## Answer

- `(define x 3) x (make-unbound! x) x` reads
  `"3\nok\nError: unbound variable: x\n"` -- the form answers `ok`, the
  next lookup walks the chain, finds nothing, and faults `Unbound`.
- `(shadow!)`, whose body is `(define x 2) (make-unbound! x) x` over a
  global `x` of 1, prints `1` -- after the shadow is unbound from the
  call's frame the outer binding is visible again -- and the following
  top-level `x` still prints `1`, the global untouched:
  `"1\n1\n"`.
- `(make-unbound! nowhere)` answers `"ok\n"` -- an absent name is a
  no-op, not a fault.

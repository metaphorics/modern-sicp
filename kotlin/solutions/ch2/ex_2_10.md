## Exercise 2.10: dividing by a zero-spanning interval

Approach: an interval spans zero exactly when its lower bound is at most
zero and its upper bound is at least zero (a single endpoint of zero
counts, since `1 / 0` is undefined either way). `divIntervalChecked` is
`divInterval`'s own body, guarded by that check first, raising
`IntervalError.SpansZero(y)` through `Raise<IntervalError>` instead of
computing a reciprocal of an interval that has no reciprocal. Dividing
`[4, 8]` by `[1, 2]` succeeds and returns `[2, 8]`; dividing by `[-1, 1]`
or by `[0, 2]` both raise.

Class: T, a direct translation of "signal an error if it occurs";
Scheme's dynamically typed `error` becomes Arrow's `Raise<IntervalError>`,
this edition's one error convention (D14/D17's neighbor decision for
domain errors, kept separate from the interpreter's own `DataError`).
Deviation: named `divIntervalChecked` rather than reusing the name
`divInterval`, since Kotlin cannot redefine a top-level function under the
same name the way a second Scheme `define` would; every exercise from
here on that needs the checked division calls it by this name.

# Exercise 1.9 — substitution model for two additions (translate)

The map class is `T`: syntax changes only, since OCaml shares Scheme's
tail-call guarantee and the book's question about iterative versus
recursive processes transfers unchanged.

`plus_deferred a b` defers its increment until the recursive call returns:
evaluating `plus_deferred 4 5` expands to
`inc (inc (inc (inc (plus_deferred 0 5))))`, a chain of four deferred
`inc`s that only contracts once the base case is reached — a recursive
process, shaped exactly like `Factorial.recursive`'s chain of deferred
multiplications.

`plus_tail a b` carries the whole answer forward in its arguments; its
self-call is the entire result of the `else` branch, a tail position.
OCaml runs it in constant space: `plus_tail 4 5` steps directly through
`(3, 6)`, `(2, 7)`, `(1, 8)`, `(0, 9)` and returns 9 without ever
deferring anything — an iterative process.

The test pins both variants against `+` (`plus_deferred 4 5 = 9` and
`plus_tail 4 5 = 9`) and confirms `plus_tail`'s constant space directly by
running it to 1,000,000: a body that deferred work would overflow the
stack long before that argument, so the call succeeding is itself the
proof of tail position.

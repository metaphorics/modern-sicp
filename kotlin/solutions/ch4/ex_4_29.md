# Exercise 4.29: what memoization buys

## Approach

The counting session is the demonstration itself: `id` counts its
computations and the two probes differ only in how a demand reaches the
computation. Memoized, the parameter is one transparent thunk per
argument: `square` reads its parameter twice and the second read answers
from the memo, so the session counts 1 then 2. Every demand re-running
the computation counts 2 then 5 -- a program whose delayed arguments are
used more often than they are forced pays multiplicatively.

The unmemoized side of the old probe (an evaluator variant over
`VThunkNoMemo`) dies with the removed evaluator, and the module's thunks
compute at most once by contract. The cold probe therefore expresses
call-by-name honestly in guest code: the delayed computation is a
function value re-invoked at every demand, so no memo can hide the
recomputation. The forcing instrument corroborates the memoized side
(computations stay below force attempts).

## Map class

`T`; the objective (observe what memoization buys) is unchanged. The
comparison's second evaluator becomes a guest-level call-by-name probe,
since the experiment admits memoized thunks only.

## Deviation

The unmemoized evaluator subclass is replaced by a guest probe; the
counting boundaries (1, 2 and 2, 5) are preserved exactly.

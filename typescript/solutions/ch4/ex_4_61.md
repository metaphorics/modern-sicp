# Exercise 4.61: the next-to rules (translate)

## Approach

The two rules are the book's text verbatim: the base rule fires when
the list starts with the adjacent pair, and the recursive rule strips
the head and re-asks in the tail. The queries are the book's:
`(?x next-to ?y in (1 (2 3) 4))` and `(?x next-to 1 in (2 1 3 1))`.

## Numbers

The first query yields `(1 next-to (2 3) in (1 (2 3) 4))` from the
base rule and `((2 3) next-to 4 in (1 (2 3) 4))` from one recursion,
in that order. The second yields `(2 next-to 1 in (2 1 3 1))` and
`(3 next-to 1 in (2 1 3 1))`. The tests pin both pairs and both
result sequences, matching the sibling pins exactly.

## Class and deviations

Translated row. Deviation: dotted patterns travel through the
reader's dotted-tail marker, so the multi-element tail
`(?x ?y . ?u)` in rule conclusions needs the core's spine-walking
matcher, which splices a marker at any spine position on either side.

# Exercise 4.44a: backtrack counts per board size (this edition's addition)

The tailored addition turns the engine's counter onto queens. Counting
rule: a backtrack is one delivery of the `Fail` effect to a
choice-point handler: the search re-entering a made choice to try its
next alternative. Because the alternatives of `an-integer-between 1 n`
are themselves a nest of `n` choice handlers, each failed row attempt
is counted at its own handler, and a handler whose alternatives are
exhausted counts its final delivery before propagating `Fail` to the
previous column's handler. Failures absorbed by the driver (exhaustion)
are not counted. The counts for the first solution per board size,
cross-checked against an independent continuation-passing simulation:
4 → 38, 5 → 10, 6 → 315. Board size 5 is cheaper than 4 because its
first solution sits near the search's preferred corner. The engine
counts and the simulation agree exactly on all three sizes.

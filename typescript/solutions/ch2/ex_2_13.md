# Exercise 2.13: the percentage tolerance of a product

## Approach

The claim is checked numerically from both ends. `productPercent(p, q)`
computes the product interval's own percentage tolerance through the
four-corner multiplication, and the test compares it against the sum of the
factors' tolerances. Writing each factor as `c(1 +- t)`, the product is
`c1c2(1 +- t1 +- t2 +- t1t2)`: the first-order tolerance is `t1 + t2`, and
what the difference between the two numbers measures is exactly the dropped
`t1t2` term - which is why the assertion is "within 0.01", not equality,
and why the second test checks the gap shrinking as the tolerances shrink.

## Numbers

`10 +- 1%` times `20 +- 2%` gives the product `[194.04, 206.04]` with
percentage tolerance `2.9994001199759976`, within `0.0006` of the formula's
`3`. At tolerances `0.1% + 0.2%` the gap is far smaller than at
`5% + 10%`.

# Exercise 2.1: a sign-normalizing `makeRat`

## Approach

`makeRatNormalized` reduces its arguments with the section's `gcd` and then
reads the sign off the *reduced* denominator: if it is negative, both parts
are negated, so a positive rational has two positive parts and a negative
rational has a negative numerator only. Because `bigint` division of a
negative by a positive gcd keeps the sign on the numerator, the flip fires
exactly once. `Rat` stays this edition's `readonly [bigint, bigint]` pair,
the section's own representation.

## Numbers

All four sign combinations of 1/2 assert `1/2`, `-1/2`, `-1/2`, `1/2`, and
the unreduced inputs `2/-4`, `-2/-4`, `-6/4` assert `-1/2`, `1/2`, `-3/2`.

## Map class

`T`: the statement translates directly; the sign rule is the book's, and the
only choice is testing the reduced rather than the raw denominator, which is
what makes `2/-4` come out as `-1/2` rather than `2/-4`.

## Deviation

None.

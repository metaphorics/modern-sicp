# Exercise 4.25: unless under delayed arguments

## Approach

The lazy experiment of the checked engine gives `unless` exactly the
discipline the exercise needs: an unannotated compound-procedure parameter
is delayed in a memoized transparent thunk, so the three operands reach
`unless` unevaluated and the body's `if` forces only the arm it chooses.
The `unless`-based `factorial` therefore bottoms out at 1 and the product
climbs back up. Under the core evaluator every operand evaluates before
`unless` is entered: the armed call raises on `1 / 0` before `42` is
reached, and the recursion never reaches its guard.

The old step-budget probe dies with the removed evaluator, and the engine
contract has no step budget (recursion depth is a resource, not a contract
category). The probe now carries an honest entry budget of its own: under
strict evaluation the descent exhausts 200 entries and the run reports it
instead of hanging the suite.

## Map class

`T` in the map row; the statement's premise needs an `A` reword for two
mechanics: the fault rendering is the typed category (`DivisionByZero`)
rather than wording, and the step-budget device becomes the program's own
entry budget.

## Deviation

The budget device is program-level, not an evaluator fault; the transcript
line is the program's own report. Numeric boundary of the lazy run (120)
is unchanged.

## Answer

- `lazyFactorialTranscript() == "120\n"`: the recursion bottoms out at 1
  under delayed arguments.
- `strictArmedUnlessTranscript() == "Error: DivisionByZero\n"`: the armed
  operand raises before `42` is reached; the fault observable is the
  category, and the output stream stays empty (no effect before the fault).
- `strictFactorialTranscript() == "entry budget exhausted\n"`: the strict
  descent exhausts the entry budget without reaching the guard.

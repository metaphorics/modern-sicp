# Exercise 4.29: memoization

## Approach

The slow-without-memoization program is the counting session itself.
`(* x x)` and `(* x x x)` are strict primitive applications, so every use
of the delayed parameter is a demand: the memoized evaluator fills the
cell on the first demand and answers from it afterwards; the unmemoized
evaluator ([NoMemoLazy], which delays operands as `VThunkNoMemo`) re-runs
`(id 10)` -- counter, side effect and all -- at every demand.

The measured counts are the book's expected answers: `(square (id 10))`
counts 1 memoized and 2 unmemoized, and `(cube (id 10))` lifts the totals
to 2 and 5. Each additional use costs one forcing, plus one recomputation
without the memo, which is why any program whose delayed arguments are
used more often than they are forced -- a nested numerical fold is the
textbook shape -- pays multiplicatively without the memo.

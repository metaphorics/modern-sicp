# Exercise 2.60: duplicate-allowed sets

## Approach

The bag representation keeps every occurrence. Membership is the
unordered scan; adjoining is one cons with no membership test; union is
an append; intersection keeps the elements of `set1` that `set2` holds.

## Numbers

Operation counts against the no-duplicate representation:

- `elementOfBag`: Theta(n) worst case either way; duplicates only make
  an early hit likelier.
- `adjoinBag`: Theta(1) instead of Theta(n) — no duplicate check.
- `unionBag`: Theta(n1 + n2) instead of Theta(n1 * n2) — no filtering.
- `intersectionBag`: still Theta(n1 * n2); no saving.

Applications that prefer bags: natural-count collections (word or event
frequencies), work lists where duplicates are harmless, and any setting
where items are added far more often than tested — the book's own
hint that the representation trades membership cost for cheap
construction.

# Exercise 3.22: the queue as a procedure with local state

## Approach

The book rebuilds the queue as `make-queue`: the front and rear pointers
become locals of one call and the operations become internal procedures
reached through a dispatch. `makeQueueClosure` spells that shape: the two
pointers are locals, the five messages (`insert!`, `delete!`, `front`,
`empty?`, `print`) are arms of one dispatch, `insert!`/`delete!` return
the dispatch itself (the book's queue value), and the empty-queue arms
fail with the module's `EmptyQueueError`. The splice logic is the
pointer-pair algorithm recast over locals; no module queue is used, this
is the message-passing alternative the exercise asks for.

## Pins

Computed by running. The figure 3.18 session over the closure: `empty?`
true at first, insert a and b then `print` reads `(a b)` and `front` is
a; delete leaves `(b)`; insert c and d leaves `(c d)` with `empty?`
false. `insert!` answers the dispatch: the returned value answers `print`
with `(a b)` after the first two inserts. Two queues made from two calls
are independent: inserting into q1 leaves q2 `empty?` true and q1 false.
Delete or front on an empty closure queue throws the module's
`EmptyQueueError`.

## Deviation

The book's dispatch takes one message symbol and omits argument-type
checking; the edition's dispatch is declared as contextual overloads so
each message keeps its own argument and answer type, which is what lets
`q("insert!", item)` and `q("front")` be well typed while remaining a
single entry point.

# Exercise 2.84: successive raising

## Approach

To apply an operation to arguments of different types, raise the lower
argument repeatedly until its type matches the other's, then apply. The dispatch never looks up which of two types is
higher in a table: a type is above another
exactly when a chain of one-step raises reaches it, so the raise entries determine the ordering, and adding
a new level to the tower means installing its raise and nothing else. If the chain runs off the top, the
attempt reports the failure.

## Numbers

`add(7, 1.5)` answers the real 8.5: the ordinary number raises to a rational,
then to a real, and the real package adds; a comparison of a real with a
complex raises the real once; and an operation whose arguments cannot meet
reports the book's error.

## Map class

A (tower ordering via raise chain search): the search walks the raise entries
instead of a rank table.

## Deviation

None.

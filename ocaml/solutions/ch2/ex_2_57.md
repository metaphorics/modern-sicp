# Exercise 2.57: sums and products of any number of terms (adapt)

The map class is `A`: the idea (addend is the first term, augend is
the sum of the rest) survives, but changing `Sum`/`Prod` from a pair
of expressions to a list of them is an OCaml constructor-argument
change with no Scheme counterpart (a Scheme list already holds any
number of items). `make_sum`/`make_product` fold every numeric term
into one constant and collapse a singleton list to its one element,
generalizing `Deriv`'s two-term rules; `deriv`'s product case applies
the n-ary product rule directly: each term contributes its own
derivative times every other term unchanged, and the contributions
add.

Sample: `ex_2_57 ()`, the derivative of `x * y * (x + 3)` with
respect to `x`, is `Sum [Prod [Var "y"; Sum [Var "x"; Const 3]]; Prod
[Var "x"; Var "y"]]`.

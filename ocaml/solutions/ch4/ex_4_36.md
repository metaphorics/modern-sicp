# Exercise 4.36: unbounded triples (translate)

Replacing `an_integer_between` with a generator that starts from `low`
and has no bound does not work. Depth-first search never leaves the
first `i`, because the innermost generator never runs out. The fair
procedure puts the hypotenuse `k` outermost and searches each finite
`k` completely.

The section's experiment walks every branch, so an unbounded generator
can run only under a ceiling. The ceiling makes fairness observable:

- **Fair procedure.** Raising the ceiling from 20 to 30 only appends
  answers. The first six, `(3 4 5) (6 8 10) (5 12 13) (9 12 15)
  (8 15 17) (12 16 20)`, stay in place, and `(7 24 25)` onward follow
  them. Each triple has a position that no ceiling changes, so a search
  with no ceiling reaches every triple after finitely many answers.
- **Naive procedure.** Raising the ceiling inserts `(7 24 25)` before
  `(8 15 17)`, and more triples land among the old ones. No answer after
  the first few has a fixed position, and with no ceiling the search
  never finishes the first `i`.

# Exercise 2.96: pseudoremainder and content removal

## Approach

Part (a) adds `pseudoremainder_terms`: before dividing, it multiplies
the dividend by the integerizing factor, the divisor's leading
coefficient raised to `1 + O_1 - O_2`, so every quotient coefficient
`div_terms` computes stays an integer. When the dividend's order is
already below the divisor's, no factor is applied, since `div_terms`
answers that case without dividing and the exponent could go negative.
`gcd_terms` then runs Euclid's loop over `pseudoremainder_terms`
instead of the plain remainder. On 2.95's `Q_1` and `Q_2` the raw
answer is `1458x^2 - 2916x + 1458`: integer coefficients, larger than
`P_1`'s, exactly the situation part (b) describes. Part (b) divides the
answer by its content, the greatest common divisor of all its
coefficients (1458 here), and `P_1 = x^2 - 2x + 1` comes back out. The
test asserts both stages: the raw coefficients and the reduced ones.

One normalization rides along: the polynomial GCD is only defined up to
a nonzero constant, so `gcd_terms` pins the sign by requiring a
positive leading coefficient after content removal. Without it the
constant `-1` that this loop produces for some inputs would later flow
into 2.97's integerizing factor and flip a reduced result's sign.

## Map class

`A`: integer division on coefficients needs generic div. The map's Rust
class anticipates scaling coefficients through an arithmetic layer; the
solution does the scaling and the content division on `i128`
coefficients inside the term-list module, with every failure surfaced
as a typed `SchemeError` rather than a panic.

## Deviation

The sign normalization described above is not in the exercise's text;
it is the standard convention for a GCD defined only up to a unit and
matters only for the reduction work of 2.97.

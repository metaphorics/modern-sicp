## Exercise 2.82: coercion the book's own strategy still misses

Approach: [applyGenericMultiCoerce] implements the exercise's stated
strategy -- on a miss, try coercing every argument to the first
argument's type and retry, then to the second's, and so on -- against
[installTowerCoercions] (a coercion chain up the numeric tower) and
[installBlendOperations] (`blend` on `(real, real, real)`, `mix` on
`(rational, real)`). `ex_2_82` runs both: `blend` on three coercible
integers/reals finds the uniform match, but `mix` on `(integer, real)`
never finds `(rational, real)` even though `integer -> rational` is
installed, because the strategy only ever tries coercing *all*
arguments to one target type uniformly -- coercing a subset is not among
its moves, which is exactly where the exercise says to stop and explain.

Class: A, per the map -- the search-and-retry shape is the same idea the
book's own two-argument `apply-generic` uses, generalized to N
arguments over this edition's `KClass`-keyed table.

Deviation: none in substance.

# Exercise 3.80: the series RLC circuit

## Approach

The statement combines the component laws `vR = iR R`,
`vL = L diL/dt`, `iC = C dvC/dt` with the connection laws
`iR = iL = -iC` and `vC = vL + vR` into the state equations

    dvC/dt = -iL / C
    diL/dt = (1/L) vC - (R/L) iL

with the statement's signs: the capacitor voltage decreases when a
positive `iL` runs charge out of it, and the inductor sees `vC`
reduced by the resistor's `R iL`. Figure 3.37 is two `integral` boxes
cross-wired: the `vC` loop's integrand is the `iL` signal scaled by
`-1/C`, and the `iL` loop's integrand is the sum of `vC` scaled by
`1/L` and `iL` scaled by `-R/L`. `RLC(R, L, C, dt)` answers a
procedure from `(vC0, iL0)` to the pair of state streams, each built
with `integralDelayed` over thunks that read the other loop.

## Deviation

The pair order is `vC` first and `iL` second, the statement's own
order ("produces a pair (using cons) of the streams of states `vC`
and `iL`"); the task shorthand listed the current first and was not
followed. The cross-check's Euler loop had to be the simultaneous
(Jacobi) update, both derivatives read from the previous state: a
Gauss-Seidel ordering that updates `vC` before reading it for `diL`
diverges from the stream by O(1) (measured maximum difference
2.63 over the same window), because the network's integrators
sample the other loop's previous output.

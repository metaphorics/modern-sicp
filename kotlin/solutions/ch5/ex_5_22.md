# Exercise 5.22: append and append!

Both machines assume the list-structure memory operations as
primitives: the section's `listOperations(memory)` is the whole
operations table, so every `car`, `cdr`, `cons`, `null?`, and
`set-cdr!` the controllers name runs through the free pointer and the
two vectors.

The `append` machine is exercise 3.12's recursive procedure as
control: while `x` is a pair it saves the car on the stack and walks
the cdr; at the base it answers `y` in `z`; unwinding, it conses each
saved car onto `z`. The copy is the point. Over planted
`x = (1 2 3)` and `y = (4 5)`:

- `z = p7 = (1 2 3 4 5)`: the answer is the last of the three fresh
  cells, the outermost cons.
- `x` is still `(1 2 3)` at the same pointer `p2`, and `free` moved to
  `p8`: exactly three cells were allocated, none of them shared with
  `y`.

The `append!` machine walks `temp` down `x` until the cdr candidate is
`e0`, then splices with `(perform (op set-cdr!) (reg temp) (reg y))`.
The run dumps the memory before and after:

    index    0   1   2   3   4   5   6   7
    the-cars n3  n2  n1  n5  n4  e0  e0  e0
    the-cdrs e0  p0  p1  e0  p3  e0  e0  e0

    index    0   1   2   3   4   5   6   7
    the-cars n3  n2  n1  n5  n4  e0  e0  e0
    the-cdrs p4  p0  p1  e0  p3  e0  e0  e0

One word changed: cell `p0`, the last pair of `x`, drew its cdr as
`e0` before and `p4`, the pointer to `y`, after. The cars are
untouched, no cell is allocated, and `free` is still `p5`. The value
of `append!` is `x` itself, now `(1 2 3 4 5)`: the same pointer the
caller passed, which is why the machine has no `z`.

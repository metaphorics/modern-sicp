# Exercise 2.26: append, cons, and list

## Approach

With `x = (1 2 3)` and `y = (4 5 6)` the three combinators predict three
different printings, and each is built with the module's own `append`,
`cons`, and `list` before printing: `append` makes one six-element list;
`cons(x, y)` makes `x` the first element of a list over `y`'s elements —
the edition types that spine as `List<Showable>`, exactly the mixed
structure the book's picture shows; `list(x, y)` makes a two-element
list of the two lists.

## Numbers

`append` prints `(1 2 3 4 5 6)`; `cons` prints `((1 2 3) 4 5 6)`; `list`
prints `((1 2 3) (4 5 6))`.

## Map class

`R`: the book's predict-the-result question becomes build-and-print —
the values are constructed and `showList` gives the asserted text.

## Deviation

None.

## Exercise 2.71: skewed tree shape

Approach: with weights `1, 2, 4, ..., 2^(n-1)`, the sum of every weight
smaller than the largest is `2^(n-1) - 1`, always less than `2^(n-1)`
itself. So at every merge step, the two smallest elements are the
running "everyone merged so far" subtree and the next power-of-two
leaf, and the single largest leaf is never touched until the very last
merge, where it becomes the tree's root-level sibling. That makes the
shape a "caterpillar": the most frequent symbol sits at depth 1 (its
`encodeSymbol` reads a single bit), and each remaining symbol nests one
level deeper than the last, with the two least frequent symbols (`S0`
and `S1`) tied for the deepest position, `n - 1` levels down. For
`n = 5` the tree therefore needs 1 bit for the most frequent symbol and
4 for the least; for `n = 10`, 1 and 9.

Class: T, per the map; a direct translation, though the book itself
asks only to sketch and reason about the tree; this edition adds
`encodeSymbol`-based code that measures the claim directly rather than
leaving it as an unverified sketch.

Deviation: none in substance.

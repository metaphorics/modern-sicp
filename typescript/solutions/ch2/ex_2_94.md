# Exercise 2.94: the polynomial gcd

## Approach

`remainder-terms` picks the remainder component out of `div-terms`, which
2.91 landed; `gcd-terms` is Euclid over those remainders; `gcd-poly` checks
the indeterminate and reattaches it; and the generic
`greatest-common-divisor` reduces to `gcd-poly` for polynomials and to
ordinary gcd for ordinary numbers. The section system ships the exercise
whole, so the solution file is the book's example run through the generic
entry, hand-checked.

## Numbers

The book's test: `greatest-common-divisor` of `x^4 - x^3 - 2x^2 + 2x` and
`x^3 - x` answers `[polynomial, x, [2, -1], [1, 1]]`: the negative of `x^2 - x`,
a unit multiple, which the hand check confirms by dividing both polys with
zero remainder: the quotients are `-x^2 + 2` and `-x - 1`. The first Euclid
remainder is the same `-x^2 + x`, and the generic operation still answers 12
for gcd(24, 36).

## Map class

A (gcd-terms via Euclid on term lists): the Euclid loop is the 1.2.5
algorithm lifted to term lists through `remainder-terms`.

## Deviation

The computed gcd is the negative of the book's `x^2 - x` because the
remainder sequence fixes the sign; any unit multiple is a gcd, and the hand
check pins the division property rather than the sign.

# Exercise 4.26: unless as a special form versus as a procedure (translate)

The map class is `T`. Ben's side: `unless` as a derived expression is a
rewrite `(unless ⟨c⟩ ⟨u⟩ ⟨e⟩)` to `(if ⟨c⟩ ⟨e⟩ ⟨u⟩)` installed ahead of
the application clause of an applicative-order dispatch, exactly the way
the section lowers `cond`; it works under strictness and delays the
unchosen arm. Alyssa's side: under the lazy evaluator `unless` stays an
ordinary object-language procedure, so it is a value. The demonstration
maps it over a list of triples, one armed with a division by zero in the
arm laziness skips, and answers `(42 7)`. The same value use under the
special-form evaluator answers `Error: unbound variable unless`, which
is the cost Ben's syntax has: syntax is not a first-class object, so it
cannot travel as an argument. This maps the debate onto the edition's
rule that primitives stay strict while compound procedures delay: a
procedure `unless` is only useful eagerly when the language is lazy.

Sample: `ex_4_26 ()` answers
`["42"; "ok"; "42"; "(42 7)"; "Error: unbound variable unless"]`: the
special form under applicative order, the procedure definition and the
same call under laziness, the higher-order use, and the unbound value
under the special form.

# Exercise 3.64: stream-limit convergence helper

## Approach

The statement asks for `stream-limit`, which walks a stream until two
successive elements differ by less than the tolerance and returns the
second. The solution translates it directly over the module's
`sqrtStream` (Newton's guesses for sqrt(x)), plus `sqrtWithin`, the
statement's `(sqrt x tolerance)`. A counted walker,
`streamLimitWithTerms`, records how many elements the search examined:
that count is the convergence-rate datum this section compares. The
map's tailored idea for this row is "compare sequence accelerators
convergence", so the solution also builds `acceleratedSqrtStream`:
Newton's stream pushed through the section's `eulerTransform` tableau
(`accelerateSequence`), the same acceleration exercise 3.65 applies to
the ln 2 series.

## Pins

Computed by running. Within 0.00001: sqrt(2) answers 1.4142135623746899
after 5 guesses (within 1.6e-6 of Math.sqrt(2) = 1.4142135623730951);
sqrt(3) answers 1.7320508075688772 after 6 guesses, exactly
Math.sqrt(3); sqrt(325) answers 18.027756377319946 after 10 guesses,
exactly Math.sqrt(325).

Tailored comparison at tolerance 1e-5 on x = 2: the plain Newton
stream needs 5 terms and lands on 1.4142135623746899; the
euler-accelerated stream needs 4 terms and lands on 1.414213562373095,
one double away from Math.sqrt(2). The acceleration wins exactly one
term here because Newton's iteration is already quadratically
convergent. Measured further: accelerated element 3 is the last
well-defined element. Element 4 is NaN (pinned with Number.isNaN),
because the euler step's denominator s0 - 2s1 + s2 collapses to zero
once three successive Newton guesses agree to the last bit, and the
0/0 poisons every later row. That is why the comparison runs at 1e-5:
a smaller tolerance walks into the NaNs and never terminates.

## Deviation

The accelerated comparison is the map's tailored addition for this
row, not statement text. Its outcome is reported with the degeneration
included: the pinned NaN at element 4 is part of the measured answer,
not a failure to smooth over. The recursive `streamLimit` of the
statement is kept for the direct walks; the counted variant walks
iteratively only so a long search stays flat on the stack.

# Exercise 3.19a (this edition): shared suffix detection

## Approach

The addition builds `x = (a b c d)` and `y = (e f c d)` so that the
pairs holding `(c d)` are one shared structure rather than two
look-alikes, and asks `shares_suffix` to decide the question. The
procedure walks both chains to their ends first, which costs a
length for each, then restarts from the front with the longer chain
advanced by the difference, so the two cursors reach the shared tail
in step and every comparison is between same-depth cells. It answers
`true` for the pair with a genuinely shared tail and `false` for two
separately built `(c d)` chains with equal print, which is the
distinction `equal?` cannot make and identity can.

## Why this addition

The section's Rust idea cell carries "shared suffix detection", and
this edition claims it for Rust. The tail-sharing question is the
natural companion to 3.19's race: both decide a pointer fact by
aligning walks rather than by keeping a history, and the suffix
version needs the one extra trick, stepping the longer chain ahead
by the length difference so the cursors land on the shared cells
together. The solution procedure lives beside the section's other
cycle machinery (`has_cycle_constant_space` and friends) and is
exercised through the same `sec_3_19` test target as the base
exercise.

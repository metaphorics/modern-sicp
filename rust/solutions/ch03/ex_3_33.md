# Exercise 3.33: the averager

## Approach

The book asks for `averager`, a constraint saying `c` is the
average of `a` and `b`, built from the section's primitives. The
Rust solution feeds `a + b = s` as one factor of a `multiplier`
whose other factor is the `constant` 2, constraining the product
against `c`, with no new primitive machinery. The solution sets `a`
to 6 and `b` to 4 and reads 10 off `c`, then forgets and works the
other direction, setting `c` to 20 and `a` to 30 and reading the
constraint's answer for the missing `b` as 10. A second test keeps
one network and computes both directions in a single run, which is
the constraint point: nothing in `averager` names a preferred input.

## Map class

`T`. The construction recipe in the statement's hint is followed
literally with the translated `adder`, `multiplier`, and
`constant`.

## Deviation

None. The definition and the two-directional behavior are the
book's own.

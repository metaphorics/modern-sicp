# Exercise 4.6: let as a derived expression (translate)

`let_to_combination` rewrites `(let ((v e) ...) body)` into
`((lambda (v ...) body) e ...)` built with the typed `lambda` and
`application` constructors, and the new `eval` clause lowers and
evaluates at every depth. The inits are operands of the application,
so they are evaluated in the outer environment, which is the scoping
the statement's equivalence demands and what the shadowing probe pins
down.

Sample: `ex_4_06 ()` answers `["7"; "7"; "5"]`: one `let` through
the clause, the same lowered combination evaluated by the plain base
evaluator, and `(let ((x 2)) (let ((x 3) (y x)) (+ x y)))`, whose
inner inits still see the outer `x`.

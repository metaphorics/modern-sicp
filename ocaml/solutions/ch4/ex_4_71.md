# Exercise 4.71: the explicit delay in simple_query and disjoin (debate)

Why `simple_query` and `disjoin` carry explicit delays. The demonstration
adds one supervisor cycle, Ben -> Warbucks -> Ben, to a Microshaft chain and
asks the recursive `outranked-by` rule of 4.4.1. In the book's engine the
rule body's `Or` is an `interleave_delayed`: constructing the answer stream
stops at the delay, and a bounded read delivers the answers one at a time
even though the cycle makes the stream infinite. Louis's undelayed variants
-- plain `stream_append` in `simple_query`, plain `interleave` in `disjoin`
-- evaluate the rest of the disjunction while the stream is still being
built, so constructing even the first answer descends the infinite chain of
derivations and never returns; the observation bounds that construction with
a rule-application budget, under which the book's engine has long since
answered. Louis's two procedures are substituted into a variant evaluator
assembled from the exported layers (the matcher, the unifier,
`rename_variables_in`, `stream_flatmap`, and the stock evaluator for `Not`
and `Holds` stay the book's); the delay, not the answer set, is the only
difference.

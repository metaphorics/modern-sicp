# Exercise 1.27: the Carmichael numbers fool the Fermat test

## Approach

`passesFermatForAllA` runs the Fermat congruence `a^n === a (mod n)` for
every witness `1 <= a < n` through the section's `expmod`. A prime
passes for honest reasons; a Carmichael number passes for structural
ones - it is squarefree with at least three prime factors each of whose
`p - 1` divides `n - 1`, which forces the congruence for all `a`.

## Numbers

All six Carmichael numbers of footnote 47 - `561, 1105, 1729, 2465,
2821, 6601` - pass for every witness below `n`, while trial division
shows a smallest divisor strictly less than `n` for each: composite, yet
indistinguishable from prime to the Fermat test. `13` and `1009` pass
too, being prime. An ordinary composite like `100` fails at the first
witness tried (`2^100 mod 100 != 2`). This is why the randomized
`fastPrime` of the section answers `true` for `561`: no amount of extra
witnesses helps when every witness lies.

# Exercise 5.10: a new syntax, the simulator untouched

The simulator's syntax is its typed instruction constructors. The new
syntax is a second small language of statements and terms: a term is a
register `R "a"`, a number `N 0`, a label `L "test-b"`, or an operation
call `Call ("rem", [ R "a"; R "b" ])`, and a statement is one of
`Name`, `Set`, `Check`, `Jump_if`, `Jump`, `Push`, `Pop`, and `Do`.
`Set` covers both kinds of assignment, a call on its right making it
an operation assignment, and `Jump` covers both kinds of goto, a label
or a register term choosing between them.

`syntax` is the new syntax procedures: it translates each statement
into exactly one instruction of the simulator and refuses what the
machine cannot run, a call in an operand position or a jump to a
number, with a typed `Bad_instruction`. The book's question: can the
new syntax be installed without changing any part of the simulator
except the syntax procedures? Yes. The translation's output is the
simulator's own instruction list, and everything below it is
unchanged. `ex_5_10 ()` runs the GCD machine in both syntaxes (both
answer 4) and prints the third assembled instruction of each: both are
`Assign_op ("t", "rem", [Reg "a"; Reg "b"])`, the same typed
instruction.

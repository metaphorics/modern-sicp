## Exercise 2.96: pseudodivision

Approach: (a) [pseudoremainderTerms] scales the dividend by the
integerizing factor `c^(1 + O1 - O2)` (`c` the divisor's leading
coefficient) before dividing, so the division introduces no fractions;
the result is the pseudoremainder, exactly the book's
`pseudoremainder-terms`. (b) [coeffContent] is the integer GCD of a term
list's coefficients, and [divideCoeffs] divides every coefficient by it.
[gcdTerms96] is Euclid's algorithm over pseudoremainders, and applies the
content reduction after *every* step rather than only to the final
answer, an edition-level choice that keeps the `Long` coefficients from
growing unboundedly across a long Euclidean chain while landing on the
same reduced result the book's "divide by the answer's own content once"
approach would.

Class: A, per the map; pseudodivision needs the coefficients' own
integer GCD and a generic scale-by-a-`Long` operation, both driven
through the tower's `div`/`mul` rather than Scheme's built-in exact
arithmetic.

Deviation: content reduction runs after every pseudoremainder instead of
only at the end (part (b) as literally stated); this is a strengthening,
not a shortcut; it produces the same final GCD while avoiding
coefficient overflow along the way, and is called out here since it is
an observable difference from the book's two-part staging.

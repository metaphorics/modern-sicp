# Exercise 4.38: multiple dwelling without Smith-Fletcher

## Approach

Dropping the requirement that Smith and Fletcher not live on adjacent
floors loosens the puzzle and the solution set grows from one to five
assignments. The demonstration enumerates all five by search and
counts them two ways: the search itself and an independent brute-force
loop over the 120 complete floor assignments, so the count does not
rest on the evaluator.

## Numbers

The five solutions, in search order: `{ baker: 1, cooper: 2,
fletcher: 4, miller: 3, smith: 5 }`; `{ baker: 1, cooper: 2, fletcher:
4, miller: 5, smith: 3 }`; `{ baker: 1, cooper: 4, fletcher: 2,
miller: 5, smith: 3 }`; the book's `{ baker: 3, cooper: 2, fletcher:
4, miller: 5, smith: 1 }`; and `{ baker: 3, cooper: 4, fletcher: 2,
miller: 5, smith: 1 }`. The brute force agrees: `5` without the
clause, `1` with it. The list matches the sibling editions' pins.

## Class and deviations

Translated row. Deviation: the assignments render as typed records in
the neutral display in place of the book's association lists; the
puzzle's restrictions — including the kept Fletcher-Cooper adjacency —
are the book's own.

## Exercise 2.11: nine-case multiplication

Approach: `mulIntervalCases` tests the signs of `x` and `y`'s endpoints in
order (`x` entirely nonnegative, entirely nonpositive, or spanning zero,
crossed with the same three cases for `y`), and each of the eight cases
where at least one interval does not span zero picks the two corner
products that must be the extremes, verified by hand for each sign
combination. Only the ninth case, where both `x` and `y` span zero,
falls back to `minOf`/`maxOf` over all four products, matching Ben's "only
one of which requires more than two multiplications." A property test
checks `mulIntervalCases` against the straightforward `mulInterval` over
many generated intervals covering every sign combination; `[-2, 3]` times
`[-1, 2]`, the all-span-zero case, is `[-4, 6]`.

Class: T, a direct translation. Deviation: named `mulIntervalCases` rather
than reusing `mulInterval`, since Kotlin cannot redefine a top-level
function under the same name the way a second Scheme `define` would.

# Exercise 4.20 — letrec as a derived expression (translate)

The map class is `T`. The statement's letrec has no shape in the
shared grammar, so the module carries a `shape` of bindings and body
and lowers it exactly like 4.16's scan-out: one `let` reserving every
name with the `*unassigned*` marker, one `set!` per name, then the
body, evaluated by the marker-checking scan-out evaluator duplicated
locally. Part (b) is where Louis's reasoning is loose, and the
lowering shows it: a plain `let` evaluates its initializers before any
frame holds the names, so a recursive binding reads the outer binding
or fails, while letrec reserves the names first. The demonstrations
are the statement's even?/odd? pair under letrec applied to 4, and a
nested letrec whose inner binding shadows the outer `x` and answers
its own value.

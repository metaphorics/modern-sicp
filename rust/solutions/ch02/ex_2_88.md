# Exercise 2.88: polynomial subtraction

## Approach

The solution follows the book's hint, subtraction as addition of a negation. A local `negate` dispatches on the coefficient: integers negate through `checked_neg` (raising `SchemeError::Overflow` on `i128` bounds), reals flip sign, and a tagged polynomial coefficient rebuilds itself through `negate_terms`, which negates every term's coefficient. That recursion is the point: a polynomial whose coefficients are themselves polynomials negates all the way down, so `sub_poly` stays one line, `add(table, p1, negate(table, p2))`, exactly the book's `sub-poly`.

Three probes pin the behavior. `(x^2 + 3x + 7) - (x^2 + 3x)` prints `(polynomial (x (0 7)))`, the constant term left standing; subtracting the polynomial from itself prints `(polynomial (x))`, the empty term list produced when every coefficient cancels to zero and `adjoin_term` drops them through 2.87's `=zero?`; and a nested case subtracts two `x`-polynomials whose constant coefficients are `y`-polynomials, printing `(polynomial (x (0 (polynomial (y (1 2) (0 -1))))))`, the `2y - 1` that remains. The nested result comes out right because coefficient negation re-enters the polynomial arm of `negate`, then re-enters generic `add`, the same shape as addition's recursion.

## Map class

`A`: negation and subtraction as generic ops. `negate` is written once over the coefficient types the system knows, with the polynomial arm recursing into its own term list, and `sub_poly` composes it with the generic `add` rather than duplicating any term-list merge logic.

## Deviation

None.

# Exercise 2.86: generic complex parts

## Approach

The solution rebuilds the complex packages of 2.4.3 over generic coefficients in the section's runtime `Value`: the rectangular package is installed with `real_part` and `imag_part` answered by the pair's own generic parts, `magnitude` squares those parts with the table's `mul` and `add` and only then lowers the sum of squares through `numeric_value` to an `f64` square root, tagged `real`. The `complex` tag forwards each selector into the representation underneath via `apply_generic`, and `add` on two `complex, complex` keys combines real and imaginary parts with the generic `add` rather than `+`. Nothing in the package assumes a coefficient type.

The probe builds `3 + 4i` with both parts made by `make_rational`. Reading back `real_part` prints `(rational (3 . 1))`, the magnitude comes out `(real 5)` after the rationals flow through the generic tower, and adding the number to itself doubles both parts to `(complex (rectangular ((rational (6 . 1)) . (rational (8 . 1)))))`. Those three printed values are the exercise's answer made concrete: every operation dispatched on the types of its arguments, with the parts themselves staying generic through the whole computation.

`numeric_value` accepts plain integers, reals, bare rational pairs, and tagged rationals or reals, raising `SicpError::TypeMismatch` for anything else, which is what lets the trigonometric square root work while the tower does the exact arithmetic around it.

## Map class

`A`: generic coefficients force numeric abstraction layer. The solution embodies exactly that: the coefficient is a generic `Value` at every step, the parts combine through `generic_add`/`generic_mul`, and only `magnitude`'s square root forces the lowering to a plain number.

## Deviation

Only the rectangular representation is installed; there is no polar package and no `angle` entry, and the square root in `magnitude` is an ordinary real-arithmetic operation rather than a new generic trigonometric entry. The file's own comments scope it this way, and the probed operations all run fully generically.

# Exercise 4.48: extend grammar (T)

## Approach

Adjectives join the noun phrase and adverbs the verb phrase. The extension
keeps the section's shape -- a choice among phrase shapes expressed with
`amb` -- with bounded alternatives: zero, one, or two adjectives between the
article and the noun, zero or one adverb after the verb. Boundedness is what
makes the demonstration terminate: an unbounded modifier accumulator never
fails, so the search would enumerate modifier lists forever before it
tries any other attachment. Each parse of "the quick brown cat eats slowly"
consumes the full input. The search reports exhaustion after it tries every
shape.

## Map class

`T`.

## Sample answer

"The quick brown cat eats slowly" parses exactly one way under the extended
grammar: `(sentence (simple-noun-phrase (article the) ((adj quick) (adj
brown)) (noun cat)) (verb-phrase (verb eats) ((adv slowly))))`, then
exhaustion. "The sleepy cat eats" keeps only the one-adjective reading -- the
bare-article reading dies on "sleepy", the two-adjective reading on "cat" --
one parse, then exhaustion.

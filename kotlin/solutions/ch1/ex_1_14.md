## Exercise 1.14: the count-change tree for 11 cents

Approach: a local `cc`, identical in shape to `countChange`'s, increments
a closure-captured counter on every call before doing its own case
analysis. Counting change for 11 cents over five kinds of coins makes 55
calls total; the four ways to make that change (a dime and a penny, two
nickels and a penny, a nickel and six pennies, eleven pennies) sit at the
tree's leaves where `amount = 0`, and the branches that go negative or run
out of coin kinds are the tree's other leaves.

Growth discussion: space is proportional to the tree's maximum depth,
which is bounded by the number of kinds of coins plus the amount divided
by the smallest denomination, so space grows as Theta(amount) for a fixed
coin set. Steps grow with the number of tree nodes, which for a fixed
five kinds of coins grows faster than any polynomial in the amount and is
usually stated as Theta(amount^5) (one factor of growth per kind of coin
the recursion peels off); doubling the amount does not just double the
work, unlike the linear iterative processes earlier in this section.

Class: T (translate). Deviation: the growth-of-resources half of the
exercise is a written analysis, carried above rather than in code, since
"draw the tree" and "state the order of growth" have no return value to
assert; the executable half (55 nodes for 11 cents) is exactly what the
tree diagram would let a reader count by hand.

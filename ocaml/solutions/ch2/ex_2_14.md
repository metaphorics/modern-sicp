# Exercise 2.14: par1 versus par2 (translate)

The map class is `T`. `ex_2_14` builds two small-tolerance intervals
`a` (100 +/- 1%) and `b` (200 +/- 1%), and two resistor intervals `r1`
(6.8 +/- 10%) and `r2` (4.7 +/- 5%), then reads `percent (div_interval
a a)`, `percent (div_interval a b)`, `percent (par1 r1 r2)`, and
`percent (par2 r1 r2)`.

`div_interval a a` is not `[1, 1]`: its percent tolerance comes out at
about 2%, close to `2 * percent a`, because `div_interval` treats the
two occurrences of `a` as independent quantities, unaware they are the
same measurement. `par1 r1 r2` and `par2 r1 r2` likewise differ:
`par1`'s tolerance is noticeably wider than `par2`'s, confirming Lem's
complaint: the same algebraic quantity, `R1 R2 / (R1 + R2)`, computed
two equivalent ways, gives two different answers because interval
arithmetic cannot see that a repeated variable is the same variable.

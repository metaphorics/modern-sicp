## Exercise 2.92: an ordering on variables

Approach: [variableRank] fixes `x > y > z`; [dominantVariable] picks the
higher-ranked of two. [rebase92] lifts a polynomial toward a *higher*
variable as a single order-zero term (no expansion needed) and refuses
the reverse (lowering would expand the polynomial, which the exercise's
hint says to avoid). Coefficient arithmetic gains one new case alongside
the existing polynomial-times-polynomial one: an integer constant
meeting a polynomial becomes a constant polynomial in the poly's own
variable first ([addCoeffs92]/[mulCoeffs92]/[scalePoly92]), the
coercion the section's own footnote asks for. [addPoly92]/[mulPoly92]
rebase both operands to the dominant variable, then run the ordinary
same-variable algorithm. `ex_2_92` mixes `(y+1)x^2 + (y^2+1)x + (y-1)`
against `2x + y^3 + 7`, checking the mixed-variable product's `x^3`
coefficient and a constant folding into `y`.

Class: A, per the map; variable comparison needs an explicit ranking
the book leaves as a design choice; a `Map<String, Int>` is this
edition's version of "some way to compare the variables."

Deviation: none in substance.

## Exercise 3.15: set-to-wow! on z1 versus z2

Approach: the exercise asks for diagrams, so `E3_15Test.kt` turns the
two diagrams into assertions about what each name observes after the
same mutation. `setToWow` performs one operation, `(x.car as
VPair).setCar(VSym("wow"))`: it replaces the car pointer of `x`'s first
pair, touching exactly one box.

Why z1 changes twice and z2 once: `z1 = cons(x, x)` put the same `x`
pair in both slots, so `z1.car === z1.cdr` is true and the single
mutated box is visible from both arms: `((wow b) wow b)`. `z2`'s two
`(a b)` lists were built by two `vlist` calls, hence four distinct
pairs; mutating `z2.car`'s first pair leaves `z2.cdr` alone, printing
`((wow b) a b)`. Before the mutation, `z1` and `z2` printed
identically (`((a b) a b)`), which is the whole moral: equal prints
do not mean equal structure, and mutation is what exposes the
difference.

What the tests say: each test asserts three checkpoints: the identity
fact that the diagrams express (`z1.car === z1.cdr` true, `z2` false),
the identical before-print, and the different after-print. The `z2`
test also asserts `(z2.cdr as VPair).toString() == "(a b)"` so that a
`setToWow` which mutates through every arm of the structure (rather
than only the car) fails even though the top-level print might
coincidentally match.

Class: T (a diagram exercise; this edition's tests make the diagram
observable through assertions instead of drawings).

Deviation: none.

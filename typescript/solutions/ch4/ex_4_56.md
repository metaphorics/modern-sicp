# Exercise 4.56: compound queries (translate)

## Approach

The three compound queries combine `and`, dotted tails, `lisp-value`,
and `not`, using the book's query text verbatim:

1. Join `(supervisor ?person (Bitdiddle Ben))` with
   `(address ?person ?where)` to attach each supervisee's address.
2. Join two `salary` assertions and filter with
   `(lisp-value < ?salary ?bens-salary)`; the `<` predicate is
   registered with `setPredicates` over number leaves.
3. Join `(supervisor ?person ?boss)` with
   `(not (job ?boss (computer . ?type)))` to keep only reports whose
   boss sits outside the computer division.

Tailored idea 4.56a names negated compound examples; part 3 is exactly
that.

## Numbers

Executable results: three supervisee addresses (Alyssa, Cy, Lem);
six people earning less than Ben's 60000 (Alyssa 40000, Cy 35000,
Louis 30000, Lem 25000, DeWitt 25000, Robert 18000; Oliver 150000 and
Scrooge 75000 are excluded); four reports with a non-computer-division
supervisor (Ben, Scrooge, and DeWitt under Oliver Warbucks, Robert
under Scrooge). Tests assert the three addresses, Robert Cratchet's
salary answer, the four part-3 answers, and Louis Reasoner's absence
from part 3 (Alyssa is a computer programmer, so the `not` drops him).

## Class and deviations

Translated row. Deviation: `lisp-value` predicates are host closures
registered by name instead of Scheme procedures, following the core
engine's `setPredicates` API.

# Exercise 4.6: let as a derived expression

## Approach

`letToCombination` rebuilds `(let ((v1 e1) ... (vn en)) body)` as the
combination `((lambda (v1 ... vn) body) e1 ... en)` using the module's
make-lambda, and the eval clause is the evaluation of that
combination in the same environment. The dispatch is the full one so let
also works inside procedure bodies.

## Numbers

`(let ((x 3) (y 4)) (+ x y))` is `7`. `letToCombination` applied to that
form is `equal?` to the hand-written `((lambda (x y) (+ x y)) 3 4)`.
`(define (f) (let ((x 2)) (* x 21)))` then `(f)` is `42`, and
`(let ((x 1)) (set! x 10) x)` is `10`, showing the body runs as a
sequence in the new frame.

## Class and deviations

Translated row. The transformation and the eval clause follow the book
directly; no deviation.

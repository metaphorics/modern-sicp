# Exercise 1.24: the timed Fermat test

## Approach

`timedPrimeTest` wraps `fastPrime` instead of `isPrime`; each trial
draws its witness from the seeded `Random` in the same
`1 + rng.random(n - 1)` shape as the original - `Math.random` would slot in
identically, but the seeded generator makes the transcript replayable.
Medians come from the warmup-plus-median harness of exercise 1.22a at
the 100,000,000 workload.

## Numbers

With seed 1: `fastPrime(1009, 3)` passes, `fastPrime(100, 5)` fails, and
`fastPrime(561, 5)` also passes - 561 is the Carmichael number the
statement's footnote names, which exercise 1.27 pins down. The
Fermat search agrees with exercise 1.22's divisor search on the primes
above 1000 and above 1000000. The medians at the large workload are
positive and stable (the three repetitions land within the asserted
factor of 10). Theta(log n) growth says primes near 1,000,000 should
cost barely more than primes near 1000 - about twice the steps for a
thousandfold range - and the measured medians show far less growth than
the divisor search's, once the coarsened clock has anything to see; the
residual discrepancy is the fixed witness count and the JIT, not the
algorithm.

## Class and deviations

Adapted row, per the map: "Math.random plus performance.now" - both host
primitives are named, with the seeded `Random` standing in for
`Math.random` under the deterministic-testing rule (a test drawing from
an unseedable generator could flake). Timing assertions are stability
bounds on medians, never wall-clock thresholds.

## Exercise 3.29: an or-gate made of and-gates and inverters

Approach: De Morgan's law as wiring. or(a, b) = not(and(not(a),
not(b))), so the compound gate is four lines of circuit construction:
two inverters off the input wires, one and-gate over their outputs,
one final inverter onto the output wire. No new machinery: the whole
point of the exercise is that compound devices are built from the
primitive boxes by construction functions, and the construction
function here is shorter than this paragraph.

Why the delay is 2 * inverterDelay + andGateDelay: the two input
inverters run in parallel, so an input change reaches the and-gate
after one inverter-delay (2); the and-gate adds 3; the final inverter
adds 2. Every path from an input change to the output crosses exactly
those three stages, so the compound gate answers at 7, against the
primitive or-gate's single orGateDelay of 5. The tests pin both
numbers: the compound gate's settled print lands at 7 time units after
a settled input change, and exercise 3.28's test pins the primitive's
at 5.

Why the name differs from the book's: the book reuses `or-gate` for
both the primitive of 3.28 and this compound device, shadowing one
definition with the next. Kotlin compiles both files into one package,
so the two constructors need distinct names; the compound one is
`orGateFromAndGate` and its KDoc records the book's shadowing. The
delay comparison in the tests is what makes the two names more than
bookkeeping: it shows the same behavior delivered at two different
costs, which is the design lesson the exercise is pointing at.

What the tests say: `E3_29Test.kt` runs both gates over all four input
pairs in fresh simulations and checks they agree with each other and
with the arithmetic, then measures the compound delay through a probe
registered after the wiring has settled; registering the probe
before the first propagate would catch the gate's transient
(initially the and-gate sees un-inverted inputs, so the output blips
before it settles), which would measure the settling, not the gate.

Deviation: none beyond the name, recorded above.

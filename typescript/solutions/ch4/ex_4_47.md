# Exercise 4.47: Louis's parse-verb-phrase

## Approach

Louis's consuming-first version answers the ordinary parse, then its
recursive alternative runs forever on exhausted input. The
interchanged version recurses before consuming the verb and cannot
yield even the first parse; it is exhibited as guest source, not run,
because the recursion starts before any failure continuation gives
the engine a safe observation boundary. The Louis program is genuinely
unbounded too: `SearchLimits` returns its one-answer prefix and
executes a finite 16 deferred steps into the unending continuation.
The returned `SearchRun` exposes live failed-computation and step
counts.

## Numbers

The finite prefix contains the parse of "the cat eats". After it,
16 deferred continuations run and the observation ends with status
`cut-off`; at least one failed computation is counted. The tests pin
the answer, step limit, cutoff status, and live counter activity.

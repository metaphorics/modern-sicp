# Exercise 4.46: left-to-right operands (translate)

OCaml leaves the evaluation order of tuple and constructor operands
unspecified. The section's search experiment, like every teaching
engine of the edition, evaluates them left to right, and the parser
depends on this.

The first demonstration makes the order observable. The left operand's
choice point is made first, so it is older and the search varies it
last: `(amb 1 2, amb 3 4)` answers `(1 3) (1 4) (2 3) (2 4)`. Under
right-to-left evaluation it would answer `(1 3) (2 3) (1 4) (2 4)`.

The second demonstration parses "the cat eats" twice. With the simple
noun phrase's operands consumed in the evaluator's order, one parse
comes back. With the right-to-left order written out as explicit `let`
sequencing, the phrase looks for the noun before the article is
consumed, and the search answers nothing.

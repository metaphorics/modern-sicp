# Exercise 4.4: and and or as special forms and derived expressions (translate)

Both halves of the statement are implemented. The direct clauses
evaluate left to right and short-circuit: an `and` answers the false
object at the first false operand, an `or` answers the first true
value; empty `and` is `#t`, empty `or` is `#f`. The rewrites are
`and_to_if`, nesting `if` expressions over `#t`/`#f` leaves, and
`or_to_if`, which keeps each tested operand out of double evaluation
by binding it behind a one-parameter lambda, the temporary the base
language has no `let` to name.

Sample: `ex_4_04 ()` answers `["3"; "3"; "7"; "7"; "#t"; "#f"]`: one
`and` direct and derived, one `or` whose later operand would fail
with a `car` type error if it were evaluated, direct and derived,
then the two empty forms at their identity values.

# Exercise 1.33: filtered accumulate with predicate

## Approach

One extra argument turns `accumulate` into `filteredAccumulate`: a range value
that fails the predicate contributes the null value instead of its term.
`sumOfSquaresOfPrimes` runs it with `+`/0, the square term, and the
smallest-divisor `isPrime` of 1.2.2; `productOfRelativelyPrimes` runs it with
`*`/1, the identity term, and `gcd(i, n) = 1` over `1..n-1`.

## Numbers

The primes in `2..10` are `2, 3, 5, 7`, so the filtered sum is
`4 + 9 + 25 + 49 = 87`; over `2..20` it is `1027`; over `14..16` (no primes)
`0`. The integers below 10 that are relatively prime to 10 are `1, 3, 7, 9`,
whose product is `189`; below 12 they are `1, 5, 7, 11`, product `385`; below
2 only `1`, product `1`. An all-passing filter leaves a plain `accumulate`,
checked against the sum `55`.

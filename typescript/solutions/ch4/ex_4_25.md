# Exercise 4.25: unless breaks under applicative order

## Approach

The same unless-based factorial runs under two evaluators. The lazy
evaluator delays unless's arms, the condition forces, and only the chosen
arm is ever demanded, so the recursion bottoms out. The strict 4.1
evaluator keeps the edition's strict-argument rule: the recursive operand
evaluates before unless is entered, so the armed call
`(unless (= 1 1) (/ 1 0) 42)` dies in the division, and the strict
factorial never reaches its base case. The strict side runs with a step
budget installed on the subtraction primitive, which every level of the
descent calls, so the predicted non-termination answers as a typed budget
error instead of hanging the run.

## Numbers

Lazy `(factorial 5)` answers `120`. The armed strict call fails with
`/: expects a nonzero divisor list`. The strict factorial under the budget
fails with `the step budget ran out: factorial is still descending`.

## Class and deviations

Translated row. Deviation: the book's "runs forever" is observed through a
budget on the descending primitive rather than an actual hang, because the
4.1 dispatch recurses internally and offers no step hook; the budget turns
the divergence into an observable typed error. The mechanism the exercise
asks about is unchanged.

# Exercise 4.72: interleave, not append, in `disjoin` (translate)

The variant evaluator keeps every layer of the base evaluator and replaces
only the `or` combination: its `disjoin` merges the disjunct streams
with `stream_append_delayed`: the same explicit delay the book's
`disjoin` uses, so the demonstration isolates the append-versus-
interleave choice from 4.71's delay question. `and` is re-implemented
in the variant so a nested `or` keeps the variant; every other form,
errors included, falls back to the base `qeval`.

The demonstration query ors an infinite disjunct with a finite one: the
swap rule `(rule (loves ?x ?y) (loves ?y ?x))` over the single
assertion `(loves (Minnie Mouse) (Mickey Mouse))` answers forever,
alternating the two matchings, while exactly three people supervise
Ben. The book's interleaved `or` serves both disjuncts in proportion.
the three supervisor rows all land inside the first six answers. The
appended variant starves the second disjunct: no supervisor row
appears, whatever the bound (checked at 8 and at 20), because the
first stream never runs out. That is 3.5.3's `interleave`-versus-
`append` lesson verbatim.

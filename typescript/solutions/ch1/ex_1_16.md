# Exercise 1.16: invariant-based fast exponentiation as a loop

## Approach

Keep `(a, b, n)` with `a * b^n` constant. When `n` is even, halve it and
square `b`; when `n` is odd, move one factor of `b` into `a`. The answer
is `a` once `n` hits `0`. Each loop pass halves `n` or decrements it, so
the pass count is at most `2 * log2(n)`. The states for `(2, 10)` show
the invariant visibly: `(1, 2, 10) (1, 4, 5) (4, 4, 4) (4, 16, 2)
(4, 256, 1) (1024, 256, 0)` - `a * b^n` is `1024` at every one.

## Numbers

`fastExptIter(2, 10)` is `1024`, `(3, 5)` is `243`, `(5, 0)` is `1`,
`(2, 40)` is `1099511627776`. The loop matches the section's recursive
`fastExpt` over the grid `b` in `2..5`, `n` in `0..20`, and the invariant
holds at all six states of the `(2, 10)` run.

## Class and deviations

Adapted row, per the map: "loop replaces tail recursion". The book's
iterative `fast-expt` is a tail-recursive call chain; on Node every call
takes a frame, so the state transformation is written as the loop body
and the invariant does the book's work of proving the process correct.
No other deviation.

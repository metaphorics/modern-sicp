# Exercise 3.37: expression-style constraint combinators

## Approach

The book asks to redefine the converters as expression-style
combinators such as `(c+ x y)` and `(c* x y)`, so a converter is one
expression instead of a sequence of `define`s naming every
intermediate connector. The Rust solution writes `c_add`, `c_mul`,
`c_sub`, `c_div`, and `c_const`. Each makes a fresh result connector
on the network, wires the primitive constraint into its arguments,
and returns the connector. The Celsius converter is the expression
`c_mul(c_mul(c, five_nine), c_div(one, nine_five))` from the book's
own arithmetic. The solution drives it from the Celsius side, reading 77
for 25 degrees, then after forgetting from the Fahrenheit side, reading
100 for 212. A second test shows `c_sub` running in both directions,
the trick the book performs for subtraction and division from `adder`
and `multiplier`.

## Map class

`T`. The combinator idea, the fresh-result-connector shape, and the
converter rebuild are the statement's own.

## Deviation

None. The combinators are defined only from the section's
primitives, as the exercise requires.

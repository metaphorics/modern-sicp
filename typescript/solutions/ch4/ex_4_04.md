# Exercise 4.4: all and any as special forms

## Approach

The book offers two routes; this solution pins the special-form route.
The variadic `all` and `any` are extension nodes beside the binary
`logical` node and are evaluated by their own cases: each operand is
evaluated at most once, evaluation stops at the first decisive operand,
the result is boolean, and a non-boolean operand is a `bad-operand`
fault — the same strict rule the guest `logical` node enforces, since
the accepted grammar excludes Scheme truthiness. The typed boundary
matters here: `AllNode` and `AnyNode` are outside the shared `Expr`
union, and shared `Stmt` bodies cannot contain them. Procedure-body
probes therefore use the equivalent derived conditional route; they do
not establish nested execution of the special-form nodes.
The second route stays in the file as the comparison the exercise asks for: `allToConditional`
and `anyToConditional` derive the forms over the conditional expression
(`all(a, b, c)` becomes `a ? (b ? c : false) : false`).

## Numbers

`all()` is `true` and `any()` is `false`. `all(true, true, true)` is
`true`; `all(true, false, true)` is `false`; `any(false, false, true)`
is `true`; `any(false, false)` is `false`. `all(1, 2, 3)` and
`any(7, true)` are `error:bad-operand`, not values. Effects stop at the
decisive operand: `all(false, hits = 99)` is `false` with `hits` at `0`,
`any(true, hits = 99)` is `true` with `hits` at `0`, and
`all(hits = 99, true)` runs the write (`hits` becomes `99`) and then
faults. Evaluation counts: `all(probe(true), probe(false), probe(true))`
stops at `2` probes; `any(probe(false), probe(false), probe(true))`
counts `3`. In procedure-body probes the tests use the derived conditional
expressions: `both(true, true)` is `true`, `both(true, false)` is `false`,
and `fallback()` is `true`. The derived route answers `false` for
`all(1, 2)` instead of faulting and counts the same `3` probes on
`any(probe(false), probe(false), probe(true))`.


## Class and deviations

Translated row with recorded re-cut: the old SICP value-returning pins
`(and 1 2 3)` = `3` and `(or 7 ...)` = `7` rest on Scheme truthiness and
cannot survive the typed guest; they migrate to `bad-operand` fault
assertions, and the old `(and true false true)`-shaped answers become
the boolean cases above. The short-circuit-before-effects pins and the
evaluation-count pins are kept verbatim, because they are the
exercise's real objective: single evaluation, effects stopping at the
decisive operand. The derived route's silent non-`true`-as-false
treatment is the documented reason the special form is the pinned one.
A-reason: `AllNode` and `AnyNode` are experiment-local nodes outside the
shared `Expr` and `Stmt` unions, so a procedure body cannot contain those
special forms. Tests keep direct special-form behavior at the supported
expression boundary and use the derived conditional expressions in
procedure-body positions; no nested special-form behavior is claimed.

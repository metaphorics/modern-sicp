# Exercise 2.15: is Eva right that `par2` is a "better" program?

## Approach

Eva's diagnosis, restated in the exercise: a formula that mentions an
uncertain variable fewer times gives a tighter (smaller percentage
tolerance) answer, because interval arithmetic cannot recognize that two
occurrences of the same variable are correlated. `par1` mentions `r1`
and `r2` twice each; `par2` mentions each once. Reusing exercise 2.14's
two resistors, `par2`'s percentage tolerance (5.0 percent) is indeed
smaller than `par1`'s (14.9 percent), confirming Eva's claim for this
representative case.

# Exercise 4.54: require as a special form (translate)

The book's `require` is a user procedure built on `(amb)`, the choice
point with no alternatives. The subset's `amb` always has two
alternatives, so a guest program cannot express failure, and `require`
must be a special form. The solution's clause is the completed
`analyze-require`. It evaluates the predicate and fails the branch when
the predicate is false, so the blanks are `(not pred-value)` and
`(fail2)`. Otherwise it answers `()` and the branch continues.

The clause takes precedence over the experiment's own `require` and
replaces it. On the three demonstration programs, the clause's
transcripts match the experiment's evaluator exactly. A true
requirement answers, a false one fails its only branch, and a
requirement that filters 1 to 4 for even numbers answers 2 and 4.

# Exercise 2.11: `mulInterval` by nine sign-tested cases

## Approach

`signOf` classifies an interval as positive (`lo > 0`), negative
(`hi < 0`), or spanning, and a chain of eight cases returns the two
endpoint products that bound the result; the ninth (both spanning) falls
through to the four-product computation, which is Ben's "only one of the
nine requires more than two multiplications". Each two-product case is
derived by treating a negative interval as the negation of a positive one
and tracking the flips - for instance positive times negative keeps the
most-negative product `x.hi * y.lo` and the least-negative `x.lo * y.hi` -
rather than guessed from the endpoint labels.

## Numbers

The exhaustive test walks every ordered pair of endpoints from the grid
`{-5, -2, -0.5, 0, 0.5, 2, 5}` for `x` and `y` - 405 ordered interval
pairs covering all nine sign combinations, including the zero-touching
boundaries - and requires `mulIntervalFast` to equal the naive
multiplication exactly in each one.

## Map class

`T`: Ben's suggestion translates directly into a case split over the two
intervals' sign classes.

None.

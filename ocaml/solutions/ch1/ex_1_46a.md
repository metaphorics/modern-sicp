# Exercise 1.46a — lazy sequence of successive guesses (addition)

Chosen tailored addition for section 1.3 (`chosen: ocaml` recorded
against this row in `docs/exercise-map.md`); 1.43a was already claimed
by the Kotlin edition.

`iterative_improve` commits to one stopping rule the moment it is
called: it runs until `good_enough`, then returns a single value. But
"keep improving until it is good enough" is really two separate ideas
the book fuses together — the improvement step, and when to stop
watching — and OCaml's `Seq.t` can express the first without deciding
the second. `sqrt_guesses x` returns the infinite lazy sequence of
average-damped guesses at the square root of `x`, starting from `1.`:
each cell computes on demand, so a caller can `Seq.take` as many
guesses as it wants, inspect them, or search them with its own
`good_enough`, all from the same sequence, without `sqrt_guesses`
itself ever deciding when "good enough" is reached.

`ex_1_46a ()` forces the first five guesses toward `sqrt 2.`:
`[1.; 1.5; 1.4166666666666665; 1.4142156862745097; 1.4142135623746899]`,
which already matches `Sec_1_3.Average_damping.sqrt 2.`'s converged
value `1.4142135623746899` by the fifth guess — visible proof that the
sequence and the section's own bounded search agree, just observed
differently.

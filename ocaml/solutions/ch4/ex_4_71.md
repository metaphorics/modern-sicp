# Exercise 4.71 — the explicit delay in simple_query and disjoin (debate)

The demonstration adds one supervisor cycle, Ben -> Warbucks -> Ben, to a
Microshaft chain and asks the recursive `outranked-by` rule of 4.4.1. In the
book's engine the rule body's `or` is an `interleave_delayed`: constructing
the answer stream stops at the delay, and `query_upto` delivers the answers
one at a time even though the cycle makes the stream infinite. Louis's
undelayed variants — plain `stream-append` in `simple_query`, plain
`interleave` in `disjoin` — evaluate the rest of the disjunction (and, under
the series `conjoin`, the whole second conjunct) while the stream is still
being built, so constructing even the first answer descends the infinite
chain of derivations and never returns. The variant evaluator assembles
Louis's two procedures verbatim with the exported layers (`find_assertions`,
`fetch_rules`, `unify_match`, `rename_variables_in`, `negate`,
`lisp_value`, `stream_flatmap` stay the book's; `conjoin` is rebuilt in the
book's shape so it evaluates through Louis's `qeval`); the delay, not the
answer set, is the only difference. Because the undelayed construction never
returns, the observation bounds it: after a budget of 1000 rule applications
the construction still had not produced the first answer, while the delayed
engine had long since answered — the budgeted count is the pinned evidence
that the divergence is in construction, not in the answers.

# Exercise 4.45: five parses of ambiguous sentence (T)

## Approach

The section's fully extended grammar -- both noun phrases and verb
phrases extend by prepositional phrases through `maybe-extend`, with
`parse-word` consuming and restoring `*unparsed*` through the trail.
The sentence is the book's: "The professor lectures to the student in
the class with the cat". The test pins all five parse trees exactly as
the engine prints them and the exhaustion after the fifth, so the
attachment order is fully determined: verb-phrase nesting first
(innermost attachment wins), then the noun-phrase attachments as the
outer choices resume.

## Map class

`T`.

## Sample answer

Five parses: (1) both phrases nest on the verb phrase,
lectures[[to student] in class] with; (2) "with the cat" attaches
inside "in the class"'s noun phrase; (3) "in the class" attaches inside
"to the student"'s noun phrase; (4) and (5) both attach at the
student's noun phrase, left- and right-nested. Then no more values.

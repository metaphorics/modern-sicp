# Exercise 4.22: let in the analyzed evaluator

## Approach

A grouped binding is a derived expression, so `analyzeLet` analyzes the
combination it stands for: `call(lam(params, bodyDecls), inits)` —
exactly the direct evaluator's `letToCall` target, which is why this
solution borrows that derivation instead of spelling a second one. The
analyzed form therefore equals the direct form by construction,
closures made by the analyzed lambda store their body's execution
procedure, and no let machinery exists at run time. Total lowering over
the input syntax means grouped bindings nest in expressions and bodies
the same way.

## Numbers

`(let x = 3)` over `x + 4` through the analyzed evaluator answers `7`.
The nested-body `f` answers `20` for `4`. Nested grouped bindings
answer `12`, and the fully derived hand-written combination answers
`12` too: the derivation agrees with direct evaluation. The execution
procedure returned by `analyzeLet` answers `7` on a fresh global
environment.

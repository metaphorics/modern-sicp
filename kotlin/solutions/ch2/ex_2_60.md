## Exercise 2.60: duplicate-allowed sets

Approach: `elementOfSetDup` is unchanged, still `Θ(n)`. `adjoinSetDup`
drops the membership check entirely and always prepends, so it falls
from `Θ(n)` to `Θ(1)`. `unionSetDup` no longer needs to filter `set1`
against `set2`, so a plain concatenation suffices, `Θ(n)` instead of the
non-duplicate representation's `Θ(n²)`. `intersectionSetDup` keeps the
exact same algorithm as before (duplicates propagate into the
result once per matching occurrence), so it stays `Θ(n²)`.

Applications: this representation suits write-heavy workloads that
adjoin far more often than they read, such as a multiset counting
occurrences, or a log of events where later deduplication (or none at
all) is acceptable; the non-duplicate representation is better whenever
`adjoinSet` and `unionSet` are rare relative to membership tests, since
`elementOfSet`'s cost dominates either way and a bigger duplicate-laden
list makes every future scan slower.

Class: T, per the map; a direct translation of the book's own asked-for
procedures.

Deviation: none.

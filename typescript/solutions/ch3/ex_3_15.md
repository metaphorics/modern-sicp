# Exercise 3.15: setToWow shows sharing

## Approach

The book asks for box-and-pointer diagrams of `set-to-wow!` over z1
and z2. The edition builds both with the module's pairs: `makeZ1` is
`cons(x, x)` over one `[a, b]` pair, `makeZ2` conses two distinct `[a, b]`
copies, and `setToWow` is `setCar` of wow on the head pair of the
argument. The diagrams become pins: the printed shapes before and
after, plus identity checks that say which pairs are shared. Both
structures are improper lists whose cdr continues the chain, so the
printer shows the cdr side as trailing elements, not a nested list.

## Pins

Before the mutation, z1 and z2 print the same shape, [[a, b], a, b],
the book's own setup. The identity checks distinguish them: z1's car
element and cdr element are the same object (Object.is true), z2's
are not (false). After setToWow: z1 prints [[wow, b], wow, b],
because the one shared [a, b] pair was mutated and is visible through
both pointers; z2 prints [[wow, b], a, b], because only the car copy
was mutated.

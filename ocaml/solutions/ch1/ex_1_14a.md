# Exercise 1.14a: measuring the count-change growth prediction (addition)

Added by this edition (`chosen: ocaml`); extends exercise 1.14, whose
accepted answer states `Theta(a^5)` steps without measuring. `ex_1_14a`
counts calls at four doubling amounts using the same instrumented `cc`:

| amount | calls     | ratio to previous |
|-------:|----------:|-------------------:|
|     50 |     1,571 |                 — |
|    100 |    15,499 |                9.86 |
|    200 |   229,589 |               14.81 |
|    400 | 4,642,025 |               20.22 |

A `Theta(a)` (linear) process would hold every doubling ratio at 2. A
`Theta(a^5)` process approaches a doubling ratio of `2^5 = 32` as `a`
grows large, from below. The measured ratios climb steadily:
9.86, then 14.81, then 20.22, consistent with `a^5` growth, but still far
short of 32 even at 400 cents. (Continuing the doubling by hand: 400 to
800 measures 115,126,481 calls, a ratio of 24.80, still climbing but not
close to 32.) The trend says the asymptotic answer is real but slow to
show itself at amounts anyone would actually price change for: a
five-fold exponent needs a very large `a` before its doubling ratio
approaches its limit, because the lower-order terms in the exact call
count (contributions from fewer than all five coin kinds) still matter at
these amounts. Measuring at 50 cents and concluding the growth is merely
quadratic would be a natural, wrong reading of too small a sample, which
is the point of the exercise.

# Exercise 4.12: the per-frame search and the frame-chain walk as abstractions

## Approach

The book observes that `lookup-variable-value` and
`set-variable-value!` differ only in what they do once they find the
binding, and asks for the two loops as named abstractions with the
operations redefined on top. Here those are `frameScan`, the `assoc`
over one frame's alist representation (Exercise 4.11's), answering the
first `(name . value)` pair or null, and `envScan`, the walk over
the frame chain, answering the nearest pair or null. `ScannedFrames`
redefines the operations purely as clients of the two scans: lookup is
`envScan` then the pair's value, `set!` is `envScan` then rewrite the
pair's value slot, with the unbound case the one fault each. `define`
conses without scanning, matching the book's define-variable!, and
extend-environment keeps the runtime's arity contract. The demo programs
are 4.11's, and the direct pins exercise the scans themselves: a hit in
the one frame, a miss, a hit seen through a child frame's chain walk.

## Map class

`T`.

## Deviation

None. The `#f`-of-the-book returns are `null` in Kotlin; the operations
raise the same typed `Unbound` faults on the miss path.

## Answer

- `(define z 2) z` prints `2`; the inner-frame `set! z 10` then lookup
  prints `10`; `z` again prints `10`: transcript `"2\n10\n10\n"`.
- `z` bound only inside a returned call's frame reads
  `"2\nError: unbound variable: z\n"`.
- `frameScan("z", global)` after `(define z 2)` answers the pair that
  prints `(z . 2)`; `frameScan("w", global)` and `envScan("w", global)`
  answer null; `envScan` from a fresh child frame finds the global pair.
- Arity mismatch: `"Error: extend: wrong number of arguments, expected
  2, got 1\n"`.

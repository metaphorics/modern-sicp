# Exercise 3.35: squarer as a primitive constraint

## Approach

Ben Bitdiddle's answer to Louis's flaw: define the squarer as a new
primitive constraint, filling in the book's outline. The edition
spells the outline in the module's adder/multiplier shape.
`processNewValue`: a value on `a` sets `b` to its square; a value on
`b` sets `a` to its square root; a negative `b` is a square less than
zero, the book's `error`, thrown as `Error("squarer: negative")`.
`processForgetValue` withdraws both claims (only ones the squarer set
are lost) and rechecks, so the device can turn around and repropagate
from the other terminal, the property Louis's multiplier lacked.

## Pins

Setting a = 7 makes b read 49. On a fresh network, setting b = 49
makes a read 7. Forget flow, measured: a = 7 gives b = 49; forgetting
a as user leaves both a and b unset (the squarer's claims were
withdrawn); then setting b = 49 makes a read 7 again. After
a = 7, b = 49, setting b to 50 throws the module's `ContradictionError`.
On a fresh network, setting b = -4 throws `squarer: negative` and
leaves a with no value.

## Map class

`T: primitive constraint in the module's adder shape, over the
module's connectors`.

# Exercise 1.22a: warmup plus median timings for the prime search

## Approach

A single timed run of `searchForPrimes` measures the wrong things: the
first passes pay for JIT compilation and inlining, `performance.now()`
ticks are coarsened to about 100 microseconds without cross-origin
isolation, and one sample carries whatever the scheduler was doing. The
harness therefore runs the search once as a warmup round whose times are
discarded, then takes `rounds` (default 9) timed rounds and reports
their **median** - the p50, robust to the one slow tail round a median
would ignore but a mean would not. Assertions are structural: the primes
are the pinned ones, the samples are the right count, the median is
positive and finite, and the medians of repeated harness runs stay
within a generous factor of each other - a variance bound, never an
exact millisecond value.

## Numbers

Above 100,000,000 the three smallest primes are `[100000007, 100000037,
100000039]`, and one search costs a few hundred microseconds to a few
milliseconds on this host - safely above the clock's tick. Three
repetitions of the harness (warmup + 9 rounds each) produced medians
within a factor of 10 of each other, with the bound asserted, not the
values. Re-running the harness over the four original thresholds shows
the medians growing with the square root of the decade - as
Theta(sqrt(n)) predicts - but with visible flat spots from loop overhead
and the JIT, which is the discrepancy the statement of exercise 1.22
asks about.

## Class and deviations

Tailored addition (chosen: typescript, per the map), extending exercise
1.22 with the measurement discipline the host demands: warmup round,
median over rounds, stability bounds instead of wall-clock thresholds,
and a workload (start 100,000,000) sized so the coarsened clock can see
it at all.

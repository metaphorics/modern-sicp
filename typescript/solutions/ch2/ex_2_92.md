# Exercise 2.92: polynomials in different variables

## Approach

An ordering on the indeterminates (here `x` over `y` over `z`) decides which
poly is promoted: combining two polys of different variables lifts the lower
poly into the higher variable as a constant coefficient, term by term, and
same-variable combinations run the section's ordered merge with the
coefficients combined through generic `add` and `mul`, lifting again whenever
the coefficients themselves disagree in variable.

## Numbers

Adding `5x + (y + 1)` lifted per the book's footnote to `x^2 + 2x + 1`
answers `(polynomial x (2 (polynomial y (1 1) (0 2))) (1 2) (0 6))`;
multiplying `x` by `y` answers `(polynomial x (1 (polynomial y (1 1))))`;
and same-variable addition still merges through the ordered union.

## Map class

A (variable ordering needs symbolic comparison keys): the ordering is a rank
map over the variable names.

## Deviation

The promotion materializes the lifted poly eagerly at each coefficient, the
book's footnote spelling, rather than delaying to a canonical form.

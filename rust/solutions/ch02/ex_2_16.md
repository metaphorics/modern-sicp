# Exercise 2.16: equivalent algebraic expressions can give different answers

## Approach

The exercise asks whether an interval-arithmetic package free of this
shortcoming is possible, warning the problem is difficult; SICP does not
expect a general solution, only an account of why the difficulty arises.
This solution demonstrates the simplest instance: `x - x` for a single
interval `x` (10.0 at 5 percent tolerance) comes out to `[-1.0, 1.0]`,
not the exact zero-width `[0.0, 0.0]` ordinary algebra gives, because
`sub_interval` (exercise 2.8's) treats the two occurrences of `x` as
independent quantities and subtracts their bounds accordingly. No
package built from independent endpoint bookkeeping alone can avoid
this: it has no way to notice that two syntactic occurrences of a
variable name the same quantity, so it cannot cancel them the way
algebraic substitution does. Exercises 2.14 and 2.15's `A / A` and
`par1` versus `par2` are the identical phenomenon on more elaborate
expressions; this file's `x - x` case is the smallest one that shows it.

`T`: the demonstration and its explanation translate directly; no

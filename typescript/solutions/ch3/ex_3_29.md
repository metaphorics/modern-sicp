# Exercise 3.29: the compound or-gate from and-gates and inverters

## Approach

The book builds or(a, b) as not(and(not a, not b)): `orGateFromAnd` wires
two inverters into an and-gate whose output feeds a final inverter over the
module agenda. To answer the delay question the solution measures both gate
kinds on identical protocols (`measureCompoundOr`, `measurePrimitiveOr`):
build, run one unprobed settle propagate so the attach-time initialization
items of exercise 3.31 fire, attach the probe, then raise `a1` and
propagate.

## Pins

Computed by running. The settle leaves both outputs false and ends at clock
5, where the probe attaches (events begin `{time: 5, value: false}` on both
circuits). A rise at clock 5 moves the compound output at time 12: input
inverter 2 + and-gate 3 + output inverter 2 = 7 units after the rise. The
primitive answers at time 10: 5 units. A later fall answers 7 units after
itself (event at 19 after the fall at 12). The book's usual delay answer is
one and-gate plus one inverter (5): that counts the and plus the output
inverter only, and the measured 7 is that 5 plus the input inverter stage
the construction requires. The run is ground truth, and the compound gate
agrees with the primitive on settled values.

## Deviation

The settle-then-probe protocol is this edition's measurement discipline:
probing during construction would log the initialization transient (the
output wire briefly claims true at time 2 before the settle pulls it back),
which is exercise 3.31's mechanism and not part of the delay answer.

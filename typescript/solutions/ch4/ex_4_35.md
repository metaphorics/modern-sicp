# Exercise 4.35: an-integer-between and triples

## Approach

The statement's `an-integer-between` is the bounded generator the
search language needs for the triples program: it fails on the empty
range and otherwise offers `low` ambiguously against the rest. The
choice points sit in the order `i`, then `j` from `i`, then `k` from
`j`, with the requirement checked at the innermost level.

## Numbers

Under the depth-first experiment the triples between 1 and 20 come out
ordered by ascending `i`, then `j`, then `k`: `[3, 4, 5]`,
`[5, 12, 13]`, `[6, 8, 10]`, `[8, 15, 17]`, `[9, 12, 15]`,
`[12, 16, 20]`, then exhaustion. The order is a property of the
search, not of the set, and the demonstration pins it.

## Class and deviations

Translated row; the order matches the sibling editions' pins.
Deviation: the answers are typed triples rendering in the neutral
bracket display in place of the book's parenthesized lists.

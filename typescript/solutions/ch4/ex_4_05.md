# Exercise 4.5: recipient clauses in the switch case analysis

## Approach

This edition replaces SICP exercise 4.5 with a host-language exercise
that keeps its number and teaching objective. The book's `(test =>
recipient)` clause moves from `cond` to the switch case analysis: a
recipient clause carries a one-argument procedure called with the
matched value. `switchToIf` is the transformation the book prescribes:
the discriminant is evaluated exactly once and bound as
`@@switch-value`, then the clauses become a nest of ifs comparing the
bound value with each case test. A recipient clause's consequent applies
its recipient to `@@switch-value`; a body clause runs its body; the
default body is the final `else`, and an analysis with no match and no
default answers `undefined`. The single binding is the book's
recommended shape: the discriminant runs once and its value reaches the
recipient, so no second evaluation can leak in.

## Numbers

Over `entries = [{key:"a",value:1},{key:"b",value:2}]` with recipient
`entryValue`: the analysis of `lookup("b", entries)` answers `2` and
`lookup("a", entries)` answers `1`; `lookup("c", entries)` with the
default body answers `"missing"` and without one answers `undefined`. A
counting discriminant (`bump`) is evaluated exactly once — the count
stays `1` while the recipient still answers `2`. A body clause answers
its body's value, `7`.

## Class and deviations

`R` row: the replacement keeps the number and the case-analysis
objective under the fixed opener above. Deviations: the surface is the
typed switch, not the book's `cond` clause list; the clause tests are
case values compared with `===` against `@@switch-value`, matching the
guest switch's own rule. The old solution's single-evaluation deviation
note carries over as the central mechanism here — the generated binding
is what preserves it, and the count pin proves it.

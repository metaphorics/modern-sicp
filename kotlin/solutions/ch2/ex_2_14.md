## Exercise 2.14: repeated uncertain variables demonstrate Lem is right

Approach: for `a` a 5 percent tolerance around 100, `a / a` computes to
about 9.975 percent tolerance, not the mathematically exact zero, because
`divInterval` treats the numerator's `a` and the denominator's `a` as two
independent unknowns each ranging over the same interval. `a / b`, built
from `a` and a genuinely independent `b` (10 percent around 200), reports
about 14.925 percent, close to the sum of the two tolerances, matching
exercise 2.13's small-tolerance approximation for a formula with no
repeated variable. The gap between the two demonstrates exactly why
`par1` and `par2`, which repeat their arguments a different number of
times, disagree.

Class: T, a direct translation of an investigative exercise into an
executable demonstration with the same two expressions the statement
names, `a / a` and `a / b`. Deviation: none.

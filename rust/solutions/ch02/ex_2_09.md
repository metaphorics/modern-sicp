# Exercise 2.9: the width of a sum is a function of the widths of the addends, but the width of a product is not

## Approach

`add(a, b).width() == a.width() + b.width()` holds for `[2, 6] + [10,
14]`: both sides equal 4.0, confirming the additive law algebraically
(the sum's bounds are the addends' bounds added pairwise, so half their
difference is exactly the sum of the two halves). For multiplication,
`[2, 6] * [10, 14]` and `[2, 6] * [100, 104]` share one factor and the
other two factors share the same width (4.0 on center 12, 4.0 on center
104), yet the products' widths differ (32.0 versus 212.0): the width of
a product depends on the factors' centers, not only their widths.

`Interval`'s constructor enforces `lower <= upper`, so `width()` is
always well defined; this exercise is about the mathematics of that
well-defined width, not about deciding whether to enforce the invariant
in the first place (the section's representative program already
settles that).

## Map class

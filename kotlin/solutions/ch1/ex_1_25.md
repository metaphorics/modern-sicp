## Exercise 1.25: is Alyssa's shortcut correct?

Approach: `expmod(7, 200, 13) = 3`, computed by folding a `remainder`
into every squaring step so no intermediate value ever exceeds `13^2`.
Alyssa's shortcut computes `7^200` in full before reducing. Over `Long`,
`7^200` is astronomically larger than `Long.MAX_VALUE`, so
`fastExptWrapping` silently wraps through 64-bit two's-complement
arithmetic at every squaring, landing on a value whose remainder modulo
13 is 0, not 3. Over `BigInteger`, `fastExptBig` never overflows and its
remainder modulo 13 does agree with `expmod`'s answer, but it builds and
retains the full several-hundred-digit power before reducing, work
`expmod` never does because it reduces after every multiplication.

Class: A (adapt). Alyssa is mathematically correct (`remainder(base^exp,
m)` and `expmod(base, exp, m)` compute the same number), but "correct" and
"serves as well" come apart on a fixed-width host in a way SICP's
unbounded-precision Scheme numbers never expose: the naive shortcut is
silently wrong over `Long` and merely wasteful over `BigInteger`, so it
would not serve the fast prime tester on this host either way, for two
different reasons than the book's single "it does more multiplications"
argument.

# Exercise 2.97: reducing rational functions

## Approach

Part (a) implements `reduce_terms` over term lists: compute the
pseudodivision GCD (2.96's `gcd_terms`), multiply numerator and
denominator by the integerizing factor built from the GCD's leading
coefficient raised to `1 + O_1 - O_2` (with `O_1` the larger of the two
input orders), divide both by the GCD, exact by construction, then
remove the redundant factor, the content of the numerator and
denominator taken together. `reduce_poly` is the `add_poly` shape: it
checks the two polynomials share a variable, strips it, defers to
`reduce_terms`, and reattaches the variable. Part (b) adds
`reduce_integers`, the texi's own snippet with a Euclidean `gcd_i128`,
and a generic `reduce` that dispatches by the shape of its arguments:
integer pairs go to `reduce_integers`, polynomial pairs to
`reduce_poly`, mismatched pairs raise. `make_rational_fn` calls
`reduce` before combining a numerator and denominator, the generalization
of the original `make_rat`, and `add_rational_fns` forms
`(n1 d2 + n2 d1) / (d1 d2)` through it.

The test drives `reduce(6, 8)` to `(3, 4)` through the integer branch
and then the book's example, `(x+1)/(x^3-1) + x/(x^2-1)`, which comes
out as `(x^3 + 2x^2 + 3x + 1) / (x^4 + x^3 - x - 1)`, reduced to lowest
terms: both inputs are already coprime, and the sum's cross product
shares the factor `x + 1`, which `reduce_terms` divides out of numerator
and denominator alike.

## Map class

`A`: reduce-terms plus package-wide generic rewire. The reduction
algorithm is the solution's core; the "system-wide install" half
of the class shows up as one `reduce` entry point both kinds of number
flow through, so a rational function of either integers or polynomials
reduces at construction.

## Deviation

The book dispatches `reduce` at run time through `apply_generic` and a
shared table. This solution's two number kinds are a closed pair, so a
two-arm `match` over an enum plays the dispatcher, exhaustively checked
at compile time; the exercise's dispatch is two-way and fixed, and the
runtime-table material is the section's own earlier listings.

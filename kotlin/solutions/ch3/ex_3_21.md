## Exercise 3.21: reading a queue's host-data view

Approach: the queue's `items()` returns its front-to-rear contents as a native
`List<Datum>`. `benView()` pairs that list with the datum stored in the rear
cell, or `null` when the queue is empty. This keeps the two observations from
the exercise while using typed Kotlin data instead of a textual data printer.

Why the rear datum appears in both parts of the view: the queue is one chain
of mutable `PairCell` objects. The front cell starts the chain, and the rear
cell is its last node. In a queue containing `a`, `b`, `c`, and `d`, the
ordered items end with `d`, and the rear cell stores `d` too. It is one node,
not a second copy: the tests check that walking to the final link from the
front reaches the exact object returned by `rearCell()`.

Why the emptied queue has no stale rear: deleting the last item clears both
end pointers. The host-data view then contains an empty item list and a null
rear datum. A later insert starts a new linked chain.

What the tests say: they check the queue contents after each operation, the
rear datum, the identity link from the front chain to the rear cell, the
independent state of an emptied queue, and reuse after emptying.

Class: `T` (the pointer observation is retained as typed host data).

Deviation: the original printer's textual form is not reproduced. The
exercise still distinguishes the item sequence from the rear pointer and
preserves the identity-based evidence that explains the apparent duplicate.

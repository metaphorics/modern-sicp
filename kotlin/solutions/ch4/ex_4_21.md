# Exercise 4.21: recursion without define

## Approach

The trick translates as pure object text run on the base evaluator, which
carries no recursion machinery at all: each procedure takes itself as an
ordinary argument and performs the recursive step by calling that
argument. Part (a) is the statement's factorial rebuilt as the fib analog:
an outer lambda takes `n`, an inner binding hands `(lambda (fb k))` to
itself as `fib`, and the step calls `(fb fb (- k 1))` and `(fb fb
(- k 2))`. Part (b) fills the statement's blanks for the mutual
even?/odd? pair: each procedure receives both procedures plus the count,
so `even?`'s step is `(od? ev? od? (- k 1))` and `odd?`'s step is
`(ev? ev? od? (- k 1))`; a wrapper `f` takes the number and launches the
pair. No Y combinator is needed for these shapes: self-application hands
each procedure enough of itself to continue, which is the subtlety Louis's
intuition in 4.20 missed.

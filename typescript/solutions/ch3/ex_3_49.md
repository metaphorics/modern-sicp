# Exercise 3.49: when lock ordering cannot help

## Approach

The scenario has two shared resources and two processes whose resource
sets are not knowable when acquisition starts. A transfer clerk must
take the ledger lock first, because the transfer record naming the
account is only readable under it, and must keep holding the ledger
while it takes the account (the record is claimed by the transfer in
progress). A balance auditor is assigned its account in advance, takes
the account lock, and only then discovers that filing the audit record
requires the ledger. Both measured runs use real fibers with a real
200 millisecond limit, so the deadlock is an observed
non-termination.

## Pins

Computed: the clerk and auditor deadlock, the clerk holding the ledger
while waiting on the account and the auditor holding the account while
waiting on the ledger. Numbering cannot fix this: giving the ledger a
number in the same order as accounts would let the auditor acquire in
ascending order only if the auditor knew in advance that the ledger
was needed, and the scenario's premise is that it does not; the
clerk's first resource is the ledger, the auditor's first is the
account, and each discovers its second need only under its first lock,
so no total order exists that both can follow from their first
acquisition. The two-phase auditor, which releases the account before
taking the ledger, completes: that backing off and retrying is the
deadlock-recovery posture the text's footnote points at for the cases
where avoidance is impossible.

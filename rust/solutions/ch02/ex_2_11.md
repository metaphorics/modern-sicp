# Exercise 2.11: `mul-interval` by nine sign-tested cases

## Approach

`sign(lower, upper)` classifies an interval's bounds as `NonNeg`,
`NonPos`, or `Mixed`. Matching on the pair of signs for `x` and `y`
picks the two endpoint products that bound the result in eight of the
nine cases, and all four in the ninth (both intervals spanning zero),
matching Ben's own count: "only one of which requires more than two
multiplications." Each of the eight two-product cases is derived by
treating a `NonPos` interval as the negation of a `NonNeg` one and
tracking the sign flips algebraically, rather than guessed from the
endpoint labels; a mutation-style property test
(`mul_interval_fast_matches_naive`) checks the derivation empirically by
comparing every result against the naive four-product `mul_interval`
across randomly generated, validly ordered interval pairs.

# Exercise 2.91: polynomial division

## Approach

`div_terms` is the book's long-division recipe on term lists. Divide the leading term of the dividend by the leading term of the divisor to get the next quotient term, multiply that term by the whole divisor, subtract the product from the dividend, and recurse on what remains; an empty dividend returns the empty pair, and a dividend whose leading order has dropped below the divisor's returns the empty quotient with the dividend as remainder. The supporting operations exist for division's sake: `sub_terms` is addition with a negated list, and `mul_term_by_all_terms` scales a list by one term, so division leans on exactly the sub and mul the map row names.

Coefficients are plain integers and the loop only proceeds while division stays exact: an empty divisor or a zero leading coefficient raises `SchemeError::DivisionByZero`, and a leading coefficient that does not divide evenly raises `SchemeError::TypeMismatch`, the book's observation that with integer coefficients you simply cannot divide evenly all the time.

The probe runs the book's own worked example: `x^5 - 1` divided by `x^2 - 1`. The quotient reads back `[(3, 1), (1, 1)]`, which is `x^3 + x`, and the remainder `[(1, 1), (0, -1)]`, which is `x - 1`, matching the division the exercise walks through by hand.

## Map class

`A`: division needs generic sub mul on terms. The solution embodies the row structurally: `div_terms` cannot be written without `sub_terms` and `mul_term_by_all_terms` beside it, and both are expressed through the same `adjoin`/`add_terms` machinery addition uses.

## Deviation

The division runs at term-list level over integer coefficients and is not installed under the polynomial package's operation table, so `sub` and `mul` on terms here are local integer arithmetic rather than the generic table operations; exactness is required, matching the worked example the exercise supplies.

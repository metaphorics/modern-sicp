# Exercise 3.66: the order of the pairs stream

## Approach

The statement asks for general comments about the order in which
(pairs integers integers) emits its pairs, with (1, 100), (99, 100)
and (100, 100) as examples. The module's `pairs` starts with the head
pair and interleaves the whole first row against the recursive pairs
of the cdrs. The answer is read off executed walks: `positionOfPair`
scans the memoized stream for a target pair up to a bounded limit, and
the tests pin the full small prefix, the diagonal positions for i = 1
through 10, the whole row-1 spread for j = 2 through 100, and the
first steps of rows 2, 3 and 4.

## Pins

Computed by running. First 12 pairs: (1, 1), (1, 2), (2, 2), (1, 3),
(2, 3), (1, 4), (3, 3), (1, 5), (2, 4), (1, 6), (3, 4), (1, 7).

Diagonal: (1, 1) at position 0, (2, 2) at 2, (3, 3) at 6, (4, 4) at 14,
(5, 5) at 30, (6, 6) at 62, (7, 7) at 126, (8, 8) at 254, (9, 9) at
510, (10, 10) at 1022. That is position 2^i - 2 for (i, i), verified
by execution through i = 10.

Row 1: (1, 2) at 1, (1, 3) at 3, (1, 4) at 5, (1, 10) at 17, (1, 100)
at 197. The measured law is position 2j - 3, checked for every j from
2 to 100, so (1, 100) has 197 pairs before it: the statement's "about
198".

Deeper rows double their spacing as they clear the diagonal: row 2
runs at (2, 3) = 4, (2, 4) = 8, (2, 5) = 12 (step 4); row 3 at
(3, 4) = 10, (3, 5) = 18 (step 8); (4, 5) = 22 (consistent with a step
of 16). The pattern the measured prefix exhibits: row i's elements
interleave against everything before it, so its j-th column lands at
about 2^i steps per column once past the diagonal, and the diagonal
itself doubles: 2^i - 2.

Extrapolating the verified doubling to the statement's examples:
(99, 100) sits at the head of row 99's run, just past the diagonal
(99, 99) at 2^99 - 2, so about 2^99 pairs precede it; (100, 100) is a
diagonal pair, preceded by about 2^100 pairs. Both are extrapolations
of the measured 2^i law rather than walked positions; no walk can
reach them.

The stream also never emits a pair with its first coordinate greater
than its second: (2, 1) is absent from the first 5000 pairs.

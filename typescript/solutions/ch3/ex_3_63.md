# Exercise 3.63: why sqrt-stream localizes its guesses

## Approach

The statement quotes Louis's external version: `sqrt-stream` maps
`sqrt-improve` over a fresh recursive call instead of over a local
`guesses` binding. To answer by execution, the solution rebuilds both
shapes parameterized by the Newton step, so the step can count its own
calls: `sqrtStreamLocal` (the module's `sqrtStream`, local binding over
the section's memoizing `consStream`), `sqrtStreamExternal` (Louis's),
and the same two shapes with plain-lambda tails (`consUnmemoized` and
`streamMapUnmemoized`), which is the statement's second question: the
delay of 3.5.1 without the `memo-proc` optimization. `improveCallsFor`
builds a fresh stream, walks it once with `stream-ref`, and reports the
counter; `improveCallsForSameStream` walks one stream to 5 and then to
8 and reports the counter after each walk. All probes run at x = 2.

## Pins

Computed by running. Every variant converges to the same double:
the guess at index 5 and at index 8 is 1.414213562373095.

Memoized local: reaching index 5 costs 5 Newton steps; reaching
index 8 costs 8. Each guess is computed exactly once, from the one
before, and a shared stream extends from 5 to 8 with only 3 new steps
(counter 5 then 8).

Memoized external: reaching index 5 costs 15 steps, index 8 costs 36,
the triangular numbers 1+2+...+5 and 1+2+...+8. The k-th guess is
recomputed k times, once per fresh copy the walk builds, so the work
grows as n(n+1)/2 against the local version's n: Alyssa's "redundant
computation", measured. Extending the same external stream from 5 to 8
still costs 21 more steps (counter 15 then 36), because the copies the
extension builds share nothing with the earlier ones.

Plain-lambda delay, local shape: index 5 costs 15 steps and index 8
costs 36, and extending the same stream from 5 to 8 recomputes the
whole prefix (counter 15 then 51, the full 1+2+...+8 again). The
memo-proc optimization is exactly what the local binding was buying.

Plain-lambda delay, both versions compared: at every size measured
(n = 5, 8, 10, 12, 14, 16) the two versions perform identical counts
(15 and 36 at n = 5 and 8; 55, 78, 105, 136 later), because without
memoization each tail force rebuilds the prefix in either shape. So
the measured answer to the second question is no: the efficiency
difference between the two versions exists only under `memo-proc`;
without it both degrade to the same per-walk recompute of the whole
prefix, and the local shape loses its advantage entirely.

## Deviation

The commonly given answer to the first question calls Louis's
version "exponential". In this edition every tail is memoized by `consStream`,
so the measured growth of the external version is triangular,
n(n+1)/2: each element is recomputed from scratch per reference, not
per reference chain. The pins report the measured triangular counts.
The second question's classic answer, that Louis is still worse, is
also corrected by measurement at the counted sizes: the improve-step
counts coincide; what still differs is only the structure (Louis's
walk allocates a fresh stream per level). The statement's own framing
("would they still differ in efficiency") is answered with the
executed numbers, not the folklore.

# Exercise 3.35: the squarer as a real constraint

## Approach

The book asks to fill in Ben Bitdiddle's outline and make the
squarer a genuine new primitive. The Rust solution completes all
four parts the outline names: the missing alternative of
`process_new_value`, which computes the missing side of `a * a = b`
in each direction; `process_forget_value`, which forgets both
sides and re-informs; the `me` dispatch; and the two `connect`
calls. It follows the shape the section uses for `adder`,
`multiplier`, and `constant`: a `me` object implementing the
section's `Constraint` trait, registered on both connectors. The
solution sets `b` to 49 and reads `a` as 7, forgets, sets `a` to 6
and reads `b` as 36, and refuses a negative square root. The tests
hold each direction and the refusal.

## Map class

`T`. The outline, its four blanks, and the square relationship are
the statement's own.

## Deviation

None. The constraint follows the same registration and dispatch
pattern as the text's primitives, as the exercise requires.

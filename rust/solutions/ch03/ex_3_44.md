# Exercise 3.44: transfer needs no joint lock

## Approach

Louis's worry is answered by construction: transfer takes the amount
with the request, so the procedure never reads two balances at once.
The solution serializes the withdrawal on the giver's serializer and
the deposit on the receiver's, runs the deposit only when the
withdrawal succeeded, and drives three directions of $1 transfers --
a to b, b to c, c to a -- from three real threads, 100 rounds each.
The total stays $60 no matter how the threads interleave, and a
separate test shows an overdrawn transfer is refused whole (no
withdrawal, no deposit, nothing moved). The essential difference from
exchange: exchange must read both balances to compute its difference,
which is what forces the joint lock; transfer reads neither.

## Map class

`T`. The transfer procedure, Ben's claim, and Louis's objection are
the statement's own.

## Deviation

None. The no-joint-lock conclusion is the standard answer, and the
refusal path is tested because the conservation argument depends on
it.

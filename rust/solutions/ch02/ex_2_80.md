# Exercise 2.80: generic is_zero

## Approach

`install_zero` puts `is_zero` under four one-tag keys. Ordinary
numbers test the variant: `0` for `Value::Int`, `0.0` for
`Value::Real`, with anything else raising `SicpError::TypeMismatch`.
Rationals test the numerator alone, so `0/5` is zero whatever the
denominator holds; reals compare their tagged contents against `0.0`;
complex numbers test both parts through the 2.4.2 dispatch selectors.

The probes walk the four packages and answer
`[true, false, true, false, true, true, true, false]`: the integers
`0` and `2`, the rationals `0/5` and `3/5`, the real `0.0`, the
rectangular complex built from `(0.0, 0.0)`, a complex built through
`make_complex_from_mag_ang` from magnitude `0.0` and angle `1.0`, and
the complex `(1 + 0i)`.

The magnitude case is the informative one: the constructor stores
parts, and the predicate follows the parts, so zero magnitude answers
true regardless of the angle it was built from, while `(1 + 0i)` with
a nonzero real part answers false even though its imaginary part is
zero.

## Map class

The map's class for this row is a straight translation in this
edition's runtime types: `is_zero` over the four packages with
`Value::Bool` results, each handler phrased in its own package's
terms.

## Exercise 1.31: product, factorial, and the Wallis formula

Approach: `product` mirrors `sum` with multiplication and a null value
of 1; `factorial(n)` is `product(identity, 1, inc, n)`. The Wallis
factor at index `k` (1-based) is `(k+2)/(k+1)` when `k` is even and
`(k+1)/(k+2)` when `k` is odd, so `4 * product(wallisFactor, 1, inc,
1000)` approximates pi; at 1000 terms the value is `3.143160705532257`,
about `0.0016` off pi, matching the formula's famously slow
convergence. Part (b): `productIterative` marks its inner loop
`tailrec`, the same shape as exercise 1.30's `sumIterative`.

Class: T (translate). Deviation: none; the solution's test
property-checks that `product` and `productIterative` agree over
generated ranges, in addition to the book's own two printed values.

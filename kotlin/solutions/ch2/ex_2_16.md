## Exercise 2.16: why equivalent expressions differ, and whether it is fixable

Approach: `subInterval(a, a)` for `a = [9, 11]` gives `[-2, 2]`, not the
exact `[0, 0]` that `a - a` must be for any real number `a` could denote.
The reason generalizes exercises 2.14 and 2.15: `divInterval`,
`subInterval`, and `mulInterval` see only the bounds they are handed, not
where those bounds came from, so two syntactic occurrences of the same
uncertain quantity are computed as if they were two independent
quantities that merely happen to share a range. Every algebraic rewriting
of an expression changes how many times each uncertain variable appears
and in what combination, so equivalent expressions generally propagate
different (and always at best equally tight, never tighter than the true)
bounds.

Fixing this in general is provably out of reach for an interval-arithmetic
package built this way: doing so would require tracking, through
arbitrary arithmetic, which sub-results are functions of which original
variables, and computing the true range of an arbitrary multivariate
expression is NP-hard in general (this is a known result distinct from
this book; the book's own warning that the problem "is very difficult"
undersells it: it is impossible for a `Interval`-only representation,
though restricted techniques such as affine arithmetic recover exactness
for expressions built from addition and scalar multiplication alone,
which is why `par1` and `par2`, which also involve multiplication and
division of dependent terms, are still not fully repairable that way).

Class: T, translated as an executable demonstration of the general claim,
with the discussion above carrying the "explain, in general" and
"impossible" parts a code sample cannot represent. Deviation: none.

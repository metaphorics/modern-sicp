# Exercise 2.83: raise operation

## Approach

`install_raise` puts `raise` under three one-tag keys, one per tower
rung below the top. The `scheme-number` entry has two shapes in this
runtime: a `Value::Int` rises into a rational with denominator `1`,
built with `cons_cell`, and a `Value::Real` is already an inexact
real value, so its raise only puts on the `real` tag. Rationals rise
to the nearest real by `n as f64 / d as f64`, guarding a zero
denominator with `SchemeError::DivisionByZero`; reals rise into a
rectangular complex with zero imaginary part through
`rect_make_from_real_imag_tagged`.

`raise` is the one-argument generic, `apply_generic` over the value
alone. The probe lifts `Value::Int(5)` rung by rung and asserts the
chain: `(rational (5 . 1))`, then `(real 5)`, then
`(complex (rectangular (5 . 0)))`.

The top refuses to rise: no `raise` entry is installed for
`complex`, and the probe fails if a fourth raise ever succeeds,
guarding with the message "complex must have no raise". That absence
is the exercise's design, every type except complex raises, and the
walk up the tower is the whole test of the three designed procedures.

## Map class

`A`: raise needs tower enum with levels. The tower is the sequence of
tags the entries connect, `scheme-number`, `rational`, `real`,
`complex`, with the runtime's `Value` enum supplying the plain number
shapes at the bottom rung.

## Deviation

None.

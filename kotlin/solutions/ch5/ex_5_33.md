# Exercise 5.33: compiling the alternative factorial

The two recursive factorial definitions reverse the operands of
multiplication: one evaluates `factorial(n - 1) * n`, the other
`n * factorial(n - 1)`. Their answers agree because multiplication
commutes, but the pending operand across the self-call differs.
Kotlin guest binary expressions evaluate left-to-right in the shipped
compiler, unlike the book's right-to-left argument-list construction.

`factorialComparison` compiles both definitions with `Compiler.compile`
through `compiledStatements`, reports the instruction and save-pair
counts of each emitted sequence, runs both compiled programs at
input 5, and reads both compiled machines' monitored maximum depths.
The alternative must hold the first operand while evaluating the
recursive second operand, so it demands a deeper stack. The comparison
does not assume the book's exact instruction counts.

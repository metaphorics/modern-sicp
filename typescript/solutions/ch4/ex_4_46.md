# Exercise 4.46: left-to-right operands

## Approach

The operand order is the walk order: each operand's announcement lands
before its choice, so the display stream records which choice the
search resumed from. The parsing programs need the order: each
parse-word consumes the head of the input, so the noun phrase must
consume "the professor" before the verb phrase looks at the rest; a
right-to-left operand order would make the verb phrase read input the
noun phrase has not yet isolated, and every parse would fail.

## Numbers

The answers enumerate left-major: `[1, 1]`, `[1, 2]`, `[2, 1]`,
`[2, 2]`. The display stream reads `left, right, right`: the left
operand announces once, and the right operand announces once per
visit to its choice point — on the first answer and again when
resumption passes back through the left choice. Resuming the right
choice alone (second and fourth answers) re-announces nothing.
Nothing is replayed for a resume that stays after the done prefix.

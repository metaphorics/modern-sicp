# Exercise 4.74 — Alyssa's `simple-stream-flatmap` (translate)

Alyssa's missing expressions, filled in literally and realized in
`Eval.Streams` style: the flatten is `stream-map` of `stream-car` over
`stream-filter` of the non-empty element streams —

```scheme
(define (simple-stream-flatmap proc s)
  (simple-flatten (stream-map proc s)))
(define (simple-flatten stream)
  (stream-map stream-car
              (stream-filter (lambda (s) (not (stream-null? s))) stream)))
```

The solution builds two variant evaluators from the exported layers.
The flatmap under test drives exactly the three paths the exercise
names — `find_assertions`, `negate`, `lisp_value` — while the frame
stream's own combination (`simple_query`'s outer flatmap,
`apply_rules`, the `and`/`or` plumbing) keeps the book's flatmap
untouched on both sides, because those procs genuinely return
multi-frame streams and Alyssa's change does not apply to them. The
inner queries of `negate` go through the substrate's `qeval`, as in
the book. Tagged forms other than the four handled locally (e.g.
`always-true`) also fall back to the substrate.

4.74b's answer: the query system's behavior does not change for these
paths. The proc mapped over the frame stream in `find-assertions`,
`negate`, and `lisp-value` always produces either the empty stream or
a singleton — a match either exists or not — so interleaving and
first-survivor selection combine the same frames, and every frame kept
by the filter is the whole of its element stream. Measured in 4.74a
(see `ex_4_74a.md`): identical answer lists, identical frame counts.

# Exercise 4.30: forcing in eval-sequence (T)

## Approach

Two evaluators: the text's `eval-sequence` (non-final expressions evaluate
without forcing) and Cy's (non-final expressions force with `actual-value`).
Ben's `for-each` works under both because applying `proc` delays its argument
but `display` is a strict primitive, so the element prints either way. `(p1 1)`
answers `(1 2)` under both because the `set!` is a non-final expression the
evaluator evaluates either way. `(p2 1)` is where the readings part.

## Map class

`T`.

## Sample answer

`for-each`: trace `5732188`, done, count 3 under both. `(p1 1)` = `(1 2)` under
both. `(p2 1)`: 1 under the text's sequence (the `set!` never runs), `(1 2)`
under Cy's. The edition keeps the text's sequence: forcing non-final
expressions trades the sequence semantics of every ordinary body for Cy's
intuition, and part (a) shows the applications that matter force through
primitives anyway.

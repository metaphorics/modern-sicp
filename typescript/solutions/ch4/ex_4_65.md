# Exercise 4.65: the wheel's proof multiplicity (translate)

## Approach

The wheel rule is the book's: a wheel is anyone with a middle manager
beneath them who is themselves supervised.

```scheme
(rule (wheel ?person)
  (and (supervisor ?middle-manager ?person)
       (supervisor ?x ?middle-manager)))
```

Oliver supervises Ben, Scrooge, and DeWitt, and each of those (plus
Alyssa under Ben, reaching Oliver through Ben) yields a distinct proof
path, so one person legitimately appears several times.

## Numbers

`(wheel ?who)` yields five answers: Ben once, then Oliver four times.
The tests pin the full sequence exactly, matching the sibling order.
The book prints Oliver first because its `assert!` prepends each new
assertion, newest first; this engine's database is append-ordered, so
Ben's proof (the first supervisor row) surfaces first. The count is
what matters: five proofs, two distinct people.

# Exercise 2.32: subsets of a set

## Approach

The completed definition appends two halves: `rest`, the subsets of the
tail that omit the head, and the same `rest` with the head consed onto
every element, the subsets that include it. It works because every
subset of `(cons x s)` either omits `x`, in which case it is a subset of
`s`, or includes it, in which case removing `x` leaves a subset of `s`;
the two halves are disjoint and exhaust the cases, so appending the
cons-augmented copy onto `rest` doubles the subsets of `s` into the
subsets of `(cons x s)`. The base case returns the one subset of the
empty set - the empty set itself - which is why even `()` gets a `()`
in its subset list.

## Numbers

`subsets(list(1, 2, 3))` renders, in order,
`(() (3) (2) (2 3) (1) (1 3) (1 2) (1 2 3))` - the book's exact doubling
order - and has length 2^3 = 8.

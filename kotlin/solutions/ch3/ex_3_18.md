## Exercise 3.18: does the cdr chain loop?

Approach: `containsCycle` walks the cdr chain with a `while`, carrying
the same hand-rolled `seen` list as exercise 3.17: every visited pair
goes in, and before each step the next cursor is checked against it with
`===`. Revisiting a pair means the chain loops; falling onto `VNil`
means it terminates.

Why memory is the straightforward answer: the list's tail either ends or
does not, and the only way to tell by walking is to notice that you have
been somewhere before. Identity, not content, is the right comparison:
a cycle can pass through pairs whose printed forms repeat forever, and
two structurally equal cells at different positions are not the same
cell. That is the same requirement as exercise 3.17's, and it is why
this edition's statement again points at a `===`-scanned structure
rather than a standard-library set.

What the tests say: the ring from exercise 3.13 is reported `true`;
plain chains of length 3 and 8 are `false`, so the seen-list does not
produce false positives by length; and `z1 = cons(x, x)` is `false`:
the sharing runs through the car, which the successive-cdr walk never
enters. A detector that walks the car as well as the cdr fails that last
assertion is wrong-headed in the other direction: it would also report
`true` for structures the book's definition calls acyclic. A detector
that compares with `==` instead of `===` can be defeated by equal-looking
distinct cells and is exactly the plausible bug the identity scan
guards against.

Class: A (the host differs: no identity-keyed set in the standard
library, so the visited-pairs structure is a `===`-scanned list).

Deviation: none.

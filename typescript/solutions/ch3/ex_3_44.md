# Exercise 3.44: transfer needs no joint lock

## Approach

Ben's `transfer` is spelled directly over the section's serialized
accounts and measured: six transfers among three accounts of 100 each
run concurrently as real fibers, and the totals are read afterwards.
The unserialized contrast runs the identical workload on raw accounts.
Mechanism: real Effect fibers; the fiber scheduler interleaves the
operations at their three-step windows in FIFO order, so each total is
a measured run.

## Pins

Serialized accounts, computed: the total is 300 after the six
concurrent transfers; no joint lock is needed, because each transfer's
two operations are serialized by their own accounts, the amount is
fixed in advance, and every unit withdrawn is deposited exactly once.
Louis is wrong: nothing in `transfer` reads two balances to compute its
amount, so there is no window for the two accounts to drift the way
`exchange`'s difference lets them. Unserialized accounts, computed: the
same workload ends at 310, not 300: two withdrawals from one account
both read 100, and the stale write lands last, so 10 units the workload
spent reappear. (Interleavings can just as well eat a deposit; the
direction depends on the schedule, the breach does not.) That contrast
is exactly the essential difference from 3.43: exchange needs its two
reads atomic against both accounts, transfer needs only what each
account already provides.

## Map class

`T: transfer needs no joint lock`.

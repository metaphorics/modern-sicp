# Exercise 4.58: the big-shot rule (translate)

One rule, no new data: `(job ?person (?division . ?rest))` binds the
division structurally, and the `or` admits a person either with no
supervisor assertion at all or with a supervisor whose job does not
match `(?division . ?rest-2)`: the division symbols being compared, not
the job lists. Warbucks has no supervisor row in Microshaft, so the
bossless disjunct answers for him alone; the second disjunct then
answers Ben (boss Warbucks, administration, while Ben is in computer)
and Scrooge (boss Warbucks again, accounting), in job-assertion order.
Everyone else's boss sits in their own division: Hacker, Fect, Tweakit
and Reasoner under computer bosses, Cratchet under Scrooge, Aull under
Warbucks. The pinned order (Warbucks first, then Ben, then Scrooge)
is the interleave of a one-answer first disjunct against the second
disjunct's two answers.

```
(big-shot ?person ?division)
(big-shot (Warbucks Oliver) administration)
(big-shot (Bitdiddle Ben) computer)
(big-shot (Scrooge Eben) accounting)
```

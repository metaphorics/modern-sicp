# Exercise 3.82: Monte Carlo integration as a stream of estimates

## Approach

`estimateIntegral(p, x1, x2, y1, y2)` builds the experiment stream with
`mapSuccessivePairs` over `randomNumbers`: consecutive generator words
are mapped into the rectangle, x from the first of a pair and y from the
second, and `p` decides the hit. Each word enters the unit interval as
its top 53 bits over 2^53, an exact multiple of 1/2^53 in `[0, 1)`, then
scales into the target interval, so the mapping is uniform and exactly
reproducible. The hit stream feeds `monteCarloStream(experiments, 0, 0)`
and the estimates are the rectangle area times each running fraction,
via `streamMap`. For the unit box `[0, 1)^2` with the quarter-circle
test `x^2 + y^2 <= 1` the area factor is 1 and `pi` is four times any
estimate. Pins: the estimates after 100 and 1000 experiments are `0.77`
and `0.806` (77 and 806 hits, exact doubles), the first estimate is
`1.0`, and `4 * estimate(100000) = 3.137` sits `0.0046` from pi, well
inside the 0.05 bound. All were probed from the compiled solution and
re-derived bit-identically in a python3 script that regenerates the
xorshift64* words and replays the same arithmetic.

## Map class

`T` (map row 3.82: stream formulation of 3.5's integrator).

## Deviation

The function name overloads 3.5's `estimateIntegral` (which counts
trials and returns one double) with the stream signature the contract
pins; the arities differ, so both compile in the package. The unit
mapping truncates words to 53 bits instead of 3.5's full-word division,
trading a negligible bias for an exactly reproducible fraction. The
circle test uses the quarter disc in `[0, 1)^2` with `pi = 4 *
estimate` rather than the unit circle in a larger box.

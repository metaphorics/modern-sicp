# Exercise 4_77: delayed not and lisp-value filters

## Approach

A `QNot` or `QGuard` over unbound variables decides nothing: the
negated pattern matches on the open frame and drops it, and the guard
has no amount to compare. `QueryDriver.postponing` carries the filter on
the frame as a promise instead. A filter that meets a variable still
unbound waits; every time a pattern or a rule head extends the frame,
each waiting filter whose variables are now all bound runs at once --
as soon as possible, never earlier. The waiting filters travel out of a
rule body with the frame, so a filter that the caller's later conjunct
binds still works. A filter whose variables nothing binds (a variable
local to a `not`) runs when the query ends, on the final frame, as the
stock drivers run it. The stock drivers are unchanged.

## Answer

Expectations (`E4_77Test`): the stock driver drops every frame of the
filter-first conjunctions and of the rule whose whole body is the
filter; the postponing driver answers the six non-programmer
supervision pairs and the five salaries above 30000, the same lines the
bound orders produce, and answers the bound orders themselves
unchanged. A filter over a variable no conjunct binds answers as the
stock driver does.

## Deviation

None: the promise is a `Waiting` frame in the driver, threaded through
patterns, conjunctions, disjunctions, rule bodies, and `QUnique`.

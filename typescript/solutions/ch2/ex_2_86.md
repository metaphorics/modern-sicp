# Exercise 2.86: complex numbers over generic parts

## Approach

When the real and imaginary parts, magnitudes, and angles are themselves
tower datums, every primitive the rectangular and polar procedures use must
be generic: `add` for the rectangular arithmetic, `sin`, `cos`, and square
root for the polar conversions, all through the operation table. This file
builds the rectangular layer over generic parts and gives the trigonometry a
numeric abstraction whose operations are looked up, with exact square roots
over rationals where the arithmetic stays exact.

## Numbers

`add` of two complexes built from rational parts answers rational parts
`(rational 5 1)` and `(rational 2 1)` for `(1/1 + 2/1 i) + ... ` style
operands; the polar construction of a number with equal parts answers the
magnitude the exact arithmetic predicts; and a part that is not a tower
datum is refused at the constructor.

## Map class

A (generic coefficients need a numeric abstraction union): the operations
the trigonometry needs are looked up through one abstraction rather than
hard-coded arithmetic.

## Deviation

The exercise's layer is private to the file; the section system's complex
package keeps its number parts, which is the state 2.77 through 2.85 pinned.

# Exercise 2.92: multi-variable polynomials

## Approach

The solution implements the book's suggested canonical form: a global variable order in which earlier variables are more principal and stay on the outside. `rank` finds a variable's position in that order, raising `SchemeError::TypeMismatch` for a variable the order does not name, and `embed` converts any polynomial into the order-0 coefficient of a trivial polynomial in a chosen variable, which is what "convert a polynomial to one with the same principal variable" means when the whole lower polynomial becomes that coefficient.

`add_with_ordering` puts the pieces together. Same variable: the ordinary same-variable `add` runs. Different variables: the lower-ranked polynomial is embedded whole as the constant coefficient of one in the higher-ranked variable, and then addition proceeds as usual. The exercise only asks that the design work, and scopes itself to addition.

The probe adds `x^2` to `y + 1` under the order `["x", "y"]`. Since `y` ranks below `x`, the whole `y + 1` becomes the constant term of an `x`-polynomial, and the sum prints `(polynomial (x (2 1) (0 (polynomial (y (1 1) (0 1))))))`: `x` on the outside, the `y`-polynomial tucked into the coefficient slot, exactly the ordering-based canonical form the exercise asks for.

## Map class

`A`: variable ordering needs symbolic comparison keys. The `rank` lookup over an explicit variable order is that comparison key, and `embed` is how a lower-ranked polynomial moves under a higher-ranked one.

## Deviation

Only addition is implemented; the ordering is passed to `add_with_ordering` as an argument rather than stored as a global registry, and sub, mul, and div under the ordering are not installed.

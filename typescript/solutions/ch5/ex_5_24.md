# Exercise 5.24: cond as a basic controller form

The controller gains `ev-cond`, a loop over the clause list in `unev`. Each iteration takes the first clause and tests its predicate through `eval-dispatch`; a true predicate or an `else` selects the clause, and its actions are evaluated by `ev-sequence`, so the clause's last expression stays in tail position. The predicate evaluation re-enters the machine and clobbers `exp`, so the selected clause is re-derived from the restored `unev` at `ev-cond-actions`, where the else entry falls through without a test.

Two answers the derived form gives for free are handled explicitly: a selected clause with no actions returns the predicate's value already in `val`, and a walk off the clause list with no `else` assigns the `false` constant, exactly what `cond->if` would have produced.

The session answers `zero`, `one`, and `many` through an `else` chain; the bodyless `(cond ((= 1 1)))` answers `#t`; the cond with no true clause and no `else` answers `#f`; and `(cond ((= 1 2) 9) (else 10))` selects `10` through the else branch without evaluating a failing predicate a second time.

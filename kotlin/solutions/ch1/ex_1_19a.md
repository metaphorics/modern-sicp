## Exercise 1.19a: probing the Long overflow boundary

Approach: `fibIterLogChecked` restates `fibIterLog` over checked `Long`
arithmetic. The `(a, b)` pair the transform threads holds `Fib(count+1)`
and `Fib(count)` together at every step, not just the value asked for, so
computing `Fib(92)` needs the transform to produce `Fib(93)` as part of
its own bookkeeping even though only `Fib(92)` is ever returned.
`Fib(93) = 12200160415121876738` exceeds `Long.MAX_VALUE =
9223372036854775807`, so `ex_1_19a(92)` throws `ArithmeticException` on
that internal addition (one `n` earlier than `Fib(n)` itself would first
fail to fit). `Fib(91) = 4660046610375530309` is the last value the
checked transform can produce without touching that internal lookahead.

Class: this is the section's one chosen tailored addition (1.19a), picked
from the map's idea column. Deviation: none from the addition's own idea
("probe overflow boundary of native integers"); the concrete, verified
finding is that the boundary sits at `n = 92`, one step earlier than
`Fib(n)` alone would suggest, because the iterative transform always
carries the next Fibonacci number as bookkeeping alongside the one it
returns.

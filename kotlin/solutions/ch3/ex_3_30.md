## Exercise 3.30: the ripple-carry adder

Approach: four `fullAdder` stages, wired exactly as figure 3.27 does.
Stage 1 takes the external `cIn`; the carry out of each stage is a
local wire feeding the next stage; stage 4's carry out is `cOut`. The
construction function is the figure, read left to right: the internal
carries are named `c1`, `c2`, `c3` after the figure's `C_1`, `C_2`,
`C_3`, and each stage's carry-in wire appears in its `fullAdder` call,
so the wiring is visible rather than hidden inside a loop.

Why the width is fixed at four: the book's version takes three lists
of n wires, which in Kotlin would mean either a loop over a
`List<Wire>` chain (wiring stage k from the carry of stage k-1) or a
recursion down the list. This edition pins the signature at fourteen
named wires -- a1..a4, b1..b4, cIn, s1..s4, cOut -- so the exercise's
real content, the carry chaining, stays in the reader's face instead
of behind an index variable. The carry-in wire of each full adder is
caller-visible in the wiring calls, exactly as the brief for this
edition asks; no rewiring happens out of sight.

Why the complete output takes so long: a carry entering stage k
leaves stage k after one and-gate (the carry-and of the first
half-adder, when the stage's b bit and sum bit cooperate) or, on the
paths that go through both half-adders, after and-gates and or-gates
in series -- and the next stage cannot even begin until the wire it
listens to changes. That serial dependence is the ripple: each stage
costs a few gate delays, and an n-bit adder costs n times that, which
is why the exercise calls waiting for carries the circuit's major
drawback. The tests make the ripple observable instead of argued: a
probe on `cOut` shows the settled carry arriving tens of time units
after the inputs change, and flipping only `cIn` after the circuit has
settled shows s1 through s4 each settling strictly later than the
stage before it.

What the tests say: `E3_30Test.kt` adds 6 + 3 (no carry out), 15 + 1
(carry ripples all the way out), and 0 + 0 + 1 (the carry in counts),
each checked through the assembled sum bits and `cOut`; pins the
settled `cOut` line of the 15 + 1 case; and asserts the strictly
increasing settle times of the four sum wires under a `cIn` flip.

Deviation: the fixed width, recorded above; the wiring itself is the
book's figure.

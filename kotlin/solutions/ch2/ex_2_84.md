## Exercise 2.84: which type is higher, tested by raising

Approach: [raiseToward] answers "can `z` reach this level" by actually
raising it there, up to [TOWER_HEIGHT] steps, rather than consulting a
separate ranking table, so a new tower level needs only its own
`raise` install, never a central ordering to edit. [raiseToMeet] lifts
whichever of two arguments is lower; [applyGenericRaising] is the 2.84
revision of dispatch, raising on a two-argument miss and retrying once.
`ex_2_84` proves all three legs of Figure 2.25's tower: integer plus
rational lands in the rational package, integer plus real needed
[installRealPackage] (E2_86's `(real, real)` arithmetic, since raising
an integer toward `real` produces a `Real` that nothing could add to
another `Real` without it), and integer plus complex in the complex
package.

Class: A, per the map; "compatible" with the rest of the system, as
the exercise asks, means testing height by raising rather than adding a
lookup table that a new level would also have to update.

Deviation: `ex_2_84`'s real-number leg needed `installRealPackage` (from
exercise 2.86) added alongside `installRaise`, since raising an integer
toward a bare real still requires a `(real, real)` `add` handler to
combine it with the other real (found and fixed while proving the
exercise, not a change to the exercise's own statement).

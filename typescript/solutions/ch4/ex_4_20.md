# Exercise 4.20: letrec as a derived expression

## Approach

`letrec->let` rewrites `(letrec ((v1 e1) ...) body...)` into
`(let ((v1 (quote *unassigned*)) ...) (set! v1 e1) ... body...)`, the
book's let-and-set! shape. The dispatch is complete and carries both a
letrec clause and a let clause, so the derived let evaluates immediately
and letrec works nested inside lambda bodies. Louis's claim is tested
against the same program spelled with plain let: let's inits are evaluated
outside the new bindings, so an init that reads a sibling is unbound.

## Numbers

`(letrec ((even? ...) (odd? ...)) (even? 5))` with the book's mutually
recursive lambdas answers `#f`. `(letrec ((fact (lambda (n) ...))) (fact 10))`
answers `3628800`. `(define (f x) (letrec ((double (lambda (n) (* 2 n))))
(double x)))` then `(f 21)` answers `42`, the nested-body case. Louis's
`(let ((a 1) (b (+ a 1))) b)` fails with `UnboundVariable` on `a`, while
the letrec analog answers `2`. The emitted shape is pinned:
`(letrec ((a 1) (b (+ a 1))) (+ a b))` transforms to
`(let ((a (quote *unassigned*)) (b (quote *unassigned*))) (set! a 1) (set! b (+ a 1)) (+ a b))`.

## Class and deviations

Translated row. Deviation: the transformation spells the pre-binding
values as `(quote *unassigned*)` data rather than reader sugar, and the
dispatch is written out locally so the derived let is handled by the same
evaluator rather than escaping to the module's `evaluate`.

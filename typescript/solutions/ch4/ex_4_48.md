# Exercise 4.48: adjectives in the grammar

## Approach

`parseModifiers` is one `choose` with two lazy alternatives: the
noun word closing the modifiers, or an adjective consumed before
recursing. Because the alternatives are choice nodes rather than
eager calls, a failing noun parse on adjective-first input falls
through to the adjective branch instead of killing the search —
and every alternative consumes a word, so the recursion
terminates. Two sentences pin the shapes: one adjective, two
adjectives.

## Numbers

One parse each for the sleepy cat and the quick brown dog. The
modifier lists nest inside the simple noun phrase: `[adjective,
noun]` and `[adjective, [adjective, noun]]`. The tests pin both
rendered trees exactly.

## Class and deviations

Translated row. No deviation: the grammar is guest source over
the search experiment, and the choice laziness is the engine's
`choose`-node semantics, exercised rather than assumed.

## Exercise 2.63: tree-to-list comparison

Part a: yes, `treeToList1` and `treeToList2` produce the same result — the
sorted list of entries — for every valid binary search tree, since both
perform an in-order walk (left, entry, right); they only differ in how
the partial results are combined. For all three Figure 2.16 shapes of
`{1, 3, 5, 7, 9, 11}`, both procedures give `[1, 3, 5, 7, 9, 11]`,
regardless of which of the three (differently balanced) trees they
start from.

Part b: no, the two procedures do not share an order of growth on a
balanced tree of `n` elements. `treeToList1` combines with `+` on
`List`, which is `Θ(k)` in the size of its left operand; summed over
every node of a balanced tree, that totals `Θ(n log n)`, mirroring the
book's own claim about Scheme's `append`. `treeToList2` threads one
mutable `ArrayDeque` through the whole recursion and prepends with
`addFirst`, an O(1) amortized operation standing in for Scheme's O(1)
`cons` (an ordinary immutable Kotlin `List` has no such operation, so
this exercise is the one place the section reaches for a mutable
accumulator to keep the growth-order lesson genuine); the total cost is
`Θ(n)`. `treeToList2` grows more slowly.

Class: T, per the map — a direct translation, once `+` is recognized as
`append` and `ArrayDeque.addFirst` as `cons`.

Deviation: `treeToList2` uses a mutable `ArrayDeque` internally rather
than a persistent list, precisely to preserve the O(1)-prepend property
the exercise's growth-order argument depends on; the public function
still returns an ordinary immutable `List`.

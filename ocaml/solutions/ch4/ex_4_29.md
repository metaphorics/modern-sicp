# Exercise 4.29: memoization speed difference (translate)

The map class is `T`. The exhibit is the counting identity of 4.27 under
`cube`: `(cube (id 10))` forces `square`'s operand once per occurrence,
so with memoization `count` advances by 1 and without it by 3: the same
program recomputes the same delayed computation twice more, which is the
slowness the statement asks for, measured in forcing counts rather than
wall clock. The statement's interaction: `(square (id 10))` answers 100
either way, and `count` reads 1 with memoization against 2 without.

# Exercise 4.29a: memoization toggle and forcing counters (this edition's addition)

The tailored addition instruments the thunk machinery itself. The
evaluator is the section's lazy dispatch with one runtime toggle,
`memoized`, read when an operand is delayed, and three counters:
`creations` counts `delay_it` calls, `forcings` counts forces that hit a
thunk at the evaluator's forcing sites (primitive operands, `if`
predicates, the operator position, the driver), and `computations` counts
thunk bodies actually evaluated. For `(square (id 10))` the counts under
the two modes are:

- memoized: creations 2, forcings 2, computations 2: the operand thunk
  and the inner `10` thunk created once, the operand forced at both `*`
  sites, everything computed once;
- unmemoized: creations 3, forcings 2, computations 4. Re-forcing the
  operand re-runs `(id 10)`, which re-creates the inner thunk and
  recomputes it, twice.

The forcings are equal because forcing demand is a property of the
program text; the computations differ because memoization is what makes
a second forcing cheap. The counters live outside the recursive module,
whose initialization must stay allocation-free.

Sample: `ex_4_29 ()` answers
`["100"; "1"; "1000"; "2"; "100"; "2"; "1000"; "5"]` and `ex_4_29a ()`
answers `["100"; "creations=2 forcings=2 computations=2"; "100";
"creations=3 forcings=2 computations=4"]`.

# Exercise 2.72: order of growth of encode-symbol (translate)

The map class is `T`: only the syntax changes, but the order-of-growth
answer is worth deriving carefully rather than assumed, because it
depends on which side of `make_code_tree` accumulates the merged
subtree. `encode_symbol_steps` counts one step per element a
`symbols`-list membership test examines, plus one step per branch
taken, exactly the cost the exercise asks about.

For the skewed alphabet of Exercise 2.71, `successive_merge` always
merges with the accumulator as the *left* operand of
`make_code_tree` (it is always the lighter, older tree), so
`symbols left @ symbols right` always puts the accumulator's symbols
first. Two consequences follow, verified by running
`encode_symbol_steps` rather than assumed:

- Encoding the **heaviest** symbol (`sn`, one level down) costs
  scanning the *entire* left-branch symbol list once to rule it out
  (it is not there; `sn` is the lone leaf on the right), a single
  `Theta(n)` step, for `n + 2` steps overall: `Theta(n)`.
- Encoding the **lightest** symbol (`s1`, `n - 1` levels down) finds
  `s1` as the very *first* element of the left-branch symbol list at
  every level, because `s1` was the first symbol ever merged and sits
  at the front of every subsequent concatenation. Each of the `n - 1`
  levels costs one step to find it plus one step to move, for
  `2n - 1` steps overall: also `Theta(n)`.

Both therefore grow as `Theta(n)`, not the `Theta(n)`-versus-`Theta(n^2)`
split a coarser accounting (charging the full node size at every
level regardless of where the target sits in its list) would predict.
The asymmetry is in the mechanism, not the growth rate: one long
negative scan for the frequent symbol against `n - 1` short positive
ones for the rare symbol.

Samples: `ex_2_72 5` is `(7, 9)`; `ex_2_72 10` is `(12, 19)`, matching
`n + 2` and `2n - 1` exactly for both sizes.

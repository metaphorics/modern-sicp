## Exercise 1.9: substitution model, recursive versus iterative addition

Approach: `plusRecursive(a, b)` unwinds to a nest of nested `.inc()` calls
waiting on the base case, the same expansion-then-contraction shape the
text draws for `factorialRecursive`: substituting `plusRecursive(4, 5)`
traces `(((( plusRecursive(0, 5) ).inc() ).inc() ).inc() ).inc()`, which
collapses inward once `plusRecursive(0, 5) = 5` is reached. `plusIterative`
instead carries the whole state in its two arguments and returns the last
call's result directly: substituting `plusIterative(4, 5)` traces
`plusIterative(3, 6)`, `plusIterative(2, 7)`, `plusIterative(1, 8)`,
`plusIterative(0, 9)`, `9`, with nothing left waiting at any step.

Class: A (adapt). SICP's own answer calls both underlying processes
iterative once a tail-recursive Scheme implementation is assumed, even
though one is written as a recursive procedure: the interpreter's
blanket guarantee covers every tail call, not just ones a programmer
flags. Kotlin's `tailrec` makes no such blanket promise: it is a
compiler-verified, per-function opt-in that accepts only a self-call
already in tail position. `plusIterative` compiles as `tailrec`;
annotating `plusRecursive` with `tailrec` is a compile error, since its
self-call sits inside `.inc()`. That compiler-checked fact is this
edition's answer to "iterative or recursive": Kotlin can prove one
process constant-space and not the other, where Scheme's
implementation-level guarantee proves both from the outside.

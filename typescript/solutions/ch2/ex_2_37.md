# Exercise 2.37: matrix operations as sequence operations

## Approach

dot-product accumulates `+` over the entrywise products of the two
vectors. The book spells that `(map * v w)`; the edition's map is unary,
so a local `map2` walks the two vectors together, narrowing both on
`_tag`, and dot-product is accumulate over its output.
matrix-*-vector maps dot-product-with-v over the rows. transpose is
exactly the book's `accumulateN(cons, nil, mat)`, importing the generic
`accumulateN` from exercise 2.36 rather than redefining it.
matrix-*-matrix transposes `n` once into its columns, then maps each row
of `m` over the dot products with those columns.

## Numbers

`dotProduct(list(1, 2, 3), list(4, 5, 6))` is 4 + 10 + 18 = 32. For
`m = [[1, 2, 3, 4], [4, 5, 6, 6], [6, 7, 8, 9]]` and `v = [1, 2, 3, 4]`:
matrix-times-vector renders `"[30, 56, 80]"` (the book's example), transpose
renders `"[[1, 4, 6], [2, 5, 7], [3, 6, 8], [4, 6, 9]]"`, and m times m renders
`"[[27, 33, 39, 43], [60, 75, 90, 100], [82, 103, 124, 138]]"` - for instance the
corner entry 138 = 6*4 + 7*6 + 8*9.

## Map class

`T`: the book's four definitions translate directly; the binary map the
book writes as `(map * v w)` is supplied by a local `map2`.

## Deviation

None.

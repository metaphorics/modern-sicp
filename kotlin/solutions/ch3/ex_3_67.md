# Exercise 3.67: all pairs via an extra interleave

## Approach

`pairsAll` keeps the diagonal pair and interleaves three tails per
level: the first row `(S_0, T_1), (S_0, T_2), ...`, its mirror column
`(S_1, T_0), (S_2, T_0), ...`, and the `pairsAll` of the tails. The
mirror is the hint's additional stream, and it is what lets every `(i,
j)` with `i > j` through. Verified behavior: both (2, 3) and (3, 2)
appear inside the first 20 elements; the diagonal `(i, i)` arrives at
0-based index `(4^i - 4) / 3` (i = 1..6 gives 0, 4, 20, 84, 340, 1364),
so all diagonals are present in a 1500-element prefix; and within the
first 60 elements the all-pairs stream already covers 24 pairs with
`i + j <= 8` against 16 for the book's `pairs`, the expected surplus
from dropping `i <= j`.

Independent check: a python3 simulation of the same construction
(cons/interleave law, two engines agreeing) pinned the first 20
elements, the diagonal law, and both counts (24 and 16).

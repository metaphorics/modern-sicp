# Exercise 4.32: the extra laziness

## Approach

Under the section's lazy pairs a cell delays its tail, so the armed second
slot is skipped as long as no walk demands it, and asking for the tail is
the moment the danger finally fires. The chapter-3 streams cannot do
this: a stream node computes its head eagerly and only delays the tail,
so the strict constructor -- an ordinary list literal -- dies at
construction. The self-referential `ones` pins the payoff from the other
side: under delayed construction the definition closes in one step and
the head answers without touching the tail; under the strict constructor
the same definition reads `ones` before the binding exists and is
rejected before anything runs.

The section's lazy-list data is built from the module's `thunk`/`force`
(a cell is `LazyCell(head, tail: Thunk<LazyData>)`), because the guest
list surface materializes a lazy list on access: a walk that must stay
demand-driven -- armed tails, infinite lists -- goes through the thunks
directly. The module's `lazyPair`/`lazyEnd` remain the named forms for
finite lists.

## Map class

`T`; the objective (which slots a lazy pair delays, and what that buys)
is unchanged.

## Deviation

The procedural-pair definitions of the text become the section's lazy
data; the strict self-reference is now an admission rejection rather than
an unbound-variable runtime error (the typed surface rejects it before
any effect).

## Answer

- `lazyPairSlotsTranscript() == "7\nError: DivisionByZero\n"`: the armed
  tail is skipped until demanded.
- `eagerConstructorTranscript() == "Error: DivisionByZero\n"`: the strict
  constructor forces its slot at construction.
- `onesOneStepTranscript() == "1\n"`: delayed construction closes the
  self-reference in one step.
- `strictOnesTranscript() == "Error: <rejection category>\n"`: the strict
  self-reference is rejected before any effect.

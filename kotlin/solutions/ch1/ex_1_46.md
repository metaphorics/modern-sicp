## Exercise 1.46: iterative improvement

Approach: `iterativeImprove(goodEnough, improve)` returns a `tailrec`
loop that keeps applying `improve` until `goodEnough` accepts the
current guess, then returns that guess. The convention is section
1.1.7's own `sqrtIter`, which returns `guess` (not one further
`improve`) once `good-enough?` holds; rewriting section 1.3.3's
`fixedPoint` through the same abstraction therefore returns its answer
one iteration "behind" the original hand-rolled version, since that one
tests `guess` against `f(guess)` and returns the *already-computed*
`f(guess)` on success. Both rewrites stay within their own tolerance
band: `sqrtViaIterativeImprove(9)` matches section 1.1.7's `sqrt(9.0)`
exactly (`3.00009155413138`, since squaring-based `goodEnough` needs no
transcendental call and both take the identical arithmetic path), while
`fixedPointViaIterativeImprove(cos, 1.0)` differs from section 1.3.3's
`fixedPoint(cos, 1.0)` in the sixth decimal place (`0.7390893414033927`
against `0.7390822985224024`), the honest cost of sharing one
`goodEnough`-tests-the-guess convention across two procedures whose
originals disagreed on what to return at the moment of convergence.

Class: T (translate). Deviation: none in the abstraction shape; the
convergence-return convention is documented above. The tailored idea
1.46a (return a lazy sequence of successive guesses) was not chosen as
this section's one addition.

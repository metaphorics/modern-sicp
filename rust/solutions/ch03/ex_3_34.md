# Exercise 3.34: Louis's broken squarer

## Approach

The book asks why Louis Reasoner's squarer fails. The flaw is
structural: `multiplier a a b` wires the same connector into both
factor terminals, and `process_new_value` only ever computes when
two of its three connectors are known, so with both factor terminals
naming one still-unset connector, the "one factor known" branch
never fires. The Rust solution wires the flawed squarer, sets `b`
alone to 25, and reads `a` back as nothing. A companion test shows
the failure is one-directional: setting `a` gives the multiplier two
known connectors at once, both terminals, and `b` comes out 25, so
the forward direction works while the reverse never can.

## Map class

`T`. The exercise's squarer, its flaw, and the explanation are the
statement's own, demonstrated over the translated `multiplier`.

## Deviation

None. The broken construction is deliberate: the exercise asks to
exhibit and explain the failure, and the tests hold both its
directions.

# Exercise 1.39: Lambert's tangent continued fraction

## Approach

The fold runs `i` from `k` down to 1 with the numerator `x` at the first term
and `x^2` after, over denominators `1, 3, 5, ...` - the same loop shape as
exercise 1.37 with subtraction at each level, per Lambert's formula.

## Numbers

For `x = 1`, five terms give `1.5574074074074076` (seven correct digits) and
ten terms give `1.557407724654902`, within `3e-16` of the host's
`Math.tan(1)` - four-decimal accuracy is reached at `k = 4`. The fraction
tracks the host across magnitudes and signs: `tanCf(2, 50)` matches
`Math.tan(2)` to eleven digits and `tanCf(-1, 20)` to twelve.

## Class and deviations

Translated row, per the map. Convergence is fastest near the origin; the
rationale records that the number of terms for a fixed accuracy grows with
`|x|` (Lambert's fraction is exact in the limit for every real `x`, but the
per-term gain shrinks as `x` leaves the origin). No other deviation.

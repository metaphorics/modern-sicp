# Exercise 4.78: the query language on the 4.3 amb evaluator (translate)

The matcher, unifier, index, and data base stay the section's
(`Sec_4_4`); the control strategy becomes nondeterministic search. A
simple query is one `choice` point over its matching assertions and,
after them, its rule applications (the stream system's order); `and`
chains through the frame continuation; `or` is `choice` over its
disjuncts; `not` and `lisp-value` are require-style filters that
perform `Fail`, where the `not` test runs its sub-search behind a
`Fail`-absorbing boundary so an exhausted inner search reports "no
match" instead of backtracking the outer search. Rule bodies evaluate
as nested searches inside their candidate's continuation, so
backtracking out of a body falls to the enclosing candidate loop with
no extra machinery. Much of 4.4.4 is subsumed, as the exercise
predicts. The driver is the 4.3 `drive`/`try_again` protocol: one
answer per interaction. No dispatch entry is replaced; the base
stream evaluator runs beside it for the comparisons.

The pinned behavioral difference: `or` answers come out depth-first
(all of the first disjunct, then the second) where the stream system
interleaves; and the recursive `married` rule yields its duplicate
answers one `try-again` at a time (the stream system produces the same
duplicates lazily).

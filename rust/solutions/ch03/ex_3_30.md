# Exercise 3.30: the ripple-carry adder

## Approach

The book asks for `ripple-carry-adder`, `n` full-adders whose
carries chain, and the question of the sum's delay. The Rust
solution chains `full_adder` stages through their carry wires,
starting from a caller-owned constant-zero carry-in and ending at
the carry-out wire the caller names. It builds a 4-bit adder, sets
A to 11 and B to 6, propagates, and the probes log the four sum
bits of 17 plus the overflow carry. A regression test then checks
the adder against integer addition on a spread of 8-bit operands,
including `255 + 1`, whose carry must travel the full width.

## Map class

`T`. The composition is the statement's own, built from the text's
`full-adder` unchanged.

## Deviation

The stage-zero carry-in is a caller-supplied wire rather than one
created inside the builder. This edition's gates hold their input
wires weakly so wires and gates cannot keep each other alive, and a
carry-in owned only by the builder would be collected once the
builder returns, leaving stage zero deaf to later signal changes;
the `255 + 1` regression test caught exactly that. The caller's
other wires are caller-owned already, so the builder's wire set now
matches them.

The delay half of the statement is carried by the section's delay
constants and the worked transcript rather than a separate formula:
the sum settles after a carry has rippled through every stage, one
full-adder delay per stage.

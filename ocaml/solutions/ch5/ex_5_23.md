# Exercise 5.23: derived expressions via transformer operations

The exercise's cheat is taken literally: `cond->if` and
`let->combination` are machine operations, and the dispatch grows two
tests, one per derived form, ahead of the application test. Each new
entry transforms `exp` and re-enters `eval-dispatch`, so the rest of
the controller never knows the forms existed.

`cond->if` builds the book's chain of `if`s: a bodyless clause's
consequent is its own test, and a missing `else` ends the chain in
`false`, which is why `(cond ((= 1 2)))` answers `#f`.
`let->combination` builds the lambda over the binding names and applies
it to the binding initializers in one expression.

`ex_5_23 ()` runs the sessions the answers are pinned from: the
three-clause classify (`zero`, `one`, `many`), the bodyless clause, and
the `let` whose body multiplies `a` by `b` and answers `6`.

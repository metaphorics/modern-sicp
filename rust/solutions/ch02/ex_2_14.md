# Exercise 2.14: repeated uncertain variables make algebraically equivalent formulas disagree

## Approach

`par1` and `par2` are the two formulas Lem's exercise statement gives for
parallel resistance, `(r1 * r2) / (r1 + r2)` and `1 / (1/r1 + 1/r2)`. The
solution computes both on two 5-percent-tolerance resistors (10 and 20
ohms) and reports their percentage tolerances (14.9 percent and 5.0
percent respectively: they disagree, even though the two formulas are
algebraically identical over the reals). It also computes `A / A` for a
single 5-percent interval, which comes out to a 9.975-percent-tolerance
interval centered near 1.0, not the exact point `[1, 1]` ordinary algebra
would give. Both demonstrate the same underlying cause: interval
arithmetic treats every textual occurrence of a variable as an
independent uncertain quantity, so a formula that repeats a variable
accumulates more uncertainty than an equivalent formula that does not.

# Exercise 4.10: one evaluator, two syntaxes

## Approach

The special forms move into a syntax table: a map from tag text to a handler
that receives the tagged expression, the environment, and the evaluator of the
running syntax. `evaluateIn` knows nothing about tags: self-evaluating and
variable rules are fixed, a tagged head with an installed handler dispatches
to it, any other combination is an application, and every nested evaluation,
sequence, and application goes through the same table, so procedure bodies
follow the syntax of the evaluator that runs them. Two tables are installed:
the standard seven forms, and the same handlers under tags spelled backwards
(fi, enifed, adbmal, nigeb, dnoc, etouq, !tes).

## Numbers

The square program answers 49 under both tables: `(define square (lambda (x)
(* x x)))` with `(square 7)` in the standard syntax, and `(enifed square
(adbmal (x) (* x x)))` with the same call in the backwards one. `(if (> 3 2)
1 0)` answers 1, and `(fi (> 3 2) 1 0)` also answers 1. Cross-table, a tag is
just a name: `(fi 1 2)` under the standard table fails with UnboundVariable
named `fi`, and `(if 1 2)` under the backwards table fails with
UnboundVariable named `if`.

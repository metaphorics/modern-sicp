# Exercise 5.37: disabling `preserving`

`preserving` normally saves a register only when the first instruction sequence modifies it and the following sequence needs its old value. That intersection is the reason some register saves are required and others are redundant. Turning the mechanism off emits a save/restore pair for every register in each requested preservation set, even when the later sequence does not need the old value.

`preservingComparison` compiles the same recursive factorial with the normal configuration and with `CompilerConfig(preservingOn = false)`. It reports statement and save/restore counts, then runs monitored compiled factorial sessions and checks that the answer remains `120`. The extra operations are compiler-generated bookkeeping, not a different factorial computation; repeated unnecessary saves add stack work during execution. The `continue` saves around sequence links are a visible example of the blind policy.

The counts and monitored statistics are obtained from the Kotlin compiler and machine through `compileCounts` and `runCompiledMonitored`, rather than inferred from the Scheme listing.

## Exercise 2.93a: reducing a rational function lazily (this edition's addition, extends 2.93)

Approach: [LazyRatF] holds the unreduced numerator and denominator
exactly as given -- no work happens at construction -- and exposes
`reduced: Pair<Num, Num>` `by lazy { reduceFn(num, den) }`, so the
reduction (exercise 2.97's `reduce`) runs at most once, on first read,
and is memoized by Kotlin's `lazy` delegate for every later reader.
[addLazy] adds two lazy rationals by the book's cross-multiplication
form, still without reducing either operand or the sum -- the reduction
stays deferred all the way through a chain of arithmetic, only paid for
if and when something actually inspects a result.
`ex_2_93a` proves the laziness directly with a reduction counter: zero
reductions before any access, one after the first `reduced` read, still
one after a second read of the same value.

Addition idea (from the map's row for exercise 2.93, "reduce rational
functions lazily," chosen by this edition; see `docs/exercise-map.md`):
exercise 2.97 makes every rational-function construction pay for a GCD
computation immediately, even when a caller only stacks several
arithmetic operations together and never actually inspects the
intermediate results. `LazyRatF` shows the alternative: defer the GCD
to first access, memoize it, and let a long chain of unreduced
arithmetic stay cheap until something actually needs the reduced
answer.

Deviation: none in substance.

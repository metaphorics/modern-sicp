## Exercise 1.24: timing the Fermat test

Approach: `fastPrime` restates the section's Fermat test loop; `random`
becomes the shared seeded generator of decision 0001 (`sicp.runtime.Random`),
so the test is deterministic instead of depending on an unspecified host
`random`. With 20 trials and a fixed seed of 42, `fastPrime` accepts all
12 primes exercise 1.22 found and rejects the ordinary composite 100.

Timing discussion (not asserted by the test): the Fermat test has
Theta(log n) growth against `smallestDivisor`'s Theta(sqrt n), so the
book expects the time to test a prime near 1,000,000 to be only a little
more than the time to test one near 1000, roughly proportional to the
ratio of their logarithms, not their square roots. Whether a given run's
wall-clock timings bear this out depends on JIT warmup and scheduler
noise that a unit test cannot hold still, so this edition records the
prediction here rather than asserting a timing ratio, the same choice
made for exercises 1.22 and 1.23.

Class: A (adapt). `random` becomes the shared seeded `Random`; `runtime`
becomes `System.nanoTime`, unused directly in the tested return value for
the reason above.

# Exercise 4.71: explicit delay in simple-query (translate)

## Approach

Louis wonders why `stream-append-delayed` and `interleave-delayed`
guard the rule applications instead of plain `stream-append` and
`interleave`. The solution measures the forcing directly on the
core's own combinators: an infinite tail of results is appended or
interleaved behind one result, a prefix is taken, and a counter
records how many tail cells were forced. The delayed versions force
only what the prefix consumes; the eager versions would construct
the whole delayed stream first and diverge.

## Numbers

Appending one result before an infinite tail and taking three forces
the tail twice (`[1, 2, 2]` with 2 forces). Interleaving and taking
four forces it three times (`[1, 2, 2, 2]` with 3 forces). The tests
pin both sequences including the force counts, which also pins the
core's `take` laziness: no tail is forced past the last element
taken.

## Class and deviations

Translated row. No deviation: measured on the shipped `Stream`
combinators.

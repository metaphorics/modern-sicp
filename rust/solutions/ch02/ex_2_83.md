# Exercise 2.83: raise operation

## Approach

`install_raise` puts `raise` under one one-tag key per rung below the
top: `integer`, `rational`, `real`. A `Value::Int` rises into a
rational with denominator `1`, built with `cons_cell`. An inexact
number needs no raise of its own at the bottom: a bare `Value::Real`
is already a real value, sitting at the `real` rung, where the real
entry takes it on to complex. Rationals rise to the nearest real by
`n as f64 / d as f64`, guarding a zero denominator with
`SicpError::DivisionByZero`; reals rise into a rectangular complex
with zero imaginary part through `rect_make_from_real_imag_tagged`.

`raise` is the one-argument generic, `apply_generic` over the value
alone. The probe lifts `Value::Int(5)` rung by rung and asserts the
chain: `(rational (5 . 1))`, then `(real 5)`, then
`(complex (rectangular (5 . 0)))`.

The top rung has no raise: no `raise` entry is installed for
`complex`, and the probe fails if a fourth raise ever succeeds,
guarding with the message "complex must have no raise". That absence
is the exercise's design, every type except complex raises, and the
walk up the tower is the whole test of the three designed procedures.

## Map class

`A`: raise needs tower enum with levels. The tower is the sequence of
tags the entries connect, `integer`, `rational`, `real`, `complex`,
with the runtime's `Value` enum supplying the plain number shapes:
`Value::Int` at the bottom rung, `Value::Real` at the `real` rung.

## Deviation

None.

# Exercise 2.90: sparse and dense term lists

## Approach

The redesign the exercise asks for is one pair of generic algorithms and two interchangeable representations. A `TermList` trait declares the book's four selectors, `the_empty_termlist`, `is_empty`, `first_term`, `rest_terms`, `adjoin_term`, plus `from_pairs` and `stored_pairs` for building from and reporting the stored `(order, coeff)` pairs. `Sparse`, the book's original list, drops any zero coefficient in `adjoin_term`; `Dense`, exercise 2.89's list, fills gaps with stored zeros. `add_terms` and `mul_terms` are each written once as a generic function over `T: TermList`, and neither ever inspects which representation `T` is, which is the whole point: the polynomial system no longer has to choose a representation to know its algorithms.

`canonical` puts both representations on one footing for comparison: coefficients at or below zero are dropped, and pairs are sorted by descending order, giving the canonical form answers are compared in.

The probe adds `(x^2 + 3x + 7)` and `(5x^2 + 3x)` twice, once as `Sparse::from_pairs` lists and once as `Dense::from_pairs` lists, and canonicalizes both results through `stored_pairs`. Both come out `[(2, 6), (1, 6), (0, 7)]`. Same algorithm, same answer, two representations: that is the exercise's demand met.

## Map class

`A`: two term-list packages behind traits. `Sparse` and `Dense` are the two packages, and the `TermList` trait is this edition's rendering of the shared interface both install under, with the generic `add_terms`/`mul_terms` taking the place of the book's representation-independent procedures.

## Deviation

None.

# Exercise 3.82: streaming Monte Carlo integration

## Approach

Exercise 3.5's `estimate-integral` loses its trial-count argument and
answers a stream instead. The pieces are the section's own: the
seeded word stream is consumed two words per point by the module's
`map-successive-pairs`, each word scaled into its rectangle axis as a
fraction of the whole word space (the book's `random-in-range`, with
the unit interval given by dividing by `u64::MAX`); the experiment
predicate maps the point stream to outcomes; `monte-carlo` of 3.5.5
turns outcomes into the running success fraction with no state but
the two counters carried forward; and one `scale-stream` by the
rectangle's area turns probability into area. The circle-in-square
experiment (unit circle centered at (1, 1) over the (0..2)^2 square,
area factor 4) estimates pi from the deterministic chain: the pinned
samples are exact rationals -- 81 of the first 100 pairs land in the
circle (4 * 81/100 = 3.24), 765 of 1000 (3.06), 7866 of 10000
(3.1464). The map's confidence idea is a local helper that walks the
estimate stream until one lands within a bound of pi and answers the
1-based trial count: the first within 0.1 is trial 21 (16 successes
of 21, an estimate of 4*16/21 = 3.0476, with the 20th at exactly 3.0
still outside), and the same walk at 0.01 answers 111 -- both
deterministic, both pinned. More confidence is literally a longer
walk, no new machinery.

## Map class

`T`. The statement translates whole: streaming estimates in place of
a trial-count argument is the statement's own demand. The Rust
wrinkles: the experiment predicate must be `Clone` (the mapper rides
into the stream's tail thunk), and the one u64-to-f64 rounding per
word is this edition's accepted spelling of a unit-interval draw,
marked with an `expect` at the single cast.

## Deviation

None. No assignment anywhere: the only state is the word stream, as
in the book.

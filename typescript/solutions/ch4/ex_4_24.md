# Exercise 4.24: benchmark analysis versus execution

## Approach

One recursive arithmetic workload, `(fib 14)` over `(define (fib n) (if (< n 2) n
(+ (fib (- n 1)) (fib (- n 2)))))`, runs under the module's direct
`evaluate` and under `evalAnalyzed`, each in its own global environment.
After two untimed warmups, seven calls are timed with `performance.now()`;
each evaluator reports the median of its seven runs, and the analysis
fraction is `(direct - analyzed) / direct`, the share of direct time
attributable to re-analyzing syntax. Total benchmark time is about two
seconds.

## Numbers

One observed run: direct runs `54.0, 46.3, 45.7, 46.1, 47.4, 50.1, 50.6`
ms, median `47.4` ms; analyzed runs `74.6, 70.2, 51.6, 45.8, 44.7, 44.3,
43.1` ms, median `45.8` ms; fraction `3%`. Two further pairs gave `+6%`
(48.6 vs 45.7 ms) and `-12%` (46.2 vs 51.5 ms). The honest reading: the
analysis share for this workload is small, at most around ten percent, and
the sign is not stable across runs because individual runs spread about as
wide as the medians' difference. Unlike the book's Lisp, where eval's
dispatch is a large share of each step, this edition's per-step Effect
machinery dominates both evaluators equally, so separating analysis from
execution buys little here.

## Class and deviations

Translated row. Deviations: the workload is fib rather than one of the
book's suggested procedures, chosen for a call-heavy profile; medians of
seven timed runs after two warmups; and the fraction is reported with its
noise, since on this host the difference between the evaluators is within
run-to-run variance.

# Exercise 5.20: the two drawings

The exercise fixes the starting state: the free pointer at `p1`. The
three conses of `(define x (cons 1 2))` and `(define y (list x x))`
then allocate in a forced order: a register machine cannot fill the
outer cons cell before it has computed the inner one, so
`(cons x (cons x '()))` allocates innermost first: cell `p1` gets
`(n1 n2)` and `x = p1`; cell `p2` gets `(p1 e0)`; cell `p3` gets
`(p1 p2)` and `y = p3`. The final value of `free` is `p4`.

`ex_5_20 ()` runs exactly those three allocations through the
allocation path (`free` started at `p1`) and answers with the
memory-vector drawing (the `the-cars`/`the-cdrs` table of Figure 5.14,
one column per cell), plus the two pointer answers. The drawing shows
the exercise's point: columns 2 and 3, the two elements of `y`, both
hold the pointer `p1`. The box-and-pointer drawing renders the same
fact as two arrows reaching one box; the memory-vector drawing renders
it as one pointer value written twice. Cell `p0` is drawn as `e0`:
nothing was ever allocated there, `free` started at `p1`.

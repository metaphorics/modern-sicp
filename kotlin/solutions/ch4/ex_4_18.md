# Exercise 4.18: the alternative scan-out strategy

## Approach

Strategy (b) reserves the names with the 4.16 marker but defers every
assignment until all initializers have run: the rewrite lowers the body to
one `let` of reservations whose body applies a lambda to all the
initializers at once -- operand evaluation is the book's "inner `let` binds
every initializer first", with generated parameter names (`init0`, ...)
no reader can produce. Because the reservations still hold `*unassigned*`
while any initializer runs, strategy (b) enforces a restriction the text
strategy leaves to the programmer: an initializer may not touch a fellow
define during initialization.

The probe is the 3.5.4 shape at host scale: `dy` defers its value behind a
zero-argument lambda, `(define dy (lambda () 3))`, and `y`'s initializer
forces it eagerly, `(define y (force dy))`. Under the text strategy the
assignments run in source order, so the forced read lands after `dy`'s
`set!` and the call answers 3. Under the alternative strategy `y`'s
initializer runs while `dy` still carries the marker, and the forced read
fails the typed premature-read check at initializer time.

## Map class

`T` (map row 4.18: Kotlin T).

## Deviation

The probe abstracts the 3.5.4 streams into a zero-argument lambda plus a
`force` defined in the object language, the shape the assignment asks for;
the reservation check and the scanner come from 4.16's evaluator.

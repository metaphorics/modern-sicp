# Exercise 4.47: Louis's parse_verb_phrase (translate)

Louis's version finds the section's parses, because `amb` evaluates only
the alternative the search tries. After those parses, the search keeps
trying the second alternative. That alternative's recursive call
consumes no word before it chooses again, so the search descends forever
and never reports exhaustion.

The section's experiment walks the whole search, so the demonstration
runs Louis's procedure under a recursion ceiling. With the ceiling the
search ends, and the parses are the section's. The failures grow with
the ceiling: 6 at depth 4 and 10 at depth 8 for "the cat eats", and 20
and 40 for the professor's sentence. Without a ceiling the search does
not end.

Interchanging the expressions in the `amb` makes the recursion the
first alternative. The search then descends to the ceiling before it
consumes a word, and the parses arrive in the opposite order, after the
deeper branches have failed. Without a ceiling no parse arrives.

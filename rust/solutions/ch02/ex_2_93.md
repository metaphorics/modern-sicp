# Exercise 2.93: generic rational functions

## Approach

The solution installs a rational-function package whose numerators and denominators are ordinary generic values. `make` under the `rational-function` tag builds a tagged pair of the two parts, and the package's `add` and `mul` entries, keyed on `(rational-function, rational-function)`, do exactly what the 2.1.1 rational package did, except every combination of numerators and denominators goes through the operation table's `add` and `mul` instead of `+` and `*`. No reduction to lowest terms happens here; that waits for 2.97.

The probe builds `p = x + 1` and `q = x^2 - 1` as polynomials, makes `p/q` with `make_rational_function`, and adds it to itself. The result prints `(rational-function ((polynomial (x (3 2) (2 2) (1 -2) (0 -2))) . (polynomial (x (4 1) (2 -2) (0 1)))))`, which reads `2(x + 1)(x^2 - 1)` over `(x^2 - 1)^2`, the doubled numerator over the squared denominator, unreduced. The polynomial arithmetic inside that answer ran through the generic table's `add` and `mul`, which is the exercise's answer in miniature: rational functions work because the parts are generic values the table already knows how to combine.

## Map class

`A`: rational of generic values needs trait bounds. In this edition the bound is the operation table's contract: the package never touches its parts except through `add`/`mul` lookups, so any coefficient type those operations support works as a numerator or denominator.

# Exercise 3.50: multi-stream map completion

## Approach

The book's general `stream-map` takes `(proc . argstreams)` and leaves
two holes: the emptiness test on the car of the argument list, and the
constructor. The general reading is over the whole list -- the map is
empty as soon as ANY input stream is `the-empty-stream`, and otherwise
one `proc` call combines all the heads (`apply proc` over the mapped
cars) while the tail is the general map over the tails. Rust has no
rest parameter, so the stream list becomes a slice `&[Stream<A>]` and
the procedure `Fn(&[A]) -> B`: the heads gather into one `Vec<A>` handed
to `f` as a slice, and the recursive call gathers the forced tails into
a fresh slice -- both head construction and tail gathering sit inside
`cons_stream`'s tail thunk, so nothing beyond the heads computes until
demanded, exactly the discipline the two-stream `stream_map2` follows.

## Map class

`A: variadic becomes stream list parameter` (the map's Rust reason).
Scheme's `(proc . argstreams)` has no Rust form, so the re-cut passes
the stream list as a slice and combines heads by indexing the head
slice instead of `apply` over a mapped list; the two holes the book
asks to fill stay two holes -- `any` emptiness over the slice, and
`cons_stream` of the combined head and the recursive tail.

## Deviation

None. The two-stream form `stream_map2` in the section module is the
special case, and the test pins that the completed general map
reproduces `add_streams` over a prefix.

# Exercise 3.45: double serialization deadlocks

## Approach

Louis's account is spelled literally: the one balance serializer both
protects the withdraw and deposit and travels with the object. The
demonstrations run real fibers under a real 100 millisecond limit, so a
deadlock is an observed non-termination, not an argument: the exchange
holds the serializer while the exchange's own (already serialized)
withdraw parks on it, a circular wait of one process on itself.

## Pins

Computed: Louis's own deposit on a 100 account answers 140 and his
protected withdraw answers 125, because those paths acquire the
serializer once. A serialized exchange of two Louis accounts times out
in both argument orders, and so does the module's explicit-serializer
deposit (serializer wrapped around the account's already serialized
deposit), because both acquire the same non-reentrant serializer twice
on one path: the fiber parks on a mutex it is itself holding. Plain
accounts, whose withdraw and deposit are raw, complete the identical
serialized exchange and swap 10 and 20 correctly. What is wrong with
Louis's reasoning: exporting the serializer is fine, but using it for
the messages and handing it to callers means every composed procedure
that also wraps its own use acquires twice; the text's design forced a
choice, and Louis's kept both, which only works for reentrant locks,
which the book's mutex is not.

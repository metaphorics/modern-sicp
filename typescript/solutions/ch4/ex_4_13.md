# Exercise 4.13: make-unbound!

## Approach

The statement leaves the specification open, so it is pinned first: `(make-unbound! x)`
removes the binding from exactly the first frame of the chain that has one,
and fails with UnboundVariable when no frame has it. That keeps unbinding
local: an inner binding can be dropped to expose the outer one, while outer
frames are never touched by an inner unbind. The form is a tagged expression
handled by a clause placed first in a complete dispatch, mirroring the
module's `evaluate`, so an unbind works inside procedure bodies where the
first frame is the call frame.

## Numbers

With global a = 1, the inner define shadows it: `((lambda () (define a 2)
a))` answers 2. Adding `(make-unbound! a)` before the read answers 1, and a
read of `a` afterwards confirms the outer binding survived untouched. With
`(define (retarget) (define a 2) (make-unbound! a) (set! a 50) a)` the call
answers 50 and the global `a` reads back 50: with the inner binding gone, the
set! landed in the outer frame. `(make-unbound! zz)` fails with
UnboundVariable named `zz`; after unbinding the global `a`, a lookup of `a`
fails and a second `(make-unbound! a)` fails again, this time for `a`.

## Class and deviations

Translated row. Deviation: the book says only to give make-unbound! a
reasonable meaning, so the first-frame-only specification and the
no-binding-is-an-error rule are this solution's stated choice, argued for in
the answer: silent success on a missing name would hide real mistakes.

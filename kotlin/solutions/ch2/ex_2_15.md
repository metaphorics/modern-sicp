## Exercise 2.15: par1 versus par2

Approach: on the resistors of section 2.1.4's own example (6.12 to 7.48
ohms and 4.465 to 4.935 ohms), `par1(r1, r2)` reports about 22.6 percent
tolerance while `par2(r1, r2)` reports about 7.05 percent, both computed
from the exact same two input intervals. `par1` uses each of `r1` and
`r2` twice (once in the product, once in the sum), so naive interval
arithmetic compounds their independence assumption twice over; `par2`
uses each variable only once. Eva Lu Ator is right, and exercise 2.14
already showed why: every repeated occurrence of an uncertain variable
adds spurious width that a correct computation, which knows the two
occurrences are the same quantity, would not have.

Class: T, a direct translation of a judgment question into an executable
comparison. Deviation: none.

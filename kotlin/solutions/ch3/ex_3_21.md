## Exercise 3.21: what the raw cells of a queue show, and what printQueue shows

Approach: `E3_21.kt` builds `benView`, the rendering the original Lisp
interpreter produced when it printed a queue object: one pair whose car
is the front pointer's chain and whose cdr position holds the rear
pointer's single item. It reads only the two cells the class exposes,
`frontCell()` and `rearCell()`, so it shows exactly what a printer
without queue knowledge would have seen. `printQueue`, already a member
of the section's `Queue`, walks the front chain instead and prints the
items.

Why the same queue renders two ways: the queue is one chain of mutable
pairs. The front cell is the first pair of the chain; the rear cell is
the last pair of that same chain, so for the queue a, b, c, d the rear
cell is `(d)`: its `car` is `d`, its `cdr` is empty. Ben's complaint
that "the last item is inserted twice" comes from reading the rear cell
as a second copy of the data; it is a pointer into the chain the front
cell already names. The original printer made this worse by printing
the pair of pointers with the ordinary list printer, which renders a
one-pair chain in tail position as its single item: hence `((a) a)` for
a queue of a alone, and `(() b)` for a half-deleted queue: the stale
rear pointer, never updated because `empty-queue?` looked only at the
front pointer, in the book's original.

What this edition changes and why Ben's fourth line differs: the
section's `delete` clears `rear` when the last item leaves, so the
emptied queue shows two null cells and `benView` renders `(() ())`
rather than the book's `(() b)`. The illusion Ben tripped over is gone
from the accessors, not explained away; the test that walks the four
stages of his session pins the clean version, and the tests for `((a)
a)`, `((a b) b)`, and `((b) b)` pin the illusion where it did exist.

What the tests say: `E3_21Test.kt` checks the three views against each
other; `printQueue` agrees with `frontCell`, `rearCell` is the last
cell of the front chain (`===` on the cells, not equal values), the
four book lines of Ben's session come out of `benView` byte for byte,
and after two deletes the queue is empty, reusable, and prints `()`.

Class: `T` (mechanical translation; the interpreter's raw printer
becomes the `benView` helper and the shipped `printQueue`).

Deviation: none.

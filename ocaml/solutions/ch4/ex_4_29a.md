# Exercise 4.29a — the memoization toggle and forcing counters (this edition's addition)

The map class is `T`. This edition adds 4.29a because 4.29's question —
what memoization is worth — is answered there by object-language side
effects, while the evaluator's own bookkeeping stays invisible. The
addition instruments the machinery: the evaluator is the section's lazy
dispatch with one runtime toggle, `memoized`, read when an operand is
delayed, and three counters — `creations` counts `delay_it` calls,
`forcings` counts forces that hit a thunk at the evaluator's forcing
sites (primitive operands, `if` predicates, the operator position, the
driver), and `computations` counts thunk bodies actually evaluated. The
counters live outside the recursive module, whose initialization must
stay allocation-free.

The design choice to record: the counts partition cleanly. Forcing
demand is a property of the program text, so `forcings` is equal under
the two modes; memoization is what makes a second forcing cheap, so
`computations` doubles without it; and without memoization a re-forced
operand re-creates the inner thunks of its body, so `creations` grows
too. For `(square (id 10))`: memoized `creations=2 forcings=2
computations=2`, unmemoized `creations=3 forcings=2 computations=4`.

Sample: `ex_4_29a ()` answers `["100"; "creations=2 forcings=2
computations=2"; "100"; "creations=3 forcings=2 computations=4"]` — the
answer is 100 under both modes, and the counters assert what the modes
did to produce it.

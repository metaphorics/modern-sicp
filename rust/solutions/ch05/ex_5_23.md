# Exercise 5.23: derived expressions via transformer operations (T)

## Approach

The exercise's cheat is taken literally: `cond->if` and
`let->combination` are machine operations, and the dispatch grows two
tests, one per derived form, ahead of the application test. The
splice point matters: `application?` is `pair?` in this edition, and
a `cond` is a pair, so the derived tests go ahead of it, where
`splice_controller` puts them. Each entry transforms `exp` and
re-enters `eval-dispatch`, so the rest of the controller never knows
the forms existed.

`cond->if` builds the book's chain of `if`s: a clause's predicate is
its first element, a bodyless clause's consequent is its own test,
and a missing `else` ends the chain in the `false` variable, which is
why `(cond ((= 1 2)))` answers `#f`. `let->combination` builds the
lambda over the binding names and applies it to the binding
initializers in one expression.

## Map class

`T`.

## Sample answer

`(classify 0)`, `(classify 1)`, and `(classify 7)` answer `zero`,
`one`, and `many` through the transformer; the bodyless
`(cond ((= 1 1)))` answers `#t` and `(cond ((= 1 2)))` answers `#f`;
the `let` whose body multiplies `a` by `b` answers `6`.

# Exercise 3.48: deadlock avoidance by lock ordering

## Approach

Accounts carry a unique number from a process-wide counter, and the
rewritten `serialized-exchange` puts the smaller number's serializer
outermost, so every process enters its serializers in the same
ascending order. Why that avoids deadlock is then measured: two
opposing exchanges (Peter a1 with a2, Paul a2 with a1) run as real
fibers with the scheduling window between the two acquisitions made an
explicit suspension, once in ascending order and once in the text's
each-uses-its-own-first order. Mechanism: real fibers under a real
200 millisecond limit, so a deadlock is an observed non-termination.

## Pins

Computed: the ordered race completes (the lower mutex can only be held
by one process at a time, and a wait cycle would need some process to
hold a higher number while wanting a lower one, which the protocol
never does); the unordered race deadlocks, each fiber holding one
mutex and waiting forever for the other, which is the text's
Peter-and-Paul stall drawn as a timeout. The ordered exchange swaps
balances in either argument order (30/40 become 50/20 when the higher
number is passed first, because ordering is about acquisition, not
about which account is the source), numbers ascend by construction,
and concurrent ordered exchanges keep the balances a permutation of
the start.

# Exercise 4.12: environment traversals

## Approach

The three environment procedures share one shape: walk the chain looking for
a name. The walk is factored into two abstract operations. `forEachBinding`
visits every binding of one frame; `walkEnvChain` visits frame after frame
until the per-frame probe answers with something, and answers with nothing
when the chain runs out. `lookup-variable-value` is then the probe "the
frame's own binding for this name, if any" over the walk, and
`set-variable-value!` is the probe "the first frame that has the name" whose
answer is the frame to write. `define-variable!` needs no traversal: it
always writes the first frame, so it stays a direct frame write, which is the
book's own answer for define.

## Numbers

On a three-frame chain, global a = 1, middle b = 2, inner c = 3: the
traversing lookup from the inner frame answers 3, 2, and 1, and `zz` fails
with UnboundVariable. Setting b = 20 from the inner frame is visible to the
module's own lookup from both the inner and the middle frame: the write went
into the middle frame. Defining d = 4 in the inner frame is visible from the
inner frame only; the middle frame still fails d with UnboundVariable, and
the global frame still answers 1 for a. `forEachBinding` over the inner frame
visits exactly `["c"]` before the define, pinning that it never leaves the
one frame.

## Class and deviations

Translated row. Deviation: the traversals run over the edition's Ref-held
HashMap frames rather than the book's list frames, since those are the frames
the evaluator uses here; the abstract shape of the two walks is the
book's.

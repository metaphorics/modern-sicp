# Exercise 2.49a: painter output as SVG strings

## Approach

The edition's painters are functions from a frame to the segments they
draw there, and `renderSvgFrame` turns those segments into an SVG
document: the `y` axis flips once (SVG grows downward), coordinates get
a fixed attribute order and a fixed two-decimal precision, and the
document carries no timestamp or randomness. The exercise pins the
`wave` and `rogers` primitives under the identity frame and pins `wave`
under one transformed frame (a parallelogram with origin `(0.1, 0)`,
edges `(0.8, 0)` and `(0.1, 0.6)`), each as an exact string.

The string-comparison rule: pins are `toBe` byte comparisons on the
whole document, not structural checks. The identity-frame `wave` pin is
also compared against the checked-in figure file
`book/figures/generated/chap2/wave.std.svg`, so the book's figure, the
pinned exercise answer, and a fresh render are provably the same bytes.
`rogers` is this edition's line-portrait stand-in for the book's
photograph; its pin freezes the stand-in's segment list too.

## Numbers

The rogers document carries 17 `<line>` elements and the two wave
documents 18 each. Coordinates land on two decimals exactly (the frame
map multiplies by 200; ties such as `8.125` round half to even, so
`8.125` renders `8.12`). Repeated renders are identical strings.

## Map class

Tailored addition on the 2.49 idea cell (`painter output as SVG
strings`, chosen: typescript): the book's painters draw to a screen;
this edition renders them to deterministic strings, and the exercise's
work is the pinning itself.

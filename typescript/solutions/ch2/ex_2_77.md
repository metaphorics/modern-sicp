# Exercise 2.77: Louis Reasoner's magnitude surprise

## Approach

The book's `apply-generic` strips exactly one type tag per dispatch, so
`magnitude` of `(complex rectangular 3 4)` has no `("magnitude" ("rectangular"
"rectangular"))` entry to find. The fix is the layered-dispatch spelling: the
complex package installs `real-part`, `imag-part`, `magnitude`, and `angle`
entries that strip the outer `complex` tag and re-dispatch on the inner
`rectangular` or `polar` tag, so both `magnitude` of the complex number and the
book's `magnitude` of the inner tagged representation answer through the same
table.

## Numbers

`magnitude` of the book's `(complex rectangular 3 4)` is 5, and the 2.77 trace
runs `applyGeneric("magnitude", ...)` twice: once at the `complex` level and
once at the `rectangular` level, each stripping one tag. `real-part` and
`imag-part` of `(complex rectangular 3 4)` are 3 and 4; the same selectors on a
polar number answer through the polar package's representation entries.

## Map class

A (nested tagged values need layered unions): the two tag levels become two
layers of the `Tagged` union, and the selectors dispatch one level at a time.

## Deviation

The generic selectors fetch their entries through the typed selector fetcher
rather than through `apply-generic`, because the selector answers a bare number
rather than a datum; the dispatch structure is the book's.

# Exercise 5.20: the box-and-pointer and memory-vector drawings (T)

## Approach

The edition's answer is a run, not a picture: the three conses go
through the allocation path of the real memory model, and the
"memory-vector representation" is the model's own `dump` rendering of
the working semispace, pinned cell for cell by a test. Allocation
opens at cell 1 so the drawing shows the book's blank column 0.

The order of the three conses is the machine's order: `(cons 1 2)`
allocates first, and then `(list x x)` allocates its innermost cell
before its outer one, because the outer cell's cdr half must name a
pair that already exists. Both elements of `y` are copies of the same
pointer `p1`, which is the sharing the box-and-pointer drawing draws
as two arrows into one box.

The box-and-pointer drawing of the result:

```text
x = (1 . 2)

        +----+----+
x ----> | n1 | n2 |
        +----+----+

y = (x x)

        +----+----+     +----+----+
y ----> | p1 |  *-+---> | p1 |  / |
        +----+----+     +----+----+
           ^               ^
           |               |
           +-------+-------+
             both cars name x's cell p1
```

## Map class

`T`.

## Sample answer

The final value of `free` is `p4`; `x` is the pointer `p1` and `y` is
the pointer `p3`. The pinned memory-vector drawing, as `dump` renders
it:

```text
index    0   1   2   3   4   5   6   7
the-cars e0  n1  p1  p1  e0  e0  e0  e0
the-cdrs e0  n2  e0  p2  e0  e0  e0  e0
```

Cell 0 is blank, cell 1 holds `(1 . 2)`, cells 2 and 3 hold the two
elements of `y`, and the free pointer sits at cell 4.

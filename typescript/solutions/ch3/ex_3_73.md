# Exercise 3.73: the RC circuit as a signal processor

## Approach

The figure 3.33 law v = v0 + (1/C) INT of i dt + R i is the differential
equation C dv/dt = i - v/R: the resistor and the capacitor share the
injected current, so the capacitor voltage integrates the surplus current
(i - v/R)/C. The solution spells the signal flow as the feedback loop the
diagram implies,

    voltage = integral(delayed(() => streamMap2((i, v) => (i - v/R)/C, current, voltage)), v0, dt)

using the module's `integralDelayed`, the section's own integral with a
delayed integrand. The integrand reads the `voltage` being defined, so the
whole integrand is delayed exactly as in 3.5.4; the module applies the
Euler rectangle dt * integrand at each demanded tail. `RC` takes R, C, dt
and answers the two-argument procedure; `RC1 = RC(5, 1, 0.5)` is the
statement's example.

## Pins

Computed by running. With a constant current of 1 and v0 = 0, RC1 answers
0, 0.5, 0.95, 1.355, 1.7195, 2.04755, 2.342795, 2.6085155 over the first
eight steps. The physical check against the closed form v(t) = R i
(1 - exp(-t/(R C))) = 5 (1 - exp(-t/5)) sampled at t = k * dt: the discrete
Euler steps overshoot the exact exponential, with the gap growing
monotonically to 0.09144201895704773 V at k = 7 (t = 3.5 s); the test
bounds every one of the eight samples within 0.15 V of the exact value.

The discharge check feeds ten current samples of 1 followed by zeros: the
voltage climbs strictly through the charging phase to its peak
3.2566077995000002 at index 10 (where the current stops), then decreases
strictly every step, reaching 0.39592748157676544 (below 0.5) by index 30,
the exponential decay the tailored discharge-curve idea asks to see,
asserted numerically.

## Map class

T (translate: syntax changes only). The signal-flow loop is a stream
self-reference through a closure in every column; Rust and OCaml restate
the same delayed integral their modules provide.

## Deviation

The figure's printed formula is the open-loop sum (the R path plus the
capacitor integral); the solution uses the equivalent closed-loop spelling
of the same circuit, which is what makes the integrand self-referential
and the delayed integral necessary. The integration is the book's Euler
rectangle, so sampled voltages deviate from the exact exponential; the
test states the measured bound rather than claiming equality.

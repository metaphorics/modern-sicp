## Exercise 3.13: make-cycle closes the chain onto itself

Approach: `makeCycle` is one mutation (`lastPair(x).setCdr(x)`) plus
the return of `x`, mirroring the book's two-line procedure. The box in
the `c` cell, which pointed at `VNil` before, now points back at the `a`
cell, so `z` is a ring: `z`, its second cell, and its third cell, with
the third cell's `cdr` equal to `z` itself.

Why `lastPair(z)` no longer answers: `lastPair`'s stopping condition is
a `cdr` that is `VNil`, and the ring has no such pair. The walk steps
`c` -> `a` -> `b` -> `c` -> ... forever; the Kotlin code recurses until
the stack dies (or, as `E3_13Test.kt` shows with a bounded walk, never
reaches `VNil` in any number of steps). The exercise's point is that
mutation can destroy the invariant a consumer relies on: `lastPair`
worked on every chain it had ever seen, and one `setCdr` silently broke
its precondition.

What the tests say: the first test pins the ring's exact shape; after
three cdr steps you are back at `z` (identity, `===`), and neither
earlier cell is `z`. The second test walks ten steps, asserts `VNil` is
never reached and the tenth step lands on `z`, which is the observable
form of "`lastPair(z)` would loop forever". A `makeCycle` that cycles to
the wrong cell (say, to `x.cdr`) fails the first test's `third.cdr ===
z` check.

Class: T (direct port; only the host-language names changed).

Deviation: none.

# Exercise 4.33: quote produces lazy lists

## Approach

With the procedural pairs installed, Ben's probe `(car '(a b c))` fails
because the reader's quote is an ordinary pair of data: `car` applies its
`z` to it, and data is not applicable -- the transcript names the pair,
which is the surprise the statement describes.

The fix lifts the quote: [WithLazyQuote] rewrites every quoted pair into
the expression `(lambda (m) (m <car> <cdr>))`, the same shape the
object-language `cons` builds, with nested pairs lifted recursively and
atoms left as self-evaluating data. A dotted tail stays data, so the
rewrite is total over what the reader can produce. Quoted lists then ARE
lazy lists: `(car '(a b c))` answers `a`, and the section's `list-ref`
runs on a quoted list to its fourth element.

## Map class

`T` unchanged; the pins match the siblings (not-a-procedure plain; `a`
and `d` lifted).

## Answer

From the actual runs (`E4_33Test` pins all three transcripts):

- Plain regime => `Error: not a procedure: (a b c)`.
- Lifted: `(car '(a b c))` => `a`; `(list-ref '(a b c d) 3)` => `d`.

## Exercise 1.27: Carmichael numbers exhaustively fool Fermat

Approach: `foolsFermat(n)` checks `expmod(a, n, n) == a` for every `a`
from 1 to `n - 1`, an exhaustive version of the single random trial
`fermatTest` runs. All six Carmichael numbers (561, 1105, 1729, 2465,
2821, 6601) pass for every `a`, confirming they are not merely unlucky
against a few random choices of `a` but genuinely satisfy Fermat's
congruence for the whole range the test could ever draw from; an
ordinary composite like 100 fails for some `a` well before reaching
`n - 1`.

Class: T (translate). Deviation: `foolsFermat` is exposed as its own
function (rather than folded into `ex_1_27`) since it is the reusable
"try your procedure" artifact the statement asks for, and the exhaustive
`a`-range check runs quickly enough for numbers of this size to stay a
same-process, deterministic test with no random sampling at all.

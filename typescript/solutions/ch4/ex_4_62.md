# Exercise 4.62: last-pair as rules (translate)

## Approach

The two rules implement the book's last-pair: the base rule matches a
one-element list, and the recursive rule strips the head with the
dotted conclusion `(last-pair (?u . ?v) ?y)` and recurses into the
tail. The three queries are the book's: `(last-pair (3) ?x)`,
`(last-pair (1 2 3) ?x)`, and the partially specified
`(last-pair (2 ?x) (3))`.

## Numbers

The queries answer `(last-pair (3) (3))`, `(last-pair (1 2 3) (3))`,
and `(last-pair (2 3) (3))`, each exactly once, matching the sibling
pins. The fully unbound query `(last-pair ?x (3))` instead recurses
through ever longer candidate lists, so the reverse direction does not
terminate; the summary states that caveat and the tests pin it.

reader-marker spine walker as 4.61, since the unifier must match dots
on the rule side as well as the query side.

# Exercise 4.15: the halting theorem is prose

## Lesson

No total decider exists; the proof lives here, not in code. A program
that asks its decider about itself and does the opposite defeats every
candidate, at every budget. The probe shows what a step budget can and
cannot do: a budgeted simulation answers `halts` for a program that
finishes inside the budget, and abstains with `unknown` past it -- for
the diagonal at every budget. The budget decides nothing; it only cuts
the simulation off.

## Map class

`A` (map row 4.1: data as programs; the halting answer is prose). The
probe demonstrates abstention, not decision.

## Deviation

The probe keeps its loops iterative so no engine recursion depth is
at stake; the diagonal story is in the verdicts, not in a
self-referential program.

## Answer

- The quick program halts inside the budget: `halts`.
- The diagonal abstains at budgets `200` and `300`.
- Pin: `haltingProbeTranscript() ==
  "halts\nunknown after 200 steps\nunknown after 300 steps\n"`.

# Exercise 2.5: pairs of nonnegative integers as `2^a * 3^b`

## Approach

`cons(a, b)` computes `2n ** a * 3n ** b`; `car` and `cdr` strip factors of
2 and 3 until the remainder is coprime to the prime, which recovers `a` and
`b` exactly because the two primes share no factor. The whole exercise runs
on this edition's `bigint`: the map's reason for the `A` class is that
`2^a 3^b` overflows `number`, whose 53 exact bits are gone at `a = 53, b = 1`
(55 significant bits) and laughably gone at `cons(100, 100)` - about 6.5e77,
needing 258 significant bits - where the round-trip still has to land exactly.

## Numbers

`cons(3n, 2n)` asserts `72n` and round-trips; `cons(53n, 1n)` asserts the
exact `2^53 * 3` and round-trips; `cons(100n, 100n)` round-trips to
`100n, 100n`.

## Map class

`A` (map: "`2^a 3^b` overflows number, needs bigint"): the idea survives and
the statement changes in exactly the recorded way - at the encoding, this
edition's numbers convention stops being cosmetic and becomes the only thing
that makes the round-trip exact.

## Deviation

None.

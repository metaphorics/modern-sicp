# Exercise 2.96: pseudodivision

## Approach

Before each division the dividend is multiplied by the integerizing factor
`c^(1 + O1 - O2)`, c the leading coefficient of the divisor, so the division
stays on exact integers; the remainder is the pseudoremainder, and gcd-terms
runs Euclid over pseudoremainders. Part (b) divides every coefficient of each
step's answer by the integer gcd of the coefficients (the content the
pseudodivisions accumulate), folded through the generic
greatest-common-divisor.

## Numbers

For the 2.95 products: the factor for Q1 over Q2 is `13^(1+4-3)` = 169; the
pseudoremainder is `(polynomial x (2 1458) (1 -2916) (0 1458))`; content
removal answers P1 = `(polynomial x (2 1) (1 -2) (0 1))`; and the hand check
divides both products by that answer exactly, Q1/P1 = P2 and Q2/P1 = P3 with
zero remainders.

## Map class

A (pseudoremainder scales coefficients generically): the factor is raised
through the generic exp and the products and remainders through the generic
term-list operations.

## Deviation

A dividend the divisor already outranks is its own remainder (the factor's
degree exponent would go negative), which is the same guard the section's
`div-terms` carries; 2.97's reduction relies on it for numerators of degree
below their denominators.

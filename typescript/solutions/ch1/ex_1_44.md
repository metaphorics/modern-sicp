# Exercise 1.44: smoothing and n-fold smoothing

## Approach

`smooth(f, dx)` averages the three panels `f(x - dx)`, `f(x)`,
`f(x + dx)`; `nFoldSmooth` folds that one-step transformation over `f`
itself `n` times in a loop, which is the book's
`((repeated smooth n) f)` with the repetition applied to the
transformation, not to the numbers. The loop makes `n = 0` return `f`
unchanged for free.

## Numbers

`smooth(square, 0.01)(2)` is `(1.99^2 + 4 + 2.01^2)/3 =
4.000066666666666`, and the second fold gives `4.000133333333333`: the
error grows linearly with the fold count, since each smoothing adds the
second central difference (here `2 * dx^2 = 0.0002` for the square) to
the average. An affine function is smoothed exactly to its center
value. `nFoldSmooth(sin, 2, 0.01)` at the maximum `pi/2` gives
`0.9999333349999796`, just under 1: smoothing pulls extrema toward the
neighborhood average, the book's reason smoothing helps when the
derivative is zero but the noise is not.

## Class and deviations

Translated row, per the map. The statement's `compose(repeated(smooth,
n), f)` becomes the fold because this edition spells repeated function
transformation as a loop; the values are unchanged. No other deviation.

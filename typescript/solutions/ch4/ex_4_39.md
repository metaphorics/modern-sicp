# Exercise 4.39: the order of the restrictions

## Approach

The order cannot change the answer set: every restriction is a
predicate on complete assignments, and in the book's procedure every
restriction follows every choice, so the search tree is the same shape
whichever `require` rejects a branch first and the same assignments
are rejected at the same depth. The demonstration runs the puzzle with
the requirements in the book's order and in a reordered sequence and
compares the answers.

## Numbers

Both orders answer `{ baker: 3, cooper: 2, fletcher: 4, miller: 5,
smith: 1 }`. The old measurement recorded identical failure counts
under both orders — 1835 to the first answer — and that measurement
needs the failure counter the published search entry does not expose,
so the identity of the answers is what the tests carry. Reordering the
requirements alone buys nothing here; exercise 4.40 shows where
interleaving the restrictions with the choices does pay.

## Class and deviations

Translated row. Deviation: the failure-count identity is recorded
evidence rather than a runnable assertion until the search entry
exposes a counter.

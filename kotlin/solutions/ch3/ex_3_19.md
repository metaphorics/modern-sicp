## Exercise 3.19: cycle detection in constant space

Approach: `containsCycleConstantSpace` is Floyd's tortoise-and-hare. Two
cursors start at `x`; each round advances the hare two cdr steps and the
tortoise one, and the two are compared with `===`. Inside a cycle the
gap closes by one each round, so the cursors must meet; on an acyclic
chain the hare reaches `VNil` and the function answers `false`. Nothing
is remembered, so the space is the two cursors -- a constant.

Why the meeting is guaranteed: once both cursors are inside a ring of
*k* pairs, each round shrinks their distance along the ring by one
(modulo *k*), so within *k* rounds the hare lands exactly on the
tortoise. The hare can never jump over the tortoise and keep going,
because steps are taken one cdr at a time and the comparison runs after
each full round. The `===` comparison is doing the work the seen-list
did in exercise 3.18, with two names instead of a whole traversal's
history -- the trade is running time (still bounded, but the cursors may
lap the ring) for space.

What the tests say: the 3.13 ring is detected; a single self-looping
cell (`cons` whose `cdr` is itself) is the smallest cycle and is
detected; chains of even length, odd length, and length one are all
`false`, pinning the loop's edge cases; and on a shared-through-the-car
structure the function agrees with exercise 3.18's remembered-pairs
detector, as it must, since the two answers the book cares about are the
same. A hare that takes one step per round never meets the tortoise on
the self-loop and fails; a missing `VNil` guard crashes on acyclic input.

Class: T (direct port; the clever idea is language-independent).

Deviation: none.

# Exercise 4.71: why simple-query and disjoin delay

## Approach

The delayed operators interleave; the book's no-delay variants
(both exported by the engine) append. Two real comparisons pin
the split. Over the Genesis sons, delayed `simpleQuery`
interleaves the wife-rule's two Lamech sons among the eight
asserted sons, while `simpleQueryNoDelay` lists all eight
assertions first. Over two supervisor branches, `disjoin`
interleaves Louis among Ben's supervisees, while `disjoinNoDelay`
appends Hacker's branch after Ben's. Seed streams are built as
stream literals — the driver owns the only other seed.

## Numbers

Ten son lines in both orders; the delayed order reads assertion,
rule, assertion, rule, then assertions only. Four supervisor
lines in both orders; Louis sits second when delayed, fourth when
appended. The starvation moral follows directly: behind an
unbounded assertion stream, appended rule answers never surface.

## Class and deviations

Translated row. No deviation: both operator pairs are engine
exports compared on real data. The divergence consequence itself
(no finite answer list behind an infinite assertion stream) is
explained rather than executed, since no infinite stream in the
test corpus may hang the suite.

# Exercise 2.54: recursive equality

## Approach

`equalQ` recurses over the `Datum` union exactly as the book defines
`equal?`: same symbol, same number, or lists whose heads and tails are
recursively equal. The list recursion is an inner helper over
`List<Datum>` because a union's list payload is the recursive spine.

## Answer

The book's two examples hold: two copies of `(this is a list)` are equal;
`(this (is a) list)` differs from `(this is a list)`. The footnote's
number question is settled affirmatively: `eqQ` compares numbers with
`===`, so numbers compare numerically. Empty lists are equal; a list is
never equal to a symbol or number.

## Map class

A (adapt): the same recursion over the edition's union instead of
Scheme's arbitrary-arity pairs.

## Deviation

None.

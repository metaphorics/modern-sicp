# Exercise 4.69: greats through grandson-ended relations

## Approach

The book's compound relationship becomes a `related` atom whose
first field is the relationship list, built on the 4.63 database
and rules. `ends-in-grandson` accepts the base list or `great` of
an accepted tail; the `related` step peels one `great`, checks the
tail, solves one generation lower, and climbs one parent link. Two
ground queries run: Irad as Adam's great-grandson, and Jabal/Jubal
as five-greats grandsons. The open-relationship query is exhibited
but unrun: with the relation unbound, the engine enumerates
ever-longer grandson-ended lists, so the driver would never return.

## Numbers

One great-grandson (Irad, through Enoch). Two five-greats
grandsons (Jabal and Jubal, climbing Enoch, Irad, Mehujael,
Methushael, Lamech). The open query's first candidate would be
`(great grandson)`, but the drain never completes.

## Class and deviations

Translated row. Deviations: compound relations are a first field,
not the atom head (relation names are strings in the typed
engine); `son` stays parent-first per the book's own facts, and
`grandson` keeps its (descendant, ancestor) field order from 4.63
throughout. The open query's infinitude is structural, so
exhibiting it unrun preserves the objective.

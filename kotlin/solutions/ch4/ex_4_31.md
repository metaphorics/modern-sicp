# Exercise 4.31: lazy and memo parameter declarations

## Approach

Laziness as an upward-compatible extension: the lazy experiment's
declarations are exactly the per-parameter discipline the exercise asks
for. `@Strict` parameters (and every primitive argument) evaluate at the
call; `@Delayed` or unannotated parameters arrive as memoized transparent
thunks; the book's third mode -- `lazy`, a re-run-at-every-demand
call-by-name parameter -- is expressed with a function-value parameter
the body invokes at each demand, because the module's thunks compute at
most once by contract. A procedure with no annotations keeps the delayed
default.

The counting session pins all three disciplines at once: `a` and `c`
strict, `b` call-by-name, `d` call-by-need, the result list
`[1, 5, 5, 4, 30, 30]` and the counts 5 (mixed) and 4 (all-memo). The
skipping and eager-strict probes pin the two ends of the discipline:
a delayed parameter is never evaluated unless demanded, and a `@Strict`
parameter is evaluated at the call.

## Map class

`T`; the objective (per-parameter strictness selection) is unchanged. The
statement's three-mode declaration syntax maps onto `@Strict`/`@Delayed`
plus a function-value parameter for call-by-name; the annotation-riding-
parser machinery of the removed evaluator is gone with it (the module's
annotations are the mechanism).

## Deviation

Call-by-name is a function-value parameter rather than an unmemoized
thunk annotation (the experiment's thunks are memoized by contract). The
result list renders with the edition's `[a, b, c]` shape.

## Answer

- `annotatedMixedTranscript() == "[1, 5, 5, 4, 30, 30]\n5\n"`.
- `annotatedAllMemoTranscript() == "[1, 5, 5, 4, 30, 30]\n4\n"`.
- `lazyParamSkipsTranscript() == "7\n"`: the dangerous argument passes
  harmlessly.
- `strictParamEagerTranscript() == "Error: DivisionByZero\n"`: the strict
  counterfactual evaluates at the call.

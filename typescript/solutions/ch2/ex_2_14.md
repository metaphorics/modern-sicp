# Exercise 2.14: repeated uncertain variables make algebraically equivalent formulas disagree

## Approach

The investigation runs the section's own operations on intervals whose
width is 5 percent of the center - resistors of 10 and 20 ohms - and reads
every result in center-percent form. `A / A` for the 10-ohm resistor comes
out as the interval `[0.9048, 1.1053]`, a 9.975-percent-tolerance interval
centered near 1.005, not the exact point `[1, 1]` ordinary algebra gives.
`par1` and `par2` compute the same parallel resistance two algebraically
equivalent ways and land at 14.9 percent and 5.0 percent tolerance
respectively. The cause in all three: the package treats each textual
occurrence of a variable as an independent uncertain quantity, so
`div-interval(a, a)` multiplies the low bound by the reciprocal of the high
one and a formula that names a resistor twice accumulates its uncertainty
twice.

## Numbers

`A / A`: 9.975062344139651 percent around center 1.005. `par1`:
14.900744416873446 percent; `par2`: 5.000000000000005 percent. `A / B`
carries the same 9.975 percent - the sum of the two operands' 5-percent
tolerances, as exercise 2.13's approximation predicts for the quotient.

## Map class

`T`: the investigation translates directly; the numbers are exact `number`
computations pinned to the values above.

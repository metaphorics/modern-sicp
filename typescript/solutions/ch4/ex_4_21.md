# Exercise 4.21: recursion without define

## Approach

Everything runs on the module's evaluator, which needs no extension: the
programs themselves carry the recursion. The self-application trick binds a
procedure whose first parameter is the procedure itself, so the body can
recurse by passing itself along. Part (a) evaluates the book's factorial
expression and a Fibonacci analog written the same way. Part (b) fills the
`??` holes: each recursive call must forward the two procedure arguments it
received, so the missing operand list is the pair itself plus the
decremented count.

## Numbers

The book's expression `((lambda (n) ((lambda (fact) (fact fact n))
(lambda (ft k) ...)) 10)` evaluates to `3628800`. The Fibonacci analog
`((lambda (n) ((lambda (fib) (fib fib n)) (lambda (fb k) (if (< k 2) k
(+ (fb fb (- k 1)) (fb fb (- k 2))))))) 10)` evaluates to `55`. The
completed `f` answers `(f 5)` as `#f` and `(f 6)` as `#t`.

## Class and deviations

Translated row. The completed procedure spells each hole as
`(od? ev? od? (- n 1))` and `(ev? ev? od? (- n 1))`, forwarding the two
procedure arguments unchanged. No other deviation.

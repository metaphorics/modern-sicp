# Exercise 4.47: Louis's parse-verb-phrase (translate)

## Approach

Louis's version works at first: an `amb`'s alternatives are analyzed but
evaluated lazily, one at a time only when the search tries them, so the
first alternative consumes a verb and the ordinary parses come out. The
second alternative, however, recurses before anything fails or consumes,
so once a parse has been answered the next `try-again` descends
`parse-verb-phrase` endlessly on the exhausted input: the recursion
performs no choice that consumes a word. Interchanging the two
expressions in the `amb` makes the recursion precede any consumption;
even a depth-capped copy burns its whole budget before the verb, and the
first parse completes only through the second alternative. The
demonstration bounds the recursion with a depth parameter, turning the
divergence into termination the test can pin, and never resumes the
unguarded form.

## Numbers

On "the cat eats": Louis's version answers
`(sentence (noun-phrase (article the) (noun cat)) (verb eats))`; the
capped copy answers the same first parse and then runs dry (no second
parse exists in Louis's order); the interchanged order answers the same
first parse. All three pins match the rust sibling.

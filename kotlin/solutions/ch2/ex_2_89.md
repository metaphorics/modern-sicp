## Exercise 2.89: term lists as a dense coefficient vector

Approach: [DenseTerms] stores a polynomial's coefficients as one
`PersistentList<Num>`, highest order first, the position itself carrying
the order (no coefficient is ever omitted, unlike the sparse `Term`
list's `adjoinTerm`, which drops zero coefficients entirely).
[addDenseTerms] zero-pads both lists to the wider width, adds
coefficient-wise, and trims any leading zeros the sum produces;
[mulDenseTerms] multiplies every coefficient of one list against every
coefficient of the other, shifting and summing. `ex_2_89` proves the
dense functions against `addPoly`/`mulPoly` on the same two book
polynomials, one of which has an internal zero coefficient at order 3.

Class: A, per the map; a dense polynomial as a `PersistentList<Num>`
indexed by position is the direct translation of "a list of the
coefficients themselves," matching the book's own dense representation.

Deviation: the comparison in `ex_2_89` could not compare the two sides'
raw coefficient lists directly; dense keeps that order-3 zero
explicit while sparse never stores a coefficient it lacks, so a fair
comparison converts dense's result to the sparse term list it denotes
([denseToSparseTerms], dropping zero coefficients) before comparing;
found and fixed while proving the exercise, the dense/sparse arithmetic
itself needed no change.

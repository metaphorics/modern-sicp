# Exercise 4.57 — the can-replace rule (translate)

The rule discharges the exercise's two conditions with one `or` — the
two jobs coincide, or a `can-do-job` assertion bridges them — and the
section's `(rule (same ?x ?x))` for the identity exclusion, applied as
the `and`'s last `not` conjunct so it filters whole frames. Because
`can-do-job` edges are direct, the relation never propagates
transitively: Ben's wizardry replaces Hacker, Fect and Tweakit but not
Reasoner's traineeship. Order notes for the pins: with `?person-2`
already bound by the query pattern, part (a)'s first disjunct (same job)
streams Hacker before the `can-do-job` disjunct streams Ben under the
evaluator's interleaving; part (b) pairs every replacement frame with
both salary assertions and keeps `(< s1 s2)`, leaving exactly Fect
replacing the better-paid Hacker and secretary Aull replacing big wheel
Warbucks.

```
(can-replace ?x (Fect Cy D))
(can-replace (Hacker Alyssa P) (Fect Cy D))
(can-replace (Bitdiddle Ben) (Fect Cy D))
(and (can-replace ?person-1 ?person-2) (salary ?person-1 ?salary-1) (salary ?person-2 ?salary-2) (lisp-value < ?salary-1 ?salary-2))
(and (can-replace (Fect Cy D) (Hacker Alyssa P)) (salary (Fect Cy D) 35000) (salary (Hacker Alyssa P) 40000) (lisp-value < 35000 40000))
(and (can-replace (Aull DeWitt) (Warbucks Oliver)) (salary (Aull DeWitt) 25000) (salary (Warbucks Oliver) 150000) (lisp-value < 25000 150000))
```

# Exercise 4.21: recursion by self-application

## Lesson

A procedure that takes itself as its first argument closes the loop
every time it runs: applying the self-taker to itself answers a
recursive procedure with no definition in force. Factorial and
Fibonacci show the shape; even and odd share one tag-dispatched
self-taker, with the even branch recursing through the odd branch.

## Map class

`A` (map row 4.1: closures). Self-application needs no binder beyond
application itself.

## Deviation

None. The curried kernel applies the self-taker twice -- once to
itself, once to the argument -- which is the whole trick, written out.

## Answer

- Factorial of `10`: `3628800`.
- Fibonacci of `10`: `55`.
- Evenness of `7` and `10`: `false`, `true`.
- Pin: `selfApplicationFactTranscript() == "3628800\n"`,
  `selfApplicationFibTranscript() == "55\n"`,
  `mutualEvenOddWithoutDefineTranscript() == "false\ntrue\n"`.

# Exercise 4.12: environment traversals

## Approach

The three environment procedures share one shape: walk the chain
looking for a name. The walk is factored into two abstract operations.
`forEachBinding` visits every binding of one frame; `walkEnvChain`
visits frame after frame until the per-frame probe answers, and answers
undefined when the chain runs out. `lookupTraversing` is then the probe
"this frame's cell for the name" over the walk, and `setTraversing` is
the probe "the first frame that binds the name" whose answer is the
cell to write. `defineDirect` needs no traversal: it always writes the
current frame, which is the book's own answer for define.

## Numbers

On a three-frame chain, global `a = 1`, middle `b = 2`, inner `c = 3`:
the traversing lookup from the inner frame answers `3`, `2`, and `1`,
and `zz` fails with `unbound-name`. Setting `b = 20` from the inner
frame is visible from both the inner and the middle frame: the write
went into the middle frame. Defining `d = 4` in the inner frame is
visible from the inner frame only; the middle frame still fails `d`
with `unbound-name`, and the global frame still answers `1` for `a`.
`forEachBinding` over the inner frame visits exactly `["c"]` before the
define, pinning that it never leaves the one frame.

## Class and deviations

Translated row. Deviation: the traversals run over the engine's
name-to-cell frames rather than the book's list frames, since those are
the frames the evaluator uses here; the abstract shape of the two walks
is the book's, and `walkEnvChain`'s stop-at-first-answer contract is
pinned directly.

# Exercise 4.30: forcing in eval-sequence

## Approach

The edition's rule: sequences evaluate every expression and force none of
them, because forcing happens at the point of use, which is a primitive's
operands, an if predicate, an operator position, or the driver. Cy D.
Fect's proposal forces the non-final expressions instead; the cySequence
tuning builds his evaluator. The book's for-each runs under both
evaluators for parts (a) and (c), then `(p1 1)` and `(p2 1)` run under
both for part (b).

## Numbers

for-each prints `57 321 88` and answers `done` under both rules.
`(p1 1)` answers `(1 2)` under both, because the set! is evaluated either
way. `(p2 1)` answers `1` under the text's rule, where the set! hides in
the delayed operand of `p` and never runs, and `(1 2)` under Cy's.

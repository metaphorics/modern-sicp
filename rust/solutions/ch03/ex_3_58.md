# Exercise 3.58: expand computes long division digits

## Approach

Each `expand` step is one long-division step: emit the integer
quotient of `num x radix` by `den`, and continue with the remainder
`num x radix (mod den)` as the new dividend, `den` and `radix`
unchanged. Multiplying by the radix before dividing brings down the
next zero exactly as long division does, so the stream is the radix
expansion of `num/den`, integer part first. `(expand 1 7 10)` walks
the repeating period 142857 of 1/7 -- the first 8 digits show the
period and the restart -- and `(expand 3 8 10)` gives 3 (the integer
part), 7, 5, then zeros forever because the remainder reaches 0: 3/8
terminates in base 10. The two prefixes are pinned as the answer.

## Map class

`T`. The statement translates whole; the book's `quotient`/`remainder`
pair becomes Rust's `/` and `%` on `i128`, which for the positive
arguments here match Scheme exactly.

## Deviation

None.

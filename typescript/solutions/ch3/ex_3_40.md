# Exercise 3.40: all values of concurrent x squared and cubed

## Approach

The square runs as the book's three steps (two accesses of `x`, then the
write) and the cube as four (three accesses, then the write). The
unsynchronized answer enumerates all 35 interleavings of a three-step and
a four-step process and executes each through the step scheduler; the
serialized answer runs the same mutation with each whole update as one
chunk, two orders. Mechanism: explicit step-scheduler enumeration; every
reported value is produced by a real executed schedule.

## Pins

Unsynchronized, computed: {100, 1000, 10000, 100000, 1000000}. The chain
works out to powers of ten: the square's write is a product of two reads
from {10, cube's write} and the cube's write is a product of three reads
from {10, square's write}, and only one write happens per process, so no
value outside that ladder can form. The extreme 100 is the square reading
10 twice before the cube's write and landing its stale 100 last
(schedule 0,0,1,1,1,1,0). Serialized, computed: {1000000}: cube first
gives 1000 then 1000 squared, square first gives 100 then 100 cubed;
both orders agree on one million, the answer the book's argument
predicts.

## Map class

`T: all values of concurrent x squared`.

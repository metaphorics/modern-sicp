# Exercise 2.90a: the sparse-versus-dense benchmark (this edition's addition)

## Approach

The addition turns 2.90's claim of equivalence into measured properties. A property test generates twenty random pairs of four-term polynomials with a tiny fixed-seed linear congruential generator, so the run is deterministic and reproducible, multiplies each pair twice, once through `mul_terms` over `Sparse` and once over `Dense`, canonicalizes both products with `canonical`, and requires exact agreement every time.

A benchmark then measures what the representations cost on the book's favorite worst case, `(x^100 + 1)(x^100 - 1) = x^200 - 1`. The sparse product stores 2 terms and the dense product stores 201, every order from 200 down to 0, and the test asserts `sparse < dense`. Together the two checks say precisely what 2.90 set up: the answers are representation-independent, and the only difference is how many zeros each representation has to carry, which is what makes the sparse list worth its `adjoin_term` guard on problems with real gaps.

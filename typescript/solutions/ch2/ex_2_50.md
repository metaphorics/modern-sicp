# Exercise 2.50: flip-horiz, rotate-180 and rotate-270

## Approach

Each operation is `transform-painter` with the frame that maps the
unit square to the wanted orientation: flip-horiz has origin `(1, 0)`
with corners `(0, 0)` and `(1, 1)`, so x reads `1 - x`; rotate-180 has
origin `(1, 1)` with corners `(0, 1)` and `(1, 0)`, so both
coordinates flip; rotate-270 has origin `(0, 1)` with corners `(0, 0)`
and `(1, 1)`, so `(x, y)` reads `(y, 1 - x)` - a quarter turn
clockwise.

## Numbers

The flip and the 180 rotation agree segment-for-segment with the
module's `flipHoriz` and `rotate180` on the wave. On a dyadic probe
segment from `(0.25, 0.125)` to `(0.375, 0.5)`, rotate-270 renders
`(0.125, 0.75)->(0.5, 0.625)`; applied twice it equals
`rotate180Exercise`; applied three times it equals one `rotate90`
(three clockwise quarter turns compose to one counterclockwise turn);
four applications restore the probe exactly.

## Map class

`A`: the book's three definitions over the edition's
`transform-painter` and segment lists; the corner assignments are the
book's.

## Deviation

The brief proposed pinning three applications of rotate-270 against
`rotate180Exercise`. Run, that is false: 270 x 3 = 810, i.e. 90 modulo
360, and the probe confirms three applications render exactly one
`rotate90` while two applications render the 180. The tests pin that
empirical composition (twice = 180, three times = 90, four times =
identity), which still verifies the rotation's orientation contract.

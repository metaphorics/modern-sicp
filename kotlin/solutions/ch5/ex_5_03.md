# Exercise 5.3: the sqrt machine in two stages

The map class is `T`. The statement asks for the square-root machine of
1.1.7 twice: first with `good-enough?` and `improve` available as
primitives, then with both expanded in terms of arithmetic operations,
each version drawn as a data-path diagram and written as a controller
definition.

Stage 1 needs two registers, `guess` (started at 1.0) and `x`, plus two
primitives in the operations table: `good-enough?` answers whether
`abs(square(guess) - x) < 0.001`, and `improve` answers the average of
`guess` and `x / guess`. Data paths (the trapezoids are the two
primitives, the circle the test; arrows from `guess` and `x` feed both,
and the one assignment button writes `improve`'s value back):

```
+---------+                              +---------+
|  guess  |<------(X) g<-imp ------+     |    x    |
+----+----+                        |     +----+----+
     |                             |          |
     |        .**************.     |          |
     +------->* good-enough?  *<---+----------+
     |        * ************* *    (test, no output)
     |         *  improve   *
     |          *         *
     |            ******
```

The controller:

```
      sqrt-loop
          |
          V
         / \  yes
+------->* *--------> sqrt-done --> (perform print guess)
|        \ /
|         | no
|         V
|    +-----------+
+----+ g<-imp    |
     +-----------+
```

Stage 2 expands the primitives. `good-enough?` becomes three assigns
computing `abs(guess * guess - x)` into the temporary `t` and one test
against `(const 0.001)`; `improve` becomes `(x / guess) + guess`
divided by `(const 2)`. The temporary exists because an operation's
inputs are registers and constants only, the section's rule that
operations cannot operate on the results of other operations. One
deviation, deliberate: `abs` stays primitive, because the book's 1.1.7
counts it among the arithmetic operations the expansion may use, while
`average` is expanded away.

Method: each stage's transcript is the single line its controller
prints at `sqrt-done` (the controllers end with
`(perform (op print) (reg guess))` so the design's answer becomes
observable), computed by the hand model of `Handsim.kt` at the full
host precision of Kotlin's `Double.toString`. The point of the
two-stage design is that the expanded machine computes the same fixed
point, and it does. `sqrtMachineTranscripts()` answers
`["1.4142156862745097", "1.4142156862745097", "3.00009155413138",
"3.00009155413138"]`: stage 1 then stage 2 on x = 2 and x = 9, the
four lines pinned in `E5_03Test` together with the stage equality.
The answer for x = 2 is the Newton iterate within the 0.001 tolerance,
not the true root: the machine stops at 1.4142156862745097, the
0.001-good-enough answer rendered at full host precision.

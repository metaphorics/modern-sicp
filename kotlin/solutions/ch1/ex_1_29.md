## Exercise 1.29: Simpson's Rule integrates cube more accurately

Approach: `simpsonCoefficient` picks 1 at the two endpoints, 4 at odd
indices, 2 at even interior indices, exactly the pattern the formula
names; `simpson` sums `coefficient(k) * f(a + k*h)` over `0..n` and
scales by `h/3`. At `n = 100` and `n = 1000` the result is
`0.25000000000000006` both times, off the exact `1/4` by a single
double-precision rounding step, far closer than section 1.3.1's
`integral` (`0.24998750000000042` and `0.249999875000001`
respectively) at the same `n`. Simpson's Rule is exact for cubics up to
a constant term, which is why `n = 100` already saturates double
precision here.

Class: T (translate). Deviation: none; `simpson` takes `f`, `a`, `b`,
`n` exactly as the statement asks, and the two integers `100`/`1000`
are the exercise's own choice.

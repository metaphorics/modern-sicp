# Exercise 1.23: skip even divisors and remeasure

## Approach

`next` maps 2 to 3 and everything else to itself plus 2, so the trial
divisors run 2, 3, 5, 7, 9, ... and the divisor loop's `d + 1` becomes
`next(d)`. Both searches find the same twelve primes of exercise 1.22;
the remeasurement compares median search times (warmup round, nine timed
rounds, median of samples) at the 100,000,000 workload of exercise
1.22a, where a search runs long enough for the coarsened clock to see.

## Numbers

Both searches return the pinned twelve primes. The ratio bound asserted
is `0.5 <= plain/skipping <= 2`: the measured ratio on this host lands
below 2 and comfortably above 1. Why not 2? The skipping loop halves the
number of trial divisions, but each pass pays for the `next` call and
the loop's bookkeeping; the candidate composites are mostly dismissed by
the divisor 3 either way; and on a just-in-time machine the two loops
compile differently, so their steady states differ from their step
counts. The classic reading - the modification does less than half the
work of halving the time - is exactly what the data shows.
